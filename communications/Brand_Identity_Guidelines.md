# Brand Identity Guidelines

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) brand represents the organization's mission, values, credibility and commitment to sustainable development.

These Brand Identity Guidelines establish standards for the consistent use of TJF's name, logo, colours, typography, imagery and messaging across all physical and digital communications.

A consistent brand strengthens recognition, builds stakeholder confidence and reinforces the Foundation's reputation.

---

# 1. Purpose

These Guidelines aim to:

- Protect TJF's visual identity.
- Promote consistent branding.
- Strengthen public recognition.
- Improve communication quality.
- Support professional presentation across all platforms.

# 4. Official Organization Name

**Primary Name**

> **The Jopetnah Foundation**

**Acceptable Short Form**

> **TJF**

The Foundation's full name should be used on first reference in publications. "TJF" may be used thereafter.

Do not create unofficial abbreviations or alternative names.

# 5. Logo Standards

## Primary Logo

The primary logo is the preferred mark for all official communications.

---

## Secondary Logo

The secondary logo may be used where space or layout constraints require an alternative configuration.

---

## Monochrome Logo

A monochrome version may be used only when colour reproduction is not practical.

---

## Minimum Size

The logo should remain legible in all applications.

Recommended minimum width:

- Print: 25 mm
- Digital: 120 px

---

## Clear Space

Maintain clear space around the logo equal to at least the height of the "T" in the logo wordmark.

No text, graphics or images should encroach on this space.

---

## Incorrect Logo Usage

Do **not**:

- Stretch or distort the logo.
- Rotate the logo.
- Change brand colours.
- Add effects or shadows.
- Place the logo on visually cluttered backgrounds.
- Recreate the logo using other fonts.

# 7. Typography

Recommended fonts:

## Headings

- Montserrat
- Poppins
- Arial (fallback)

---

## Body Text

- Open Sans
- Calibri
- Arial

Typography should prioritize readability across print and digital formats.

---

# 8. Photography Style

Images should:

- Reflect authentic community engagement.
- Show dignity and respect.
- Represent diversity and inclusion.
- Demonstrate real programme activities.
- Be high resolution and well composed.

Avoid:

- Staged or misleading images.
- Images that compromise privacy or dignity.
- Low-quality or pixelated photographs.

Consent should be obtained before using identifiable images where required.

---

# 9. Iconography and Graphics

Use icons and illustrations that are:

- Simple.
- Modern.
- Accessible.
- Consistent in style.
- Relevant to the content.

Avoid decorative graphics that distract from key messages.

---

# 10. Writing Style

All communications should be:

- Clear.
- Accurate.
- Respectful.
- Inclusive.
- Plain-language where appropriate.
- Evidence-based.

Avoid jargon, exaggerated claims or discriminatory language.

---

# 11. Brand Voice

TJF's tone should be:

- Confident but humble.
- Professional but approachable.
- Inspirational without overstating impact.
- Hopeful and solutions-oriented.
- Respectful of communities and partners.

---

# 12. Co-Branding

When working with partners:

- Obtain approval before using partner logos.
- Respect partner branding requirements.
- Maintain appropriate visual balance.
- Clearly distinguish TJF branding from partner branding.

---

# 13. Branded Materials

These Guidelines apply to:

- Letterheads.
- Business cards.
- Reports.
- Proposals.
- Presentations.
- Social media graphics.
- Banners.
- Posters.
- Signage.
- Email signatures.
- Merchandise.
- Website content.

---

# 14. Digital Brand Assets

The Communications Department should maintain a central repository containing:

- Primary logo.
- Secondary logo.
- Monochrome logo.
- Colour palette.
- Font guidance.
- Presentation templates.
- Social media templates.
- Email signature templates.
- Press kit.

Only approved assets should be used.

---

# 15. Brand Governance

The Communications & Public Relations Department is responsible for:

- Maintaining brand standards.
- Approving new visual assets.
- Supporting staff and partners.
- Periodically reviewing these Guidelines.

Questions regarding brand use should be directed to the Communications Department.

---

# 16. Review

These Guidelines should be reviewed:

- Every three years.
- Following a rebranding initiative.
- After significant organizational changes.
- When new communication platforms are introduced.

---

# Conclusion

A strong and consistent brand enhances trust, visibility and organizational credibility.

By applying these Brand Identity Guidelines, The Jopetnah Foundation presents a unified identity that reflects its professionalism, values and commitment to creating sustainable impact.

---

> **A trusted brand is built through consistency, integrity and meaningful action.**
