# Crisis Communication Plan

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) is committed to communicating responsibly during incidents that may affect its people, programmes, operations or reputation.

This Plan establishes the governance, responsibilities and communication procedures required to manage crises effectively while protecting beneficiaries, staff, partners, donors and the public.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Plan aims to:

- Protect human life and wellbeing.
- Maintain public confidence.
- Ensure accurate and timely communication.
- Reduce misinformation.
- Support coordinated decision-making.
- Protect TJF's reputation.

---

# 2. Scope

This Plan applies to communication relating to:

- Safeguarding incidents.
- Security incidents.
- Natural disasters.
- Public health emergencies.
- Cybersecurity incidents.
- Data breaches.
- Financial misconduct.
- Legal disputes.
- Reputational issues.
- Major operational disruptions.

---

# 3. Guiding Principles

During a crisis, TJF communications should be:

- Accurate.
- Timely.
- Transparent.
- Compassionate.
- Consistent.
- Respectful.
- Evidence-based.
- Approved through appropriate authority.

Where facts are still being verified, communications should acknowledge this rather than speculate.

---

# 4. Crisis Communication Team

The Executive Director should establish a Crisis Communication Team that may include:

| Role | Primary Responsibility |
|------|-------------------------|
| Executive Director | Strategic leadership and approvals |
| Communications Manager | Communication coordination and media engagement |
| Programme Lead | Operational updates |
| Human Resources Lead | Staff communications |
| ICT Lead | Digital and cybersecurity communications |
| Legal Adviser (where applicable) | Legal review and advice |
| Safeguarding Focal Person | Safeguarding-related communication |

Additional members may be appointed depending on the nature of the incident.

---

# 5. Crisis Classification

## Level 1 — Minor Incident

Examples:

- Localized operational disruption.
- Minor service interruption.
- Isolated stakeholder complaint.

Communication is generally managed internally.

---

## Level 2 — Significant Incident

Examples:

- Major programme disruption.
- Serious injury.
- Local media attention.
- Significant operational issue.

Requires coordinated communication and leadership oversight.

---

## Level 3 — Major Crisis

Examples:

- National media coverage.
- Fatal incident.
- Major safeguarding allegation.
- Widespread cyberattack.
- Large-scale fraud.
- Significant reputational threat.

Requires activation of the Crisis Communication Team and Executive Director oversight.

---

---

# 7. Stakeholder Communication

Depending on the incident, communications may be directed to:

- Beneficiaries.
- Staff.
- Volunteers.
- Board of Trustees.
- Donors.
- Development partners.
- Government authorities.
- Media.
- Local communities.
- Service providers.

Messages should be tailored to the needs and responsibilities of each audience.

---

# 8. Key Message Development

Each message should address:

- What happened.
- What is currently known.
- What actions TJF has taken.
- What stakeholders should do (if applicable).
- Where further information will be provided.

Avoid speculation or assigning blame before facts are confirmed.

---

# 9. Media Management

During a crisis:

- Only authorized spokespersons may speak on behalf of TJF.
- Media enquiries should be coordinated through the Communications Department.
- Press statements should be approved before release.
- Interviews should be carefully prepared.

---

# 10. Social Media Management

The Communications Department should:

- Monitor online discussion.
- Correct significant misinformation where appropriate.
- Pause scheduled content if necessary.
- Publish approved updates.
- Escalate emerging issues promptly.

---

# 11. Internal Communication

Staff should receive timely updates regarding:

- Operational changes.
- Safety instructions.
- Approved messaging.
- Media handling procedures.
- Available support services.

Internal communications should precede or coincide with public announcements where practicable.

---

# 12. Protection of Confidential Information

During a crisis, TJF should continue to protect:

- Personal information.
- Safeguarding-related information.
- Sensitive operational details.
- Legal matters.
- Information that could compromise safety or investigations.

---

# 13. Documentation

Maintain records of:

- Incident timeline.
- Decisions made.
- Approved statements.
- Media enquiries.
- Social media activity.
- Stakeholder communications.
- Lessons learned.

These records support accountability and future preparedness.

---

# 14. Training and Preparedness

TJF should periodically:

- Train spokespersons.
- Conduct crisis communication exercises.
- Review emergency contact lists.
- Test communication systems.
- Update response procedures.

---

# 15. Post-Incident Review

After each significant incident, the Crisis Communication Team should evaluate:

- Timeliness of communication.
- Accuracy of information.
- Stakeholder feedback.
- Media coverage.
- Internal coordination.
- Lessons learned.
- Recommended improvements.

Recommendations should be documented and tracked to completion.

---

# 16. Roles and Responsibilities

## Board of Trustees

- Provides strategic oversight during major organizational crises.
- Reviews significant post-incident findings where appropriate.

---

## Executive Director

- Authorizes major public statements.
- Leads executive decision-making.
- Activates the Crisis Communication Team when required.

---

## Communications & Public Relations Department

Responsible for:

- Drafting and coordinating communications.
- Managing media relations.
- Monitoring public sentiment.
- Maintaining communication records.

---

## All Staff

All personnel should:

- Report incidents promptly through established channels.
- Avoid unauthorized public statements.
- Refer media enquiries to authorized spokespersons.
- Follow approved communication guidance.

---

# 17. Annual Review

This Plan should be reviewed:

- Annually.
- Following every major incident.
- After crisis simulation exercises.
- When organizational risks or communication technologies change.

---

# Conclusion

Effective crisis communication is essential to protecting people, maintaining trust and supporting organizational resilience.

By following this Plan, The Jopetnah Foundation is better prepared to respond to challenging situations with clarity, compassion and accountability while safeguarding its mission, stakeholders and reputation.

---

> **In times of uncertainty, clear, honest and timely communication is one of an organization's strongest assets.**
