About The Jopetnah Foundation

Building whole communities, one life at a time.

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) is being established in Uganda as a nonprofit initiative developing an integrated, family-centred approach to community development through education, health and wellbeing, livelihoods, and environmental resilience.

“A child out of school, a family without healthcare, and a household without income are rarely separate problems. They are often the same problem, seen from different angles.”
Current status: TJF is in the final stages of its legal registration as a Company Limited by Guarantee and NGO in Uganda. We are building governance systems, programme frameworks and partnerships before launching operations. Online donations are not active. We describe our plans openly so partners can see where we stand.
Who We Are

A foundation built around the whole picture.

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) is being developed as a nonprofit platform focused on strengthening the lives and resilience of underserved people, families and communities in Uganda.

TJF's approach is grounded in the belief that sustainable progress is difficult when interconnected problems are treated separately. Education, health, household income, youth opportunity, water, sanitation and the environment can influence one another every day.

Rather than defining a community only by the immediate problem it faces, TJF seeks to understand the barriers that keep people vulnerable and to develop practical, locally grounded pathways toward stability, opportunity, resilience and greater self-reliance.

Relationship note: TJF is being established as an independent nonprofit. It draws early inspiration and networks from individuals associated with Jopetnah International Limited (a Kampala-based enterprise founded in 2016), but will operate under its own separate governance, registration, and accountability structures once formalized.

Leadership

Building the team that will steward the work.

TJF is led today by its founder and Executive Director, Mr Okello Carlos Torach. Every other role will be filled once the Board of Trustees is seated and registration is complete. We will name people here only when they have been appointed and have agreed to be named.

Roles to be filled once the Board is seated

We would rather show an honest gap than an invented name. The Board of Trustees (seven to nine people under our Constitution) comes first. It will then appoint, or confirm, the people who lead the work. Read how the Board will work on the Board of Trustees page.

The roles we plan to fill
  • Chair and trustees of the Board
  • Programme leads for Education & Youth Development, Health & Wellbeing, Livelihoods, and Environment
  • Finance lead
  • Fundraising and partnerships lead
  • Communications lead
  • Monitoring, evaluation and learning officer
  • An advisory group of volunteer advisers

These are planned roles, not current vacancies, and the list may change as our work develops.

We are actively seeking founding Board members, advisors, and an incorporation partner with experience in nonprofit governance, community development in Uganda, education, health (including veterinary and public health), livelihoods, or environmental resilience.

Our Mission

Opportunity, resilience and sustainable progress.

Mission

To advance education, health, economic opportunity and environmental resilience in underserved communities through integrated, locally led programs.

Vision

A generation of communities that are educated, healthy, economically self-reliant and environmentally sustainable.

Our Philosophy

Four pillars. One integrated model.

TJF does not view education, health, livelihoods and the environment as unrelated causes. We see them as interconnected dimensions of human wellbeing and community resilience.

Education & Youth Development

Helping children and young people access learning, mentorship, literacy, digital skills and pathways toward future opportunity.

Health & Wellbeing

Strengthening community health awareness, prevention, referral pathways, wellbeing and access to appropriate health-related support.

Poverty Alleviation & Livelihoods

Supporting skills, entrepreneurship, savings, income generation and economic opportunity so households can move beyond short-term survival.

Environment & Community Development

Strengthening climate resilience through safer water and sanitation access, climate-smart agriculture, and community-based environmental stewardship — helping households adapt to a changing climate while building healthier, more sustainable communities.

Our Distinctive Approach

From vulnerability toward resilience.

TJF seeks to move beyond short-term relief toward a development pathway that strengthens household and community capacity.

Our intended pathway:
Stabilize → Protect → Empower → Strengthen → Graduate

What this means in practice

  • Stabilize: respond to immediate vulnerabilities that threaten wellbeing and participation.
  • Protect: reduce preventable barriers to education, health, safety and basic opportunity.
  • Empower: build knowledge, skills, confidence and access to economic opportunity.
  • Strengthen: support sustainable livelihoods, savings, community systems and resilience.
  • Graduate: help households and communities move toward greater self-reliance and long-term capacity.
Our Values

How we intend to work.

Integrity

We seek to act honestly, responsibly and transparently in how we manage relationships, resources and commitments.

Community Ownership

We believe sustainable development is stronger when communities participate meaningfully in defining priorities and solutions.

Transparency

We aim to communicate clearly about our plans, progress, limitations, resources and results — including publishing key working documents on our public GitHub repository.

Sustainability

We seek approaches that strengthen capacity and resilience rather than creating unnecessary long-term dependency.

Inclusion

We seek to create opportunities that respect dignity, participation and fair access for underserved people and communities.

Learning

We intend to measure, reflect, adapt and improve as programs develop and evidence becomes available.

Institutional Development

Building the institution as we build the programs.

TJF is being developed with a deliberate focus on institutional credibility. Our growth priorities include governance, program design, safeguarding, monitoring and evaluation, financial systems, partnerships, communications and responsible resource mobilization.

We believe that ambition should be matched by capacity, evidence, accountability and responsible growth.

Our work begins in Uganda. As our model matures and the evidence supports it, TJF hopes to share what it learns with communities across East Africa and, in time, across the African continent. We will grow only as fast as our governance, safeguarding and results allow.

Foundation (Current)

Demonstration

Consolidation

Scale
