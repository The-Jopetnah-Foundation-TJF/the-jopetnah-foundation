# Knowledge Management Policy

## Executive Summary

Knowledge is one of The Jopetnah Foundation's (TJF) most valuable organizational assets. The ability to capture, preserve, share and apply knowledge strengthens programme quality, innovation, accountability and long-term sustainability.

This Knowledge Management Policy establishes the principles, systems and responsibilities for managing institutional knowledge across TJF.

The Policy promotes a culture where learning, evidence, experience and innovation are continuously transformed into improved organizational performance and greater social impact.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Policy aims to:

- Establish a structured approach to knowledge management.
- Preserve institutional memory.
- Promote learning and innovation.
- Improve decision-making.
- Encourage knowledge sharing.
- Strengthen programme effectiveness.
- Support continuous improvement.

---

# 2. Scope

This Policy applies to:

- Board members.
- Employees.
- Volunteers.
- Consultants.
- Interns.
- Partners involved in TJF programmes.

It covers:

- Programme knowledge.
- Research findings.
- Lessons learned.
- Technical expertise.
- Policies and procedures.
- Reports and publications.
- Digital resources.
- Organizational experiences.

---

# 3. Definition of Knowledge Management

Knowledge Management refers to the systematic process of:

- Creating knowledge.
- Capturing information.
- Organizing resources.
- Sharing experiences.
- Applying learning.
- Preserving institutional memory.

---

# 4. Knowledge Management Principles

TJF's knowledge management approach is guided by:

## Learning Culture

Encouraging curiosity, reflection and continuous improvement.

---

## Accessibility

Making relevant knowledge available to authorized users.

---

## Quality

Ensuring information is accurate, reliable and useful.

---

## Collaboration

Promoting knowledge exchange across departments and partners.

---

## Innovation

Using knowledge to develop better solutions.

---

## Security

Protecting confidential and sensitive information.

# 6. Knowledge Management Lifecycle

TJF shall manage knowledge through five stages:

## 1. Creation

Knowledge is generated through:

- Programme implementation.
- Research.
- Innovation.
- Staff experience.
- Partnerships.

---

## 2. Capture

Knowledge should be documented through:

- Reports.
- Case studies.
- Lessons learned documents.
- Meeting records.
- Research publications.

---

## 3. Organization

Knowledge should be stored using:

- Digital repositories.
- Document management systems.
- Knowledge databases.

---

## 4. Sharing

Knowledge may be shared through:

- Internal learning sessions.
- Training.
- Workshops.
- Digital platforms.
- Publications.

---

## 5. Application

Knowledge should inform:

- Strategic decisions.
- Programme improvement.
- Innovation.
- Policy development.

---

# 7. Knowledge Repository

TJF should maintain a centralized knowledge repository containing:

- Policies.
- Procedures.
- Reports.
- Research documents.
- Training materials.
- Templates.
- Lessons learned.
- Best practices.

- Clear classification.
- Version control.
- Access permissions.
- Search capability.
