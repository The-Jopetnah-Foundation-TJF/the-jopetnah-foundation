# Data Backup and Disaster Recovery Plan

## Executive Summary

Reliable access to information systems and digital records is essential to the operations of The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF).

This Data Backup and Disaster Recovery Plan establishes the procedures, responsibilities and controls required to protect TJF information assets and restore critical systems following technology failures, cybersecurity incidents or other disruptive events.

The Plan supports organizational resilience by ensuring that critical data can be recovered within acceptable timeframes while minimizing operational impact.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Plan aims to:

- Protect critical organizational data.
- Ensure availability of essential systems.
- Reduce the impact of technology disruptions.
- Establish backup standards.
- Define recovery procedures.
- Support business continuity.
- Protect stakeholder information.

---

# 2. Scope

This Plan applies to:

- Digital records.
- Databases.
- Cloud platforms.
- Applications.
- Email systems.
- File storage systems.
- Network infrastructure.
- User devices containing organizational information.

It applies to:

- Employees.
- IT personnel.
- System administrators.
- Third-party technology providers.

---

# 3. Disaster Scenarios

This Plan addresses potential disruptions including:

- Cybersecurity attacks.
- Ransomware incidents.
- Hardware failure.
- Software corruption.
- Accidental deletion.
- Data loss.
- Cloud service interruption.
- Network failure.
- Power disruption.
- Natural disasters.
- Human error.

---

# 4. Disaster Recovery Principles

TJF recovery activities shall follow these principles:

- Protect critical information first.
- Restore essential operations quickly.
- Maintain data integrity.
- Prioritize stakeholder impact.
- Document all recovery actions.
- Learn from incidents.

# 6. Backup Strategy

TJF shall maintain backups using appropriate methods including:

- Automated backups.
- Scheduled backups.
- Cloud backups.
- Offline backups where appropriate.

The backup approach should follow the principle:

## 3-2-1 Backup Rule

Maintain:

- 3 copies of important data.
- 2 different storage methods.
- 1 copy stored separately.

---

# 7. Backup Frequency

Backup schedules should be determined based on data importance.

| Data Category | Recommended Frequency |
|--------------|----------------------|
| Critical databases | Daily or continuous |
| Financial records | Daily |
| Programme data | Daily/Weekly |
| Department files | Weekly |
| Archived records | Periodic |

---

# 8. Backup Security

Backups must be protected through:

- Access controls.
- Encryption where appropriate.
- Secure storage.
- Regular testing.
- Monitoring.

Backup credentials must be protected from unauthorized access.

---

# 9. Backup Responsibilities

## IT Department

Responsible for:

- Backup configuration.
- Backup monitoring.
- Recovery testing.
- Storage management.
- Backup reporting.

---

## Department Heads

Responsible for:

- Identifying critical information.
- Ensuring proper document storage.
- Reporting important data requirements.

---

## Users

Responsible for:

- Saving information in approved locations.
- Avoiding unauthorized storage methods.
- Reporting data loss incidents.

---

# 10. Recovery Priorities

Systems shall be restored according to priority.

## Priority 1 — Critical Systems

Examples:

- Core databases.
- Communication systems.
- Essential operational platforms.

---

## Priority 2 — Important Systems

Examples:

- Department applications.
- Reporting systems.

---

## Priority 3 — Supporting Systems

Examples:

- Non-essential tools.
- Archived resources.

---

# 11. Recovery Time Objectives (RTO)

TJF shall establish target recovery times:

| System Category | Target Recovery Time |
|----------------|----------------------|
| Critical systems | Within 24 hours |
| Important systems | Within 72 hours |
| Supporting systems | Within 7 days |

Actual recovery targets may vary depending on available resources and circumstances.

---

# 12. Recovery Point Objectives (RPO)

TJF shall define acceptable data loss periods:

| Data Category | Target Recovery Point |
|--------------|-----------------------|
| Critical data | Minimum loss |
| Financial data | Less than 24 hours |
| Operational files | Less than 7 days |

---

# 13. Disaster Recovery Process

## Step 1: Incident Identification

- Confirm disruption.
- Assess impact.
- Notify relevant personnel.

---

## Step 2: Activation

The IT Department determines whether disaster recovery procedures should begin.

---

## Step 3: Containment

Actions may include:

- Isolating affected systems.
- Preventing further damage.
- Securing evidence.

---

## Step 4: Restoration

Activities include:

- Recovering backups.
- Restoring systems.
- Testing functionality.
- Returning services to users.

---

## Step 5: Verification

Confirm:

- Data accuracy.
- System security.
- Operational readiness.

---

# 14. Disaster Recovery Team

## Executive Director

- Approves major recovery decisions.
- Coordinates organizational response.

---

## IT Manager

- Leads technical recovery.
- Coordinates restoration activities.

---

## Department Heads

- Identify operational priorities.
- Support recovery decisions.

---

## Communications Team

- Manages stakeholder communication where required.

---

# 15. Testing and Maintenance

The recovery plan should be tested through:

- Backup restoration tests.
- Recovery simulations.
- System recovery exercises.
- Review of lessons learned.

Testing results should be documented.

---

# 16. Third-Party Recovery

Technology suppliers should maintain appropriate recovery capabilities.

Contracts should define:

- Recovery responsibilities.
- Service availability commitments.
- Data protection requirements.

---

# 17. Documentation

Recovery records should include:

- Incident description.
- Systems affected.
- Recovery actions.
- Data restored.
- Recovery duration.
- Lessons learned.

---

# 18. Review

This Plan shall be reviewed:

- Every two years.
- After major technology disruptions.
- Following significant system changes.
- After recovery exercises.

A reliable backup and recovery capability protects TJF's ability to continue serving communities during unexpected disruptions.

Through effective planning, secure backups and tested recovery procedures, The Jopetnah Foundation strengthens operational resilience and protects the information entrusted to it.
