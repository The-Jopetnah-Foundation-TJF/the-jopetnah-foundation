# Business Continuity Plan — ICT Annex

## Executive Summary

Information technology systems are essential to The Jopetnah Foundation's (TJF) operations, communication, programme delivery, financial management and stakeholder engagement.

This ICT Business Continuity Annex establishes the arrangements required to maintain, restore and protect technology services during emergencies or disruptive events.

The Annex ensures that TJF can continue critical operations while minimizing downtime, protecting information assets and supporting organizational resilience.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Annex aims to:

- Ensure continuity of critical ICT services.
- Define technology recovery priorities.
- Establish emergency ICT procedures.
- Protect organizational information.
- Support rapid restoration of systems.
- Coordinate ICT recovery activities.

---

# 2. Scope

This Annex applies to:

- ICT infrastructure.
- Information systems.
- Communication platforms.
- Data storage.
- Cloud services.
- Digital applications.
- Technology suppliers.

It supports all TJF departments that depend on ICT services.

---

# 3. ICT Continuity Principles

TJF ICT continuity shall follow:

## Availability

Critical systems should remain accessible whenever possible.

---

## Resilience

Technology systems should withstand and recover from disruptions.

---

## Protection

Information assets must remain secure during emergencies.

---

## Prioritization

Essential services must be restored first.

---

## Coordination

ICT recovery must align with overall organizational response.

---

# 4. Potential ICT Disruptions

This Annex addresses disruptions including:

- Cyberattacks.
- Ransomware.
- System outages.
- Hardware failures.
- Software failures.
- Internet disruptions.
- Cloud service interruptions.
- Power failures.
- Natural disasters.
- Office inaccessibility.
- Loss of key technology personnel.

---

# 5. Critical ICT Services

TJF shall identify critical technology services including:

| Service | Criticality |
|---------|-------------|
| Email and communication systems | High |
| Financial systems | High |
| Programme management systems | High |
| Beneficiary information systems | High |
| File storage systems | High |
| Website and public communication platforms | Medium |
| Internal collaboration tools | Medium |
| Non-essential applications | Low |

---

# 6. ICT Recovery Priorities

Recovery shall follow priority levels:

## Priority 1 — Mission Critical

Target restoration:

Within 24 hours where possible.

Examples:

- Core communication systems.
- Critical databases.
- Essential operational platforms.

---

## Priority 2 — Business Essential

Target restoration:

Within 72 hours where possible.

Examples:

- Department systems.
- Reporting platforms.
- Collaboration tools.

---

## Priority 3 — Support Services

Target restoration:

Within 7 days where possible.

Examples:

- Non-essential applications.
- Archived resources.

# 8. ICT Emergency Activation

ICT continuity procedures may be activated when:

- Critical systems become unavailable.
- A major cyber incident occurs.
- Normal technology operations cannot continue.
- Data integrity is threatened.

---

# 9. Emergency ICT Procedures

## Step 1: Assess Situation

Determine:

- Nature of disruption.
- Systems affected.
- Operational impact.
- Immediate risks.

---

## Step 2: Contain Impact

Actions may include:

- Isolating affected systems.
- Preventing further damage.
- Securing information.

---

## Step 3: Restore Critical Services

Actions include:

- Activating backups.
- Restoring systems.
- Deploying alternative solutions.

---

## Step 4: Resume Normal Operations

Activities include:

- Confirming system stability.
- Monitoring performance.
- Documenting recovery.

---

# 10. Alternative Technology Arrangements

Where primary systems are unavailable, TJF may use:

- Cloud-based alternatives.
- Backup communication channels.
- Remote working arrangements.
- Temporary equipment.
- Alternative service providers.

# 11. Remote Operations Capability

During emergencies, TJF should maintain the ability to operate remotely through:

- Secure cloud systems.
- Online collaboration platforms.
- Remote access controls.
- Digital communication tools.

# 12. Data Protection During Emergencies

Emergency conditions do not remove information security responsibilities.

TJF must continue protecting:

- Personal information.
- Financial records.
- Programme data.
- Partner information.

---

# 13. Communication Continuity

- Approved messaging platforms.
- Alternative email channels.
- Emergency contact systems.

- Service continuity arrangements.
