# Cybersecurity Incident Response Plan

## Executive Summary

Cybersecurity incidents can affect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of The Jopetnah Foundation's (TJF) information systems and operations.

This Cybersecurity Incident Response Plan establishes a structured approach for identifying, containing, investigating, recovering from and learning from cybersecurity incidents.

The Plan enables TJF to respond effectively while minimizing operational disruption, protecting stakeholders and maintaining trust.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Plan aims to:

- Establish a consistent response process.
- Minimize damage from cybersecurity incidents.
- Protect organizational information assets.
- Define roles and responsibilities.
- Support timely recovery.
- Ensure appropriate communication.
- Capture lessons learned.

---

# 2. Scope

This Plan applies to:

- Employees.
- Board members.
- Volunteers.
- Consultants.
- Contractors.
- Technology service providers.

It covers incidents involving:

- Computers and mobile devices.
- Networks.
- Cloud systems.
- Email platforms.
- Applications.
- Databases.
- Digital records.
- Third-party systems connected to TJF.

---

# 3. Definition of Cybersecurity Incident

A cybersecurity incident is any event that threatens the confidentiality, integrity or availability of TJF information systems or data.

Examples include:

- Unauthorized access.
- Data breach.
- Malware infection.
- Phishing attack.
- Ransomware.
- Lost or stolen devices.
- Account compromise.
- System disruption.
- Accidental information disclosure.

---

# 4. Incident Response Principles

TJF's response shall be guided by:

- Speed.
- Accuracy.
- Confidentiality.
- Accountability.
- Evidence preservation.
- Stakeholder protection.
- Continuous improvement.

---

# 5. Incident Response Lifecycle

TJF shall follow six response phases:

1. Preparation.
2. Detection and Reporting.
3. Assessment and Classification.
4. Containment.
5. Recovery.
6. Lessons Learned.

---

# Phase 1: Preparation

Before incidents occur, TJF shall maintain:

- Updated security policies.
- Trained personnel.
- Backup systems.
- Security tools.
- Incident contacts.
- Response procedures.

The IT Department shall periodically test response readiness.

---

# Phase 2: Detection and Reporting

Security incidents may be detected through:

- User reports.
- Security monitoring systems.
- System alerts.
- External notifications.
- Vendor notifications.

All suspected incidents must be reported immediately to:

- IT Department.
- Department supervisor where appropriate.

---

# 6. Incident Classification

Incidents shall be classified based on severity.

## Level 1 — Low

Examples:

- Minor security events.
- Isolated user issues.
- No significant impact.

Response:

- Normal IT handling.

---

## Level 2 — Moderate

Examples:

- Malware affecting a single device.
- Unauthorized access attempt.
- Limited operational impact.

Response:

- IT investigation and containment.

---

## Level 3 — High

Examples:

- Confirmed data breach.
- Multiple affected systems.
- Significant operational disruption.

Response:

- Management involvement.
- Formal incident response activation.

---

## Level 4 — Critical

Examples:

- Major cyberattack.
- Large-scale data compromise.
- Threat to organizational continuity.

Response:

- Executive leadership activation.
- Crisis management procedures.

---

# 7. Incident Response Team

The response team may include:

## Executive Director

Responsibilities:

- Provides strategic decisions.
- Approves external communications.
- Coordinates executive response.

---

## IT Manager

Responsibilities:

- Leads technical response.
- Coordinates investigation.
- Implements containment measures.

---

## Data Protection Officer (where applicable)

Responsibilities:

- Reviews privacy implications.
- Advises on notification obligations.

---

## Communications Representative

Responsibilities:

- Manages approved public messaging.
- Coordinates stakeholder communications.

---

## Department Representatives

Responsibilities:

- Support impact assessment.
- Assist operational recovery.

---

# 8. Incident Investigation

Investigation activities may include:

- Identifying affected systems.
- Determining cause.
- Preserving evidence.
- Reviewing access logs.
- Assessing data exposure.
- Documenting findings.

Investigation records must be maintained securely.

---

# 9. Containment

Containment measures may include:

- Disconnecting affected devices.
- Blocking malicious activity.
- Disabling compromised accounts.
- Isolating affected systems.
- Changing credentials.

Containment decisions should consider operational impact.

---

# 10. Eradication

Actions may include:

- Removing malware.
- Closing vulnerabilities.
- Updating systems.
- Strengthening controls.
- Restoring secure configurations.

---

# 11. Recovery

Recovery activities include:

- Restoring systems.
- Validating security.
- Monitoring for recurrence.
- Returning operations to normal.

Systems should only be restored after security verification.

---

# 12. Communication Management

Communication during incidents must be:

- Accurate.
- Controlled.
- Timely.
- Approved.

Only authorized personnel may communicate externally about incidents.

Potential stakeholders include:

- Employees.
- Donors.
- Partners.
- Regulators.
- Affected individuals.

---

# 13. Data Breach Management

Where personal data may be compromised, TJF shall:

- Assess the nature and extent of exposure.
- Protect affected individuals.
- Follow applicable notification requirements.
- Document decisions and actions.

---

# 14. Third-Party Incidents

Where vendors or partners experience incidents affecting TJF:

TJF shall:

- Request incident details.
- Assess impact.
- Coordinate response.
- Document corrective actions.

# 16. Lessons Learned

After significant incidents, TJF shall conduct a review covering:

- Root cause.
- Response effectiveness.
- Control weaknesses.
- Improvement actions.

Lessons should inform future security improvements.

---

# 17. Testing and Training

The incident response process should be tested through:

- Tabletop exercises.
- Simulated phishing tests.
- Recovery exercises.
- Staff awareness sessions.

---

# 18. Compliance

This Plan should align with:

- TJF Information Security Policy.
- Data Protection requirements.
- Applicable cybersecurity regulations.
- Donor and partner obligations.

---

# 19. Review

This Plan shall be reviewed:

- Every two years.
- After major cybersecurity incidents.
- Following significant technology changes.

---

# Conclusion

Cybersecurity preparedness enables TJF to protect its mission, people and information assets.

Through effective prevention, rapid response and continuous improvement, The Jopetnah Foundation strengthens resilience against evolving digital threats.
