# Impact Assessment Framework

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) is committed to measuring not only what it delivers, but the lasting change created through its programmes.

This Impact Assessment Framework establishes a systematic approach for evaluating long-term outcomes and sustainable development results attributable to TJF's interventions.

Impact assessment enables TJF to demonstrate accountability, improve programme design and provide credible evidence to communities, partners and donors.

---

# 1. Purpose

The Framework aims to:

- Measure long-term programme impact.
- Assess sustainability of results.
- Generate evidence for decision-making.
- Improve programme quality.
- Demonstrate value for investment.
- Strengthen organizational learning.

---

# 2. Definition of Impact

For TJF, **impact** refers to the significant and lasting changes experienced by individuals, households, institutions or communities that can reasonably be linked to TJF-supported interventions.

Impact differs from:

| Level | Focus |
|--------|-------|
| Activities | What TJF does |
| Outputs | What TJF delivers |
| Outcomes | Short- and medium-term changes |
| Impact | Long-term sustainable change |

---

# 3. Impact Assessment Principles

Impact assessments should be:

- Independent where appropriate.
- Evidence-based.
- Participatory.
- Ethical.
- Transparent.
- Inclusive.
- Objective.

---

# 4. Assessment Objectives

Impact assessments should determine:

- Whether intended change occurred.
- Whether unintended positive or negative effects occurred.
- Whether benefits are sustainable.
- Which interventions created the greatest value.
- What improvements are required.

# 6. Key Assessment Questions

Every assessment should consider:

## Relevance

- Did the programme address priority needs?

## Effectiveness

- Were intended outcomes achieved?

## Efficiency

- Were resources used responsibly?

## Sustainability

- Will benefits continue after project completion?

## Impact

- What lasting changes occurred?

---

# 7. Impact Dimensions

TJF assesses impact across multiple dimensions.

## Social Impact

Examples:

- Improved quality of life.
- Increased access to services.
- Greater social inclusion.

---

## Economic Impact

Examples:

- Increased income.
- Employment creation.
- Business growth.
- Household resilience.

---

## Institutional Impact

Examples:

- Stronger community organizations.
- Improved governance.
- Better service delivery.

---

## Environmental Impact

Examples:

- Improved conservation.
- Climate resilience.
- Sustainable resource management.

---

# 8. Assessment Methodologies

TJF may use one or more of the following approaches.

## Quantitative Methods

Examples:

- Household surveys.
- Structured questionnaires.
- Administrative records.
- Statistical analysis.

---

## Qualitative Methods

Examples:

- Focus group discussions.
- Key informant interviews.
- Case studies.
- Community storytelling.

---

## Mixed Methods

Combining quantitative and qualitative approaches provides a more comprehensive understanding of impact.

---

# 9. Data Sources

Evidence may be collected from:

- Beneficiaries.
- Community leaders.
- Government agencies.
- Partner organizations.
- Project records.
- National statistics.
- Observation.

---

# 10. Attribution and Contribution

Impact assessments should distinguish between:

## Attribution

Changes directly caused by TJF.

## Contribution

Changes influenced by TJF alongside other actors.

Where direct attribution is not possible, the assessment should explain TJF's contribution using available evidence.

---

# 11. Sustainability Assessment

Assess whether programme benefits continue through:

- Community ownership.
- Local leadership.
- Continued financing.
- Institutional capacity.
- Environmental sustainability.

---

# 12. Equity and Inclusion

Impact assessments should examine results for:

- Women and girls.
- Men and boys.
- Youth.
- Older persons.
- Persons with disabilities.
- Vulnerable populations.

Disaggregated data should be reported where feasible.

---

# 13. Impact Indicators

Examples include:

| Dimension | Example Indicator |
|------------|-------------------|
| Social | Percentage reporting improved wellbeing |
| Economic | Average increase in household income |
| Education | School completion rate |
| Health | Reduction in preventable illnesses |
| Environment | Hectares restored or protected |
| Governance | Community participation rate |

---

# 14. Data Quality Assurance

Assessment findings should be:

- Verified.
- Triangulated using multiple sources.
- Reviewed for consistency.
- Supported by evidence.

Any limitations should be documented.

---

# 15. Reporting Structure

Impact assessment reports should include:

1. Executive Summary
2. Background
3. Methodology
4. Findings
5. Analysis
6. Lessons Learned
7. Recommendations
8. Annexes

---

# 16. Learning and Use of Findings

Assessment findings should inform:

- Strategic planning.
- Programme redesign.
- Resource allocation.
- Partnership development.
- Policy advocacy.
- Future grant proposals.

---

# 17. Roles and Responsibilities

## Board of Trustees

- Reviews strategic findings.
- Oversees organizational accountability.

---

## Executive Director

- Approves major assessments.
- Ensures recommendations are implemented.

---

## MEAL Department

- Coordinates assessments.
- Ensures methodological quality.
- Prepares reports.

---

## Programme Teams

- Provide implementation data.
- Support field activities.
- Apply lessons learned.

---

# 18. Review Schedule

Impact assessments may be conducted:

- At project completion.
- One to three years after completion (where appropriate).
- At the end of strategic plans.
- Before major programme expansion.

---

# Conclusion

Impact assessment enables TJF to understand not only what was done, but what truly changed.

Through rigorous assessment, transparent reporting and continuous learning, The Jopetnah Foundation strengthens its commitment to creating sustainable and measurable improvements in the lives of the communities it serves.

---

> **True impact is measured not by the activities completed, but by the lasting positive change experienced by people and communities.**
