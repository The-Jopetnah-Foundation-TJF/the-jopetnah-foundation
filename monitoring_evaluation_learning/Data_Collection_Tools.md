# Data Collection Tools

## Executive Summary

Reliable evidence depends on reliable data.

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) adopts standardized data collection tools and procedures to ensure that programme information is accurate, timely, ethical and suitable for decision-making, learning and donor reporting.

This Guide establishes the minimum standards for data collection across all TJF programmes and projects.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Guide aims to:

- Standardize data collection methods.
- Improve data quality.
- Ensure ethical data practices.
- Promote consistency across programmes.
- Strengthen evidence-based decision-making.

---

# 2. Data Collection Principles

All data collected by TJF should be:

- Accurate.
- Complete.
- Timely.
- Relevant.
- Reliable.
- Confidential.
- Collected ethically.

---

# 3. Types of Data

## Quantitative Data

Numerical information such as:

- Number of beneficiaries.
- Attendance.
- Income levels.
- Examination scores.
- Project outputs.

Common tools:

- Structured surveys.
- Registers.
- Administrative records.

---

## Qualitative Data

Descriptive information such as:

- Opinions.
- Experiences.
- Perceptions.
- Success stories.
- Community feedback.

Common tools:

- Interviews.
- Focus group discussions.
- Case studies.
- Observation.

---

# 4. Standard Data Collection Methods

## Surveys

Purpose:

Collect standardized information from a defined group of respondents.

Recommended for:

- Baseline studies.
- Endline studies.
- Beneficiary satisfaction.
- Household assessments.

Typical fields:

- Respondent ID
- Location
- Age
- Gender
- Responses
- Enumerator Name
- Date

---

## Interviews

Purpose:

Collect detailed individual perspectives.

Suitable for:

- Key informants.
- Community leaders.
- Government officials.
- Project staff.

Interview checklist:

- Introduction.
- Consent.
- Open-ended questions.
- Closing remarks.

---

## Focus Group Discussions (FGDs)

Purpose:

Gather community perspectives through guided group discussions.

Recommended group size:

6–12 participants.

Suggested participant categories:

- Women.
- Youth.
- Farmers.
- Community leaders.
- Persons with disabilities.

Facilitator responsibilities:

- Encourage participation.
- Remain neutral.
- Record responses accurately.

---

## Observation Checklists

Purpose:

Verify project implementation and physical progress.

Typical observations:

- Infrastructure quality.
- Attendance.
- Equipment condition.
- Service delivery.
- Environmental conditions.

---

## Administrative Records

Examples:

- Attendance registers.
- Training records.
- Financial records.
- Procurement logs.
- Distribution registers.

---

# 5. Digital Data Collection

Where practical, TJF should use digital tools to improve efficiency and data quality.

Potential platforms include:

- KoboToolbox.
- Open Data Kit (ODK).
- SurveyCTO.
- Microsoft Forms.
- Google Forms.

Benefits include:

- Reduced data entry errors.
- Faster reporting.
- GPS capture (where appropriate).
- Secure storage.
- Easier analysis.

---

# 6. Sampling Guidelines

Sampling methods may include:

## Random Sampling

Used when every member of the population has an equal chance of selection.

---

## Stratified Sampling

Used when comparing different population groups.

---

## Purposive Sampling

Used for:

- Key informants.
- Subject matter experts.
- Targeted beneficiary groups.

---

## Convenience Sampling

Used only when appropriate and limitations are documented.

---

# 7. Enumerator Standards

Enumerators should:

- Understand the questionnaire.
- Receive training.
- Respect participants.
- Protect confidentiality.
- Follow ethical guidelines.
- Record responses accurately.

---

# 8. Informed Consent

Before collecting data:

Enumerators should explain:

- Purpose of the assessment.
- How information will be used.
- Voluntary participation.
- Confidentiality.
- Right to decline or withdraw.

Consent should be documented in accordance with TJF policies and applicable legal requirements.

---

# 9. Data Quality Assurance

Data should undergo the following checks:

## Accuracy

Verify that information reflects actual observations.

---

## Completeness

Ensure all required fields are completed.

---

## Consistency

Check for logical consistency across responses.

---

## Timeliness

Submit data according to reporting schedules.

---

## Validity

Confirm that the correct measurement methods were used.

---

# 10. Data Storage and Security

Collected data should be:

- Stored securely.
- Password protected where applicable.
- Backed up regularly.
- Accessible only to authorized personnel.

Sensitive information should never be shared without authorization.

# 12. Reporting Timelines

| Data Type | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Attendance | Immediately after activity |
| Activity Reports | Monthly |
| Indicator Data | Monthly or Quarterly |
| Outcome Surveys | Mid-term and Endline |
| Impact Assessments | As scheduled |

---

# 13. Common Data Collection Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Incomplete forms | Enumerator training and supervision |
| Duplicate records | Unique beneficiary IDs |
| Data entry errors | Validation and spot checks |
| Low response rates | Community engagement and scheduling |
| Loss of data | Regular backups and secure storage |

---

# 14. Continuous Improvement

TJF will periodically review:

- Data collection tools.
- Survey questionnaires.
- Enumerator guidance.
- Digital systems.
- Quality assurance procedures.

Lessons learned should inform future revisions.

---

# Conclusion

High-quality programmes depend on high-quality data.

By applying standardized data collection methods, ethical practices and strong quality assurance, The Jopetnah Foundation ensures that its decisions, reporting and learning are based on credible evidence.
