# Beneficiary Feedback Mechanism

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) values the perspectives of the people and communities it serves.

This Beneficiary Feedback Mechanism establishes clear, accessible and transparent processes for receiving, documenting, responding to and learning from feedback, complaints, compliments and suggestions.

The mechanism strengthens accountability, improves programme quality and promotes trust between TJF and its stakeholders.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Mechanism aims to:

- Strengthen accountability to beneficiaries.
- Improve programme quality through stakeholder feedback.
- Provide safe channels for raising concerns.
- Ensure timely and fair responses.
- Promote continuous organizational learning.

---

# 2. Scope

This Mechanism applies to:

- Beneficiaries.
- Community members.
- Volunteers.
- Staff.
- Partners.
- Contractors.
- Service providers.
- Any individual interacting with TJF programmes or operations.

---

# 3. Guiding Principles

Feedback management should be:

- Accessible.
- Inclusive.
- Confidential where appropriate.
- Respectful.
- Timely.
- Transparent.
- Fair.
- Free from retaliation.

---

# 4. Types of Feedback

TJF welcomes:

## Suggestions

Ideas to improve programmes or services.

Examples:

- New activities.
- Service improvements.
- Community priorities.

---

## Compliments

Positive recognition of:

- Staff conduct.
- Programme quality.
- Partnerships.
- Service delivery.

---

## General Feedback

Comments related to:

- Programme implementation.
- Communication.
- Community engagement.

---

## Complaints

Concerns relating to:

- Delays.
- Unfair treatment.
- Poor service quality.
- Operational issues.
- Resource distribution.

---

## Serious Concerns

These require immediate escalation and may include:

- Fraud or corruption.
- Abuse.
- Exploitation.
- Harassment.
- Safeguarding concerns.
- Serious misconduct.

Such matters should be managed in accordance with TJF's Safeguarding, Whistleblower and Code of Conduct policies.

---

# 5. Feedback Channels

TJF should provide multiple reporting options, including:

- In-person discussions.
- Community meetings.
- Suggestion boxes.
- Telephone.
- Email.
- Website feedback forms.
- SMS or messaging platforms (where available).

Programme teams should communicate available channels at the start of each project.

---

# 6. Feedback Handling Process

```
Feedback Received
        │
        ▼
Registration
        │
        ▼
Acknowledgement
        │
        ▼
Assessment & Classification
        │
        ▼
Investigation (if required)
        │
        ▼
Decision / Action
        │
        ▼
Response to Complainant
        │
        ▼
Closure
        │
        ▼
Learning & Improvement
```

---

# 7. Feedback Register

Maintain a secure register including:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Reference Number | Unique identifier |
| Date Received | Date |
| Feedback Type | Suggestion, Complaint, Compliment, etc. |
| Source | Beneficiary, Partner, Community Member, etc. |
| Description | Summary of issue |
| Assigned Officer | Responsible staff member |
| Status | Open, Under Review, Closed |
| Date Closed | Date |
| Outcome | Resolution summary |

---

# 8. Response Standards

TJF aims to:

| Activity | Target Time |
|---|---|
| Acknowledge receipt | Within 5 working days |
| Initial assessment | Within 10 working days |
| Provide response | Within 30 working days (where practicable) |
| Close routine cases | As soon as resolution is confirmed |

Serious matters may require different timelines depending on the nature of the investigation.

---

# 9. Confidentiality

Information should be:

- Shared only with authorized personnel.
- Stored securely.
- Managed in accordance with TJF's data protection and confidentiality requirements.

The identity of complainants should be protected whenever appropriate and lawful.

---

# 10. Protection from Retaliation

No individual should experience retaliation for raising a concern in good faith.

Retaliation against complainants, witnesses or staff participating in an investigation may result in disciplinary action.

---

# 11. Escalation Procedures

Feedback should be escalated where it involves:

- Safeguarding concerns.
- Fraud or financial misconduct.
- Criminal activity.
- Significant legal risk.
- Reputational risk.
- Serious policy breaches.

Escalation should follow applicable TJF governance procedures.

---

# 12. Learning and Improvement

Feedback trends should be reviewed regularly to identify:

- Recurring issues.
- Service gaps.
- Opportunities for improvement.
- Good practices to replicate.

Lessons learned should inform programme planning and organizational development.

---

# 13. Reporting

Periodic reports should summarize:

- Number of feedback cases received.
- Types of feedback.
- Resolution rates.
- Average response times.
- Key lessons learned.
- Actions taken.

Personal information should not be disclosed in public reports.

---

# 14. Roles and Responsibilities

## Board of Trustees

- Provides oversight of organizational accountability.

---

## Executive Director

- Ensures the mechanism is implemented.
- Reviews significant or high-risk cases.

---

## MEAL Department

- Maintains the feedback system.
- Analyzes trends.
- Produces periodic reports.

---

## Programme Managers

- Promote awareness of the mechanism.
- Ensure timely responses.
- Implement corrective actions.

---

## All Staff

- Treat stakeholders respectfully.
- Receive and report feedback appropriately.
- Cooperate with investigations when required.

---

# 15. Monitoring Indicators

Examples include:

| Indicator | Measurement |
|---|---|
| Feedback cases received | Number |
| Complaints resolved | Percentage |
| Average response time | Days |
| Beneficiary satisfaction | Percentage |
| Corrective actions completed | Percentage |

---

# 16. Annual Review

The Beneficiary Feedback Mechanism should be reviewed annually to assess:

- Accessibility.
- Effectiveness.
- Timeliness.
- Stakeholder confidence.
- Opportunities for improvement.

Updates should be approved through TJF's governance processes.

---

# Conclusion

Listening to communities is essential to achieving sustainable impact.

By maintaining an accessible, fair and responsive feedback mechanism, The Jopetnah Foundation strengthens accountability, builds trust and continuously improves the quality of its programmes and services.

---

> **Communities are not only beneficiaries of development—they are essential partners in shaping it.**
