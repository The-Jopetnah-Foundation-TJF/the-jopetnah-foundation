# Logical Framework (Logframe) Template

## Executive Summary

The Logical Framework (Logframe) is TJF's standard planning, implementation, monitoring and reporting tool for programmes and projects.

It establishes a clear relationship between:

- Programme Goal
- Outcomes
- Outputs
- Activities
- Indicators
- Means of Verification
- Assumptions and Risks

The Logframe ensures that all TJF interventions are results-oriented and measurable.

---

# 1. Purpose

This template is used to:

- Design projects.
- Monitor implementation.
- Measure performance.
- Report to donors.
- Support evaluations.
- Improve programme quality.

---

# 2. Instructions for Use

Complete the Logframe during programme design and review it throughout implementation.

Each level should logically contribute to the next:

```
Goal
  │
  ▼
Outcomes
  │
  ▼
Outputs
  │
  ▼
Activities
```

Indicators, verification methods and assumptions should be defined for every level.

# 5. Goal Statement

Describe the long-term development change that the project contributes to.

**Example**

> Improve the socioeconomic wellbeing of vulnerable households through sustainable livelihood opportunities.

---

# 6. Outcomes

Outcomes describe the medium-term changes expected because of the project.

Each outcome should:

- Be realistic.
- Be measurable.
- Be attributable to project interventions.

Example:

- Increased household income.
- Improved agricultural productivity.
- Enhanced access to quality education.

---

# 7. Outputs

Outputs are the direct products and services delivered by the project.

Examples:

- Farmers trained.
- Health facilities equipped.
- Youth entrepreneurship workshops conducted.
- Water systems installed.

Outputs should be:

- Specific.
- Measurable.
- Time-bound.

---

# 8. Activities

Activities are the tasks undertaken to produce outputs.

Examples:

- Conduct training sessions.
- Procure equipment.
- Mobilize communities.
- Develop learning materials.
- Construct infrastructure.
- Monitor implementation.

---

# 9. Indicators

Indicators measure progress at each level.

Indicators should be **SMART**:

- **S**pecific
- **M**easurable
- **A**chievable
- **R**elevant
- **T**ime-bound

Examples:

| Level | Sample Indicator |
|---|---|
| Goal | Percentage reduction in household poverty |
| Outcome | Percentage of participants using new skills |
| Output | Number of people trained |
| Activity | Number of workshops delivered |

---

# 10. Baseline

A baseline represents the starting value before implementation.

Example:

| Indicator | Baseline |
|---|---:|
| Farmers using improved practices | 18% |
| Youth employed | 25% |

---

# 11. Targets

Targets define the expected achievement by the end of the project.

Example:

| Indicator | Baseline | Target |
|---|---:|---:|
| Farmers using improved practices | 18% | 70% |
| Youth employed | 25% | 60% |

Targets should be:

- Ambitious.
- Realistic.
- Evidence-based.

---

# 12. Means of Verification

Means of Verification identify where evidence will come from.

Examples include:

- Attendance registers.
- Monitoring reports.
- Survey reports.
- Financial reports.
- Government statistics.
- Photographs.
- Evaluation reports.
- Beneficiary interviews.

---

# 13. Assumptions

Assumptions are external conditions required for success.

Examples:

- Communities remain willing to participate.
- Weather conditions remain favourable.
- Funding is released on schedule.
- Government policies remain supportive.

---

# 14. Risks

Identify risks that could affect implementation.

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Funding delays | Medium | High | Maintain contingency planning |
| Staff turnover | Medium | Medium | Succession planning |
| Natural disasters | Low | High | Emergency preparedness |
| Political instability | Low | High | Continuous context monitoring |

# 16. Data Quality Checks

Before reporting, verify that data is:

- Accurate.
- Complete.
- Consistent.
- Timely.
- Reliable.

Document any corrections or limitations.

---

# 17. Review and Updates

The Logframe should be reviewed:

- During project inception.
- Quarterly during implementation.
- At mid-term review.
- At project completion.
- Following major programme changes.

Changes to objectives, indicators or targets should be documented and approved.

The Logical Framework is the foundation of results-based management at TJF.
