# Partnership Framework

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) believes that strategic partnerships are essential to achieving sustainable and scalable development outcomes. This Framework establishes a structured approach for initiating, managing, monitoring and concluding partnerships that align with TJF's mission, values and strategic priorities.

It promotes consistency, accountability, mutual benefit and effective stewardship of resources across all collaborative engagements.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Framework aims to:

- Establish a standardized partnership lifecycle.
- Promote transparent and ethical collaboration.
- Support sustainable programme delivery.
- Strengthen accountability between partners.
- Minimize partnership-related risks.
- Maximize shared impact and learning.

---

# 2. Scope

This Framework applies to all formal partnerships entered into by TJF, including collaborations with:

- Government ministries and agencies.
- Local governments.
- International development organizations.
- Donor agencies and philanthropic foundations.
- Corporate organizations.
- Civil society organizations (CSOs).
- Non-governmental organizations (NGOs).
- Community-based organizations (CBOs).
- Academic and research institutions.
- Professional associations.
- Faith-based organizations.
- United Nations agencies and multilateral institutions.

---

# 3. Partnership Principles

All partnerships should be guided by:

- Shared vision and objectives.
- Mutual respect.
- Integrity and ethical conduct.
- Transparency.
- Accountability.
- Equity and inclusion.
- Sustainability.
- Continuous learning.

---

# 4. Partnership Lifecycle

Every partnership should progress through the following stages:

```
Opportunity Identification
        │
        ▼
Strategic Assessment
        │
        ▼
Partner Due Diligence
        │
        ▼
Negotiation
        │
        ▼
Agreement Development
        │
        ▼
Implementation
        │
        ▼
Performance Monitoring
        │
        ▼
Evaluation
        │
        ▼
Renewal, Expansion or Exit
```

---

# 5. Opportunity Identification

Potential partnerships may arise through:

- Strategic planning.
- Programme needs assessments.
- Donor opportunities.
- Community consultations.
- Research collaborations.
- Government initiatives.
- Corporate social responsibility programmes.
- Networking events and conferences.

Each opportunity should be assessed against TJF's strategic priorities.

---

# 6. Partner Selection Criteria

Prospective partners should be evaluated based on:

- Mission alignment.
- Technical expertise.
- Organizational capacity.
- Financial sustainability.
- Governance practices.
- Ethical standards.
- Reputation.
- Geographic presence.
- Commitment to safeguarding and inclusion.

---

# 7. Due Diligence

Before entering into a significant partnership, TJF should conduct due diligence covering:

- Legal status.
- Governance structure.
- Financial stability.
- Compliance history.
- Safeguarding practices.
- Environmental and social considerations.
- Reputational assessment.
- Conflict of interest review.

The level of due diligence should be proportionate to the nature and risk of the partnership.

---

# 8. Partnership Agreements

Formal partnerships should normally be documented through an appropriate agreement, such as:

- Memorandum of Understanding (MoU).
- Grant Agreement.
- Cooperation Agreement.
- Service Agreement.
- Consortium Agreement.

Each agreement should clearly define:

- Purpose.
- Scope.
- Roles and responsibilities.
- Deliverables.
- Financial arrangements.
- Reporting requirements.
- Confidentiality.
- Intellectual property.
- Dispute resolution.
- Duration and termination.

---

# 9. Governance and Decision-Making

Each partnership should establish:

- Governance structure.
- Decision-making processes.
- Communication channels.
- Escalation procedures.
- Meeting schedules.
- Reporting arrangements.

Significant decisions should be documented.

---

# 10. Implementation

Implementation should include:

- Joint work planning.
- Resource allocation.
- Defined milestones.
- Risk management.
- Performance indicators.
- Regular coordination meetings.

Programme teams should maintain open and timely communication throughout implementation.

---

# 11. Monitoring and Performance

Performance should be monitored against agreed indicators such as:

| Indicator | Measurement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Activities completed | % completed |
| Deliverables achieved | Number |
| Budget utilization | % |
| Stakeholder satisfaction | Survey results |
| Joint outputs | Number |
| Development outcomes | Qualitative / Quantitative |

Monitoring findings should inform continuous improvement.

---

# 12. Communication

Partners should agree on:

- Communication protocols.
- Branding requirements.
- Public announcements.
- Media engagement.
- Information-sharing procedures.

Public communications should comply with TJF's Communications Strategy and Brand Identity Guidelines.

---

# 13. Risk Management

Potential partnership risks include:

- Strategic misalignment.
- Financial non-compliance.
- Operational delays.
- Reputational damage.
- Safeguarding concerns.
- Data protection breaches.

Risks should be identified, assessed and managed throughout the partnership.

---

# 14. Conflict Resolution

Where disagreements arise, partners should:

1. Attempt informal resolution.
2. Escalate to designated focal points.
3. Hold joint review meetings.
4. Seek mediation where appropriate.
5. Apply dispute resolution provisions contained in the agreement.

The objective should be to preserve constructive working relationships wherever possible.

---

# 15. Renewal or Exit

Before the end of each partnership, TJF should assess:

- Achievement of objectives.
- Value created.
- Risks and lessons learned.
- Future collaboration opportunities.

Where a partnership concludes, an orderly exit plan should address:

- Outstanding obligations.
- Financial reconciliation.
- Asset disposition.
- Documentation and records.
- Stakeholder communication.

---

# 16. Documentation

The Partnerships Department should maintain a complete record of:

- Partner profiles.
- Due diligence reports.
- Signed agreements.
- Meeting minutes.
- Performance reports.
- Financial records.
- Evaluation reports.
- Renewal or closure decisions.

Records should be retained in accordance with the Records Management Policy.

---

# 17. Roles and Responsibilities

## Board of Trustees

- Provides oversight for major strategic partnerships.
- Reviews partnerships with significant financial or reputational implications.

---

## Executive Director

- Approves major partnership agreements.
- Represents TJF in high-level partnership engagements.

---

## Partnerships & External Relations Department

Responsible for:

- Partnership development.
- Coordination.
- Due diligence.
- Agreement management.
- Performance monitoring.
- Relationship management.

---

## Programme Teams

Responsible for:

- Technical implementation.
- Day-to-day coordination.
- Progress reporting.
- Identification of partnership opportunities.

---

## Finance Department

Responsible for:

- Financial due diligence.
- Budget oversight.
- Financial reporting.
- Compliance monitoring.

---

## Legal Adviser (where applicable)

Responsible for:

- Reviewing partnership agreements.
- Advising on legal obligations.
- Supporting dispute resolution.

---

# 18. Review

This Framework should be reviewed:

- Every three years.
- Following significant organizational changes.
- After major partnership evaluations.
- When legal or regulatory requirements change.

---

# Conclusion

Strong partnerships are built on trust, shared purpose and responsible governance.

By applying this Framework, The Jopetnah Foundation ensures that every partnership contributes to sustainable development, strengthens institutional capacity and delivers measurable value to the communities it serves.

---

> **The strongest partnerships are those where shared values, mutual accountability and collective action create lasting impact beyond what any organization could achieve alone.**
