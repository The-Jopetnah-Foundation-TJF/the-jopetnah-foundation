# Partnership Risk Management Framework

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) recognizes that partnerships create opportunities for greater impact but may also introduce risks that require proactive management.

This Partnership Risk Management Framework establishes the approach TJF uses to identify, assess, monitor and mitigate risks associated with external collaborations.

The Framework supports responsible partnership decisions, protects organizational reputation and strengthens accountability to stakeholders.

---

# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Framework is to:

- Identify partnership-related risks.
- Establish risk assessment procedures.
- Define mitigation approaches.
- Protect TJF's mission and reputation.
- Strengthen partnership governance.
- Promote informed decision-making.

---

# 2. Scope

This Framework applies to:

- Strategic partnerships.
- Programme partnerships.
- Donor relationships.
- Technical collaborations.
- Community partnerships.
- Service providers.

---

# 3. Partnership Risk Management Principles

## Prevention

TJF seeks to identify risks before they occur.

---

## Proportionality

Risk controls should match:

- Partnership size.
- Complexity.
- Financial exposure.
- Operating environment.

---

## Accountability

Each significant risk must have:

- An owner.
- Mitigation actions.
- Monitoring arrangements.

---

## Continuous Improvement

Risk management should evolve based on experience and learning.

---

# 5. Partnership Risk Categories

## 5.1 Strategic Risk

Risk that a partnership does not support TJF's mission or priorities.

Examples:

- Misaligned objectives.
- Mission drift.
- Conflicting priorities.

Mitigation:

- Strategic alignment assessment.
- Clear partnership objectives.
- Regular reviews.

---

# 5.2 Reputation Risk

Risk that partner actions negatively affect TJF's public credibility.

Examples:

- Ethical misconduct.
- Negative publicity.
- Public complaints.

Mitigation:

- Due diligence.
- Reputation screening.
- Conduct requirements.

---

# 5.3 Financial Risk

Risk related to financial management and resource use.

Examples:

- Misuse of funds.
- Poor financial controls.
- Budget overruns.

Mitigation:

- Financial assessment.
- Reporting requirements.
- Audit provisions.

---

# 5.4 Compliance Risk

Risk arising from failure to follow:

- Laws.
- Regulations.
- Donor requirements.
- Organizational policies.

Mitigation:

- Compliance checks.
- Partner agreements.
- Monitoring.

---

# 5.5 Operational Risk

Risk affecting programme implementation.

Examples:

- Limited capacity.
- Delays.
- Poor coordination.

Mitigation:

- Capacity assessment.
- Implementation plans.
- Performance reviews.

---

# 5.6 Safeguarding Risk

Risk of harm to vulnerable persons.

Examples:

- Abuse.
- Exploitation.
- Unsafe practices.

Mitigation:

- Safeguarding requirements.
- Training.
- Reporting mechanisms.

---

# 5.7 Data Protection Risk

Risk involving misuse or loss of information.

Examples:

- Unauthorized access.
- Poor data handling.

Mitigation:

- Data protection standards.
- Access controls.
- Confidentiality agreements.

---

# 8. Risk Mitigation Strategies

Possible controls include:

## Preventive Controls

Actions taken before problems occur.

Examples:

- Due diligence.
- Training.
- Clear agreements.

---

## Detective Controls

Actions used to identify issues.

Examples:

- Monitoring.
- Reviews.
- Audits.

---

## Corrective Controls

Actions taken after problems occur.

Examples:

- Improvement plans.
- Corrective actions.
- Partnership restructuring.

---

# 9. High-Risk Partnerships

Enhanced controls may apply where partnerships involve:

- Significant financial resources.
- Vulnerable populations.
- High public visibility.
- Complex operating environments.

Additional measures may include:

- Executive review.
- Increased reporting.
- Independent verification.
- Site visits.

---

# 10. Risk Ownership

## Board of Trustees

Provides oversight of significant strategic risks.

---

## Executive Director

Responsible for major partnership risk decisions.

---

## Partnerships Department

Responsible for:

- Risk identification.
- Monitoring.
- Reporting.

---

## Programme Teams

Responsible for operational risk management.

---

## Finance Department

Responsible for financial risk monitoring.

---

# 11. Risk Escalation

Risks should be escalated when they:

- Threaten programme success.
- Affect TJF reputation.
- Involve potential misconduct.
- Create significant financial exposure.

Escalation pathway:

```
Programme Team
       │
       ▼
Partnership Department
       │
       ▼
Executive Director
       │
       ▼
Board of Trustees (where required)
```

---

# 12. Partnership Review

Partnership risks should be reviewed:

- During implementation reviews.
- During renewal discussions.
- When significant changes occur.
- After major incidents.

---

# 13. Incident Management

Where partnership incidents occur, TJF should:

1. Record the issue.
2. Assess impact.
3. Take corrective action.
4. Communicate appropriately.
5. Document lessons learned.

---

# 14. Learning and Improvement

TJF will strengthen partnership risk management through:

- Lessons learned.
- Partner feedback.
- Internal reviews.
- Policy improvements.

---

# Conclusion

Effective partnership risk management allows The Jopetnah Foundation to pursue collaboration opportunities while protecting its values, resources and reputation.

Through proactive assessment, responsible governance and continuous monitoring, TJF builds partnerships that are both impactful and sustainable.

---

> **Strong partnerships require trust, but sustainable partnerships require responsible risk management.**
