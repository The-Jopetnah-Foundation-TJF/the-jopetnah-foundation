# Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) Guide

## Executive Summary

This Guide provides a standardized approach for preparing, reviewing, approving, implementing and managing Memoranda of Understanding (MoUs) entered into by The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF).

Its purpose is to ensure that all MoUs are consistent, transparent, legally appropriate and aligned with TJF's mission, governance standards and strategic objectives.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Guide aims to:

- Standardize the preparation of MoUs.
- Clarify roles and responsibilities.
- Promote accountability.
- Reduce legal and operational risks.
- Support effective partnership management.
- Ensure proper documentation and record keeping.

---

# 2. Scope

This Guide applies to all non-commercial collaborative agreements between TJF and:

- Government institutions.
- Development partners.
- Donors and foundations.
- NGOs and CSOs.
- Academic institutions.
- Research organizations.
- Corporate organizations.
- Community-based organizations.
- International agencies.

Where legally binding obligations are intended, a contract or other formal legal agreement may be more appropriate than an MoU.

---

# 3. What is an MoU?

A Memorandum of Understanding is a written document that records the intentions, objectives and responsibilities of parties working together toward shared goals.

An MoU typically establishes:

- Areas of cooperation.
- Roles and responsibilities.
- Governance arrangements.
- Communication mechanisms.
- Reporting expectations.
- Duration of collaboration.

Unless expressly stated, an MoU is generally intended to guide cooperation rather than create legally enforceable contractual obligations.

---

# 4. When an MoU Should Be Used

An MoU may be appropriate where partners intend to:

- Collaborate on programmes.
- Share knowledge or expertise.
- Conduct joint research.
- Coordinate advocacy initiatives.
- Deliver joint training.
- Exchange information.
- Explore future collaboration.

---

# 5. Standard MoU Structure

Each MoU should normally include:

1. Title
2. Parties
3. Background
4. Purpose
5. Objectives
6. Scope of Collaboration
7. Roles and Responsibilities
8. Governance Arrangements
9. Financial Arrangements (if applicable)
10. Information Sharing and Confidentiality
11. Intellectual Property
12. Branding and Public Communications
13. Monitoring and Review
14. Duration
15. Amendment Procedure
16. Termination
17. Dispute Resolution
18. Signatures

---

# 6. Required Clauses

## Parties

Clearly identify all participating organizations.

---

## Purpose

Explain why the MoU is being established.

---

## Objectives

Define measurable or clearly stated collaboration goals.

---

## Scope

Describe the activities covered by the MoU.

---

## Roles and Responsibilities

Specify what each party is expected to contribute.

---

## Governance

Define:

- Coordination arrangements.
- Decision-making processes.
- Focal persons.
- Meeting schedules.

---

## Financial Arrangements

Where relevant, clarify:

- Funding responsibilities.
- Cost sharing.
- Resource contributions.
- Budget management.

---

## Confidentiality

State how confidential information will be protected and shared.

---

## Intellectual Property

Clarify ownership and permitted use of:

- Reports.
- Research.
- Training materials.
- Publications.
- Digital content.
- Other jointly developed resources.

---

## Communications

Specify procedures for:

- Public announcements.
- Media engagement.
- Logo use.
- Joint branding.

---

## Duration

Specify:

- Effective date.
- Expiry date.
- Renewal options.

---

## Amendment

Describe how changes to the MoU may be proposed, reviewed and approved.

---

## Termination

State the circumstances under which either party may end the MoU and the required notice period.

---

## Dispute Resolution

Describe the preferred process for resolving disagreements, beginning with good-faith negotiations before escalating to mediation or other agreed mechanisms.

---

# 7. Approval Process

Before signature, each MoU should undergo:

1. Technical review.
2. Partnership review.
3. Financial review (where applicable).
4. Legal review (where appropriate).
5. Executive approval.
6. Signature by authorized representatives.

No MoU should be signed by personnel without delegated authority.

---

# 8. Signing Authority

Signing authority should follow TJF's Delegation of Authority Policy.

Generally:

| Document Type | Typical Signing Authority |
|---------------|---------------------------|
| Strategic MoU | Executive Director |
| High-value institutional MoU | Executive Director with Board oversight where required |
| Programme-specific MoU | Executive Director or delegated senior manager |
| Technical collaboration | Authorized officer as delegated |

---

# 9. Implementation

After signature:

- Share the executed MoU with all relevant teams.
- Assign focal persons.
- Develop an implementation plan.
- Schedule review meetings.
- Monitor agreed deliverables.

---

# 10. Monitoring

Progress should be reviewed periodically against agreed objectives.

Suggested indicators include:

| Indicator | Measurement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Activities completed | % |
| Meetings held | Number |
| Deliverables achieved | Number |
| Joint outputs produced | Number |
| Stakeholder satisfaction | Survey results |

---

# 11. Amendment

Amendments should:

- Be documented in writing.
- Be agreed by all parties.
- Receive the same level of approval as the original MoU where appropriate.
- Be retained with the original agreement.

---

# 12. Renewal

Before expiry, partners should assess:

- Achievement of objectives.
- Continued strategic relevance.
- Performance of both parties.
- Opportunities for expansion or revision.

A renewal decision should be documented before the expiry date.

---

# 13. Termination

An MoU may be terminated:

- By mutual agreement.
- Following material breach.
- Due to changes in law or organizational mandate.
- When objectives have been achieved.
- Where continuation presents unacceptable risk.

Termination should be documented and communicated to affected stakeholders.

---

# 14. Record Keeping

The Partnerships & External Relations Department should maintain:

- Signed MoUs.
- Amendments.
- Supporting correspondence.
- Meeting minutes.
- Progress reports.
- Renewal and termination records.

Records should be retained in accordance with TJF's Records Management Policy.

---

# 15. Roles and Responsibilities

## Executive Director

- Approves and signs MoUs within delegated authority.
- Provides strategic oversight for significant institutional agreements.

---

## Partnerships & External Relations Department

Responsible for:

- Coordinating MoU development.
- Maintaining templates.
- Managing review processes.
- Tracking implementation and renewals.

---

## Programme Teams

Responsible for:

- Drafting technical content.
- Delivering agreed activities.
- Monitoring progress.
- Reporting on implementation.

---

## Finance Department

Responsible for:

- Reviewing financial commitments.
- Confirming budget availability.
- Monitoring financial obligations where applicable.

---

## Legal Adviser (where applicable)

Responsible for:

- Reviewing legal provisions.
- Advising on risks.
- Supporting dispute resolution.

---

# 16. Review

This Guide should be reviewed:

- Every three years.
- Following significant legal or regulatory changes.
- After major partnership evaluations.
- When organizational governance arrangements change.

---

# Annex A – MoU Preparation Checklist

Before submission for approval, confirm that the MoU includes:

- ☐ Parties correctly identified.
- ☐ Clear purpose and objectives.
- ☐ Defined roles and responsibilities.
- ☐ Governance arrangements.
- ☐ Financial provisions (if applicable).
- ☐ Confidentiality clause.
- ☐ Intellectual property clause.
- ☐ Communications and branding provisions.
- ☐ Monitoring arrangements.
- ☐ Duration and renewal terms.
- ☐ Amendment process.
- ☐ Termination clause.
- ☐ Authorized signatures.

---

# Conclusion

A well-prepared Memorandum of Understanding provides a strong foundation for successful collaboration.

By applying this Guide, The Jopetnah Foundation ensures that all MoUs are consistent, transparent and aligned with organizational values, reducing risk while promoting productive and sustainable partnerships.
