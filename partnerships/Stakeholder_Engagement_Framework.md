# Stakeholder Engagement Framework

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) recognizes that meaningful engagement with stakeholders is essential for achieving sustainable impact.

Stakeholders influence, contribute to, or are affected by TJF's programmes and operations. Effective engagement ensures that decisions are informed by diverse perspectives, strengthens accountability and builds trust.

This Framework establishes TJF's approach to identifying, communicating with and involving stakeholders throughout the organization and its programmes.

---

# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Framework is to:

- Establish a consistent stakeholder engagement approach.
- Strengthen relationships.
- Improve communication.
- Promote participation.
- Support transparency.
- Enhance programme effectiveness.
- Build long-term trust.

---

# 2. Scope

This Framework applies to engagement with:

- Communities.
- Beneficiaries.
- Government institutions.
- Donors.
- Development partners.
- NGOs.
- Private sector organizations.
- Academic institutions.
- Employees.
- Volunteers.
- Board members.
- Media and public audiences.

---

# 3. Stakeholder Engagement Principles

## Inclusion

TJF seeks to ensure that diverse voices are considered, especially those of vulnerable and marginalized groups.

---

## Transparency

Stakeholders should receive accurate and timely information.

---

## Respect

Engagement should recognize different cultures, perspectives and experiences.

---

## Responsiveness

Stakeholder feedback should inform decisions and improvements.

---

## Accountability

TJF remains responsible for explaining decisions, actions and results.

---

# 4. Stakeholder Identification

Stakeholders are identified based on:

- Influence over TJF activities.
- Interest in TJF programmes.
- Impact from TJF decisions.
- Contribution to programme success.

---

# 5. Stakeholder Categories

## Primary Stakeholders

Those directly affected by TJF programmes.

Examples:

- Communities.
- Beneficiaries.
- Programme participants.

---

## Strategic Stakeholders

Organizations influencing TJF's ability to achieve objectives.

Examples:

- Government agencies.
- Donors.
- Development partners.

---

## Operational Stakeholders

Groups supporting implementation.

Examples:

- Suppliers.
- Contractors.
- Volunteers.
- Local organizations.

---

## Internal Stakeholders

Members of the Foundation.

Examples:

- Board of Trustees.
- Management.
- Employees.

---

# 6. Stakeholder Mapping

TJF uses stakeholder mapping to assess:

| Factor | Description |
|---|---|
| Interest | Level of concern or involvement |
| Influence | Ability to affect outcomes |
| Expectations | What stakeholders require |
| Engagement Needs | Appropriate communication approach |

---

# 7. Stakeholder Engagement Matrix

| Stakeholder Group | Engagement Approach |
|---|---|
| Communities | Consultation, meetings, feedback channels |
| Beneficiaries | Participation, surveys, feedback mechanisms |
| Government | Coordination, reporting, policy engagement |
| Donors | Reporting, reviews, strategic discussions |
| Partners | Joint planning and performance reviews |
| Employees | Internal communication and consultation |
| Public | Information sharing and awareness |

- Strategic stakeholder relationships.

Responsible for:

# 12. Community Participation

TJF recognizes communities as partners in development.

Community participation may include:

- Programme design.
- Needs assessment.
- Implementation.
- Monitoring.
- Evaluation.
- Learning.

---

# 13. Managing Stakeholder Expectations

TJF shall:

- Clearly communicate objectives.
- Define commitments.
- Avoid unrealistic promises.
- Provide updates.
- Address concerns responsibly.

---

# 14. Measuring Engagement Effectiveness

TJF may monitor:

- Stakeholder satisfaction.
- Number of engagement activities.
- Feedback received.
- Participation levels.
- Partnership quality.
- Issues resolved.

---

# 15. Stakeholder Risk Management

Potential risks include:

- Poor communication.
- Conflicting expectations.
- Lack of participation.
- Reputation concerns.
- Stakeholder disengagement.

Risks should be identified and managed proactively.

---

# 16. Continuous Improvement

TJF will strengthen stakeholder engagement through:

- Regular feedback.
- Relationship reviews.
- Learning sessions.
- Improved communication methods.

---

# Review

This Framework shall be reviewed every two years or earlier where organizational priorities or stakeholder needs change.

---
