# Corporate Partnership Guide

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) recognizes the private sector as a key contributor to sustainable development through innovation, investment, technical expertise and responsible business practices.

This Guide establishes the principles and procedures for developing, approving, implementing and evaluating corporate partnerships. It is intended to ensure that all collaborations are ethical, transparent, mutually beneficial and aligned with TJF's mission and strategic priorities.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Guide aims to:

- Promote responsible collaboration with the private sector.
- Ensure mission alignment.
- Strengthen accountability and transparency.
- Support sustainable resource mobilization.
- Protect TJF's independence and reputation.
- Encourage shared value and long-term impact.

---

# 2. Scope

This Guide applies to partnerships with:

- Private companies.
- Social enterprises.
- Industry associations.
- Chambers of commerce.
- Corporate foundations.
- Business networks.
- Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs).
- Multinational corporations.

---

# 3. Guiding Principles

Corporate partnerships shall be based on:

- Shared values.
- Mutual respect.
- Transparency.
- Accountability.
- Ethical conduct.
- Sustainability.
- Inclusiveness.
- Long-term value creation.

---

# 4. Partnership Objectives

Corporate partnerships may seek to:

- Mobilize financial resources.
- Deliver joint programmes.
- Promote innovation.
- Build technical capacity.
- Expand community outreach.
- Increase volunteer participation.
- Support environmental and social initiatives.
- Improve livelihoods and economic inclusion.

---

# 5. Types of Corporate Partnerships

Examples include:

## Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)

Support for community development, education, health, environmental conservation and other social impact initiatives.

---

## Strategic Partnerships

Long-term collaborations aligned with TJF's strategic priorities.

---

## Sponsorships

Support for events, campaigns, publications or community initiatives.

---

## Employee Volunteering

Corporate employees contribute professional skills or volunteer time to TJF programmes.

---

## Skills-Based Partnerships

Provision of technical expertise such as:

- Legal services.
- Financial management.
- Technology.
- Engineering.
- Marketing.
- Human resources.

---

## In-Kind Contributions

Examples include:

- Equipment.
- Software.
- Office supplies.
- Medical supplies.
- Training materials.
- Professional services.

---

## Shared Value Initiatives

Collaborative projects that generate measurable benefits for both communities and participating businesses.

---

# 6. Partner Selection Criteria

Potential corporate partners should be assessed for:

- Mission compatibility.
- Ethical business practices.
- Regulatory compliance.
- Financial stability.
- Reputation.
- Environmental and social responsibility.
- Human rights commitments.
- Labour practices.
- Diversity and inclusion.

All prospective partners should undergo due diligence before approval.

---

# 7. Ethical Considerations

TJF will not knowingly enter into partnerships that could compromise its mission, independence or public trust.

Partnerships should be carefully evaluated where there are concerns relating to:

- Corruption or fraud.
- Human rights abuses.
- Environmental harm.
- Child labour or forced labour.
- Discrimination.
- Significant unresolved legal or regulatory violations.

Any exceptions must be approved through TJF's governance processes.

---

# 8. Branding and Communications

Corporate partners may use TJF's name or logo only with prior written approval.

All joint communications should:

- Reflect agreed messaging.
- Protect TJF's reputation.
- Comply with Brand Identity Guidelines.
- Clearly define branding responsibilities.

Cause-related marketing campaigns must be documented in writing before launch.

---

# 9. Financial Contributions

Financial support may include:

- Grants.
- Sponsorships.
- Donations.
- Matching gift programmes.
- Employee giving campaigns.

Financial arrangements should be transparent, documented and managed in accordance with TJF's financial policies.

---

# 10. Governance

Each corporate partnership should define:

- Objectives.
- Deliverables.
- Roles and responsibilities.
- Governance arrangements.
- Reporting requirements.
- Performance indicators.
- Duration.
- Exit arrangements.

Major partnerships should be documented through a Memorandum of Understanding or other appropriate agreement.

---

# 11. Risk Management

Potential risks include:

- Reputational risk.
- Mission drift.
- Financial dependency.
- Conflicts of interest.
- Non-compliance.
- Public perception.

Risk assessments should be completed before entering significant partnerships.

---

# 12. Monitoring and Evaluation

Corporate partnerships should be reviewed against agreed indicators such as:

| Indicator | Measurement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Financial resources mobilized | Value |
| Joint initiatives completed | Number |
| Employee volunteers engaged | Number |
| Beneficiaries reached | Number |
| Community outcomes achieved | Qualitative / Quantitative |
| Partner satisfaction | Survey results |

Performance reviews should inform future collaboration.

---

# 13. Roles and Responsibilities

## Board of Trustees

- Oversees major strategic corporate partnerships.
- Ensures alignment with TJF's mission and governance standards.

---

## Executive Director

- Approves significant corporate partnerships.
- Represents TJF in high-level engagements.

---

## Partnerships & External Relations Department

Responsible for:

- Partnership identification.
- Due diligence coordination.
- Agreement management.
- Relationship management.
- Partnership monitoring.

---

## Communications Department

Responsible for:

- Branding approvals.
- Joint communication planning.
- Media coordination.
- Public announcements.

---

## Finance Department

Responsible for:

- Reviewing financial arrangements.
- Monitoring funds received.
- Ensuring financial compliance.

---

## Programme Teams

Responsible for:

- Implementing agreed activities.
- Coordinating with corporate partners.
- Reporting results.
- Identifying opportunities for continuous improvement.

---

# 14. Review

This Guide should be reviewed:

- Every three years.
- Following major partnership evaluations.
- When relevant legislation changes.
- When organizational priorities evolve.

---

# Conclusion

Responsible corporate partnerships enable TJF to expand its reach, strengthen innovation and mobilize resources while maintaining its integrity and independence.

Through this Guide, The Jopetnah Foundation establishes a consistent and accountable approach to engaging the private sector in ways that generate measurable and sustainable benefits for communities and all partners involved.

---

> **The strongest corporate partnerships create shared value—where business success and community well-being advance together.**
