Our Programs

Four pillars. Thirteen program pathways. One integrated purpose.

The Jopetnah Foundation is developing a portfolio of programs designed to strengthen education, health and wellbeing, livelihoods, environmental resilience and community capacity in underserved communities in Uganda.

Our approach: TJF does not treat the four pillars as isolated charitable causes. We seek to understand how challenges interact within households and communities and design practical interventions that can reinforce one another.
Current status: All programs listed below are in the design stage. Legal registration in Uganda is in progress. First pilots are planned once governance, safeguarding systems and initial partnerships are in place. No programs are currently operational.

Program Architecture

Designed around real-life interconnected needs.

A child may struggle to remain in school because a household is under financial pressure. A health crisis may reduce a family's ability to work. Unsafe water may contribute to illness and lost school or work days. Climate shocks can undermine livelihoods.

TJF's program architecture is designed to respond to those relationships while remaining practical, measurable and locally grounded.

Program development principles

  • Community-informed design and participation
  • Clear beneficiary selection and eligibility criteria
  • Measurable outputs and outcomes
  • Safeguarding and responsible implementation
  • Evidence, learning and adaptation
  • Long-term sustainability and local ownership
Pillar One

Education & Youth Development

Building educational opportunity, literacy, digital readiness, skills and pathways for children and young people.

Design Stage

Jopetnah Scholars

A proposed education-support and mentorship pathway for learners facing documented financial barriers to secondary or tertiary education.

Focus

  • Needs-based education support
  • Mentorship and study skills
  • Career guidance and transition support
  • Learner and caregiver follow-up
Design Stage

Future-Ready Labs

A proposed digital and practical-skills initiative designed to give underserved young people greater exposure to technology, employability skills and future-oriented learning.

Focus

  • Digital literacy
  • Practical technology skills
  • Project-based learning
  • Career and entrepreneurship exposure
Design Stage

Read to Lead

A proposed literacy and reading-access initiative supporting children, caregivers, schools and communities with books, reading spaces and community-led reading activities.

Focus

  • Community libraries and mobile reading resources
  • Reading clubs and storytelling
  • Book access and circulation
  • Caregiver and volunteer engagement
Indicative Year-1 pilot goal (subject to funding & partnerships): Sponsor a first cohort of vulnerable students, sized to what we can fund through each student's full school cycle, and establish one Future-Ready digital literacy hub, with student numbers matched to the hub's device capacity.
Pillar Two

Health & Wellbeing

Supporting healthier communities through prevention, health education, referral pathways and locally appropriate wellbeing initiatives.

Design Stage

Community Health Champions

A proposed community-based health education model using trained local champions to strengthen prevention knowledge, early recognition and appropriate referral.

Focus

  • Community health education
  • Prevention awareness
  • Early recognition and referral
  • Links with existing health services
Design Stage

Mobile Wellness Clinics

A proposed outreach model designed to connect underserved communities with selected preventive, screening, health-education and referral services through qualified providers.

Focus

  • Community outreach
  • Preventive screening
  • Maternal and adolescent health support where locally appropriate
  • Referral and follow-up
Design Stage

Malaria Prevention & Response

A proposed community-focused malaria initiative designed to strengthen prevention awareness, timely care-seeking and appropriate referral in coordination with qualified health and public-health partners.

Focus

  • Prevention education
  • Early care-seeking awareness
  • Community campaigns
  • Coordination with malaria-control and health partners
Design Stage

Mind & Body

A proposed health and wellbeing initiative integrating mental wellbeing, nutrition, healthy behaviors and age-appropriate adolescent health education.

Focus

  • Mental wellbeing awareness
  • Nutrition education
  • Healthy lifestyle education
  • Appropriate referral to qualified providers
Indicative Year-1 pilot goal (subject to funding & partnerships): Train 20 Community Health Champions, working with the District Health Office and existing Village Health Teams, to support net use, timely care-seeking, antenatal attendance and malaria-vaccine completion for pregnant women and children under five in our target district.
Pillar Three

Poverty Alleviation & Livelihoods

Creating pathways toward income, enterprise, financial resilience and greater household economic self-reliance.

Design Stage

Jopetnah Micro-Grants

A proposed livelihood-support initiative combining targeted seed capital, training and follow-up support for eligible entrepreneurs, with emphasis on women, youth and vulnerable households where appropriate.

Focus

  • Seed capital linked to training
  • Entrepreneurship and financial literacy
  • Business coaching
  • Household economic resilience
Design Stage

Skills-to-Income Pipeline

A proposed pathway linking vocational and employability training with employer connections, apprenticeships, self-employment and market demand.

Focus

  • Market-informed skills development
  • Work readiness
  • Apprenticeship and employer linkages
  • Self-employment pathways
Design Stage

Cooperative Savings Circles

A proposed community savings and financial-literacy model that helps participating groups strengthen saving habits, financial planning, shock resilience and productive investment.

Focus

  • Community savings groups
  • Financial literacy
  • Transparent group governance
  • Appropriate links to formal financial services
Indicative Year-1 pilot goal (subject to funding & partnerships): Establish 5 Village Savings and Loan Association (VSLA) groups (≈125 members) and support the strongest business plans with a smaller number of seed grants (about $150–$200 each), business training and follow-up.
Pillar Four

Environment & Community Development

Strengthening climate resilience through safer water and sanitation access, climate-smart agriculture, and community-based environmental stewardship — helping households adapt to a changing climate while building healthier, more sustainable communities.

Design Stage

Green Neighborhoods

A proposed community environmental initiative supporting tree planting, clean-up campaigns, greener public spaces and local environmental stewardship.

Focus

  • Tree planting and maintenance
  • Community clean-up activities
  • Green spaces and community gardens where feasible
  • Youth and community environmental engagement
Design Stage

Water & Sanitation Access

A proposed community initiative to improve access to safer water, sanitation and hygiene solutions through technically appropriate, community-owned approaches.

Focus

  • Needs and technical assessments
  • Appropriate water solutions
  • Sanitation and hygiene education
  • Community management and maintenance
Design Stage

Climate-Smart Livelihoods

A proposed initiative linking climate resilience with household livelihoods through practical adaptation, sustainable production and income-diversification opportunities.

Focus

  • Climate-resilient livelihood practices
  • Sustainable agriculture and resource use
  • Income diversification
  • Market and producer linkages
Indicative Year-1 pilot goal (subject to funding & partnerships): With two local schools, establish indigenous and fruit trees on land confirmed by a site assessment (up to 5,000), tracked for 12-month survival, and rehabilitate one district-listed community borehole for the community it is designed to serve.
Integrated Programming

Programs are designed to reinforce one another.

TJF's integrated model allows complementary programs to work together around household and community needs.

For example, educational support may be strengthened by health and nutrition interventions; livelihoods may be made more resilient through climate-smart practices; and water and sanitation improvements can contribute to better health and stronger participation in school or work.

A pathway, not a collection of isolated projects

  • Assess needs and barriers
  • Prioritize appropriate interventions
  • Deliver with local participation
  • Measure outputs and outcomes
  • Learn and adapt
  • Strengthen local capacity and sustainability
Indicative timeline

From design to responsible delivery.

The sequence below is a planning framework, not a commitment to dates or delivery. Timing depends on legal registration, governance approvals, funding, community priorities, safeguarding and partner readiness.

Stage 1 — Current

Foundation and readiness

Continue the registration process; form and orient governance structures; approve essential controls; develop programme designs, budgets and monitoring plans.

Stage 2

Pilot preparation

Confirm community priorities, assess partners, establish baselines, define safeguards and secure appropriate resources before deciding whether to launch pilots.

Stage 3

Learning and adaptation

If pilots proceed, review delivery quality and evidence with communities and partners; adapt the approach and publish verified learning where appropriate.

Stage 4

Responsible scale

Consider expansion only where evidence, local capacity, partnership quality and sustainable funding justify broader reach.

Note: We will update this page publicly as verified milestones are reached.
Program Accountability

We intend to measure what changes.

TJF's program development approach is designed around measurable results, transparent reporting and continuous learning.

Outputs

What was delivered: learners supported, training sessions held, people reached, groups formed, infrastructure established and other directly measurable activities.

Outcomes

What changed: improved retention, skills, health knowledge, income-generating activity, resilience, access or community practices.

Important: Program targets, budgets and implementation status will be adapted to available resources, community needs, technical assessments, safeguarding requirements, local partnerships and donor requirements. TJF will distinguish clearly between planned programs, pilot activities and documented results.
