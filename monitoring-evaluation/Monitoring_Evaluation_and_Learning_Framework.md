# Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) Framework

## 1. Purpose

The Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) Framework establishes the institutional approach used by The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) to plan, monitor, evaluate, report on and continuously improve its programmes.

The Framework provides a consistent methodology for measuring performance, demonstrating accountability, generating evidence and strengthening organizational learning.

---

# 2. Scope

This Framework applies to:

- All Foundation programmes
- Projects funded by donors
- Strategic partnerships
- Pilot initiatives
- Research activities
- Community engagement initiatives
- Internal organizational performance where appropriate

It is applicable across all departments and programme areas.

---

# 3. Objectives

The Foundation seeks to:

- Measure programme performance consistently.
- Assess outcomes and long-term impact.
- Strengthen accountability to stakeholders.
- Improve programme quality.
- Promote evidence-based decision-making.
- Support adaptive management.
- Meet donor reporting obligations.
- Build institutional knowledge.

---

# 4. Guiding Principles

## Accountability

The Foundation remains accountable to beneficiaries, donors, partners, regulators and its Board of Trustees.

---

## Participation

Communities, partners and beneficiaries should participate meaningfully in programme monitoring, evaluation and learning.

---

## Evidence

Programme decisions should be informed by reliable, timely and relevant data.

---

## Learning

Monitoring and evaluation are not only compliance activities but opportunities for organizational learning and innovation.

---

## Transparency

Information should be communicated openly, accurately and responsibly.

---

## Continuous Improvement

Evidence should be used to improve programme quality and institutional effectiveness.

---

# 5. MEAL System Components

The Foundation's MEAL system consists of the following interconnected components:

## Programme Planning

Defines objectives, outcomes, outputs, indicators and assumptions before implementation begins.

---

## Monitoring

Tracks implementation progress against approved work plans and performance indicators.

Monitoring focuses on:

- Activities
- Outputs
- Timelines
- Budgets
- Risks
- Compliance

---

## Evaluation

Evaluations assess:

- Relevance
- Effectiveness
- Efficiency
- Sustainability
- Impact
- Lessons learned

Evaluations may be internal, external or jointly commissioned.

---

## Accountability

The Foundation maintains accountability through:

- Stakeholder engagement
- Financial transparency
- Beneficiary feedback
- Complaints mechanisms
- Ethical conduct
- Regular reporting

---

## Learning

Learning activities include:

- Reflection meetings
- After-action reviews
- Lessons learned workshops
- Evaluation dissemination
- Knowledge sharing
- Best practice documentation

Learning informs future programme design and strategic planning.

---

# 6. Results Chain

The Foundation uses the following results chain:

```text
Inputs
   │
   ▼
Activities
   │
   ▼
Outputs
   │
   ▼
Outcomes
   │
   ▼
Impact
```

### Inputs

Resources invested in programmes.

Examples:

- Funding
- Staff
- Equipment
- Partnerships
- Technology

---

### Activities

Actions undertaken using available resources.

Examples:

- Training
- Community meetings
- Awareness campaigns
- Service delivery
- Research

---

### Outputs

Immediate products or services delivered.

Examples:

- Number of workshops conducted
- People trained
- Schools supported
- Health outreaches completed

---

### Outcomes

Short- to medium-term changes resulting from programme activities.

Examples:

- Improved knowledge
- Increased income
- Better health practices
- Enhanced institutional capacity

---

### Impact

Long-term sustainable change.

Examples:

- Reduced poverty
- Improved quality of life
- Stronger institutions
- Resilient communities

---

# 7. Performance Indicators

Indicators should be:

- Specific
- Measurable
- Achievable
- Relevant
- Time-bound (SMART)

Indicators may be:

- Input indicators
- Output indicators
- Outcome indicators
- Impact indicators

Each indicator should have:

- Definition
- Calculation method
- Data source
- Collection frequency
- Responsible officer
- Target value

---

# 8. Data Collection

Common data collection methods include:

- Surveys
- Interviews
- Focus group discussions
- Observation
- Administrative records
- Digital systems
- GIS and mapping
- Mobile data collection
- Document review

Methods should be appropriate to programme objectives and ethical standards.

---

# 9. Data Quality

The Foundation is committed to ensuring data is:

- Accurate
- Complete
- Consistent
- Timely
- Reliable
- Secure

Data Quality Assessments (DQAs) should be conducted periodically to verify data integrity.

---

# 10. Reporting

Reporting supports accountability and learning.

Typical reports include:

- Monthly programme reports
- Quarterly progress reports
- Annual reports
- Donor reports
- Financial reports
- Evaluation reports
- Lessons learned reports

Reporting schedules should align with donor agreements and organizational requirements.

---

# 11. Evaluation Types

The Foundation may undertake:

- Baseline evaluations
- Mid-term evaluations
- End-line evaluations
- Process evaluations
- Outcome evaluations
- Impact evaluations
- Independent external evaluations

The type of evaluation should reflect the scale, complexity and objectives of the programme.

---

# 12. Organizational Learning

Learning is embedded throughout the programme cycle.

Key learning mechanisms include:

- Reflection sessions
- Peer reviews
- Knowledge products
- Communities of practice
- Technical workshops
- Management reviews

Documented lessons should inform future programme design, resource allocation and policy development.

---

# 13. Roles and Responsibilities

## Board of Trustees

- Provides oversight of organizational performance.
- Reviews strategic results and evaluation findings.

---

## Executive Director

- Champions evidence-based management.
- Approves major evaluations.
- Ensures findings influence strategic decisions.

---

## MEAL Team

Responsible for:

- Indicator development
- Data management
- Quality assurance
- Reporting
- Evaluation coordination
- Organizational learning

---

## Programme Teams

Responsible for:

- Monitoring implementation
- Collecting quality data
- Maintaining programme records
- Supporting evaluations
- Applying lessons learned

---

## Finance Team

Responsible for:

- Budget monitoring
- Financial reporting
- Verification of financial data
- Compliance with donor financial requirements

---

# 14. Ethical Considerations

All monitoring and evaluation activities shall:

- Respect participant dignity.
- Protect personal information.
- Obtain informed consent where required.
- Avoid harm.
- Promote inclusion.
- Maintain confidentiality.
- Comply with safeguarding requirements.

---

# 15. Continuous Improvement

The Foundation regularly reviews its MEAL system through:

- Internal audits
- External evaluations
- Data Quality Assessments
- Stakeholder feedback
- Donor recommendations
- Lessons learned
- Emerging international standards

Recommendations should be incorporated into future planning and implementation.

---

# 16. Framework Review

This Framework shall be reviewed every two years, or earlier where significant organizational, strategic or regulatory changes occur.

---

> **The Jopetnah Foundation believes that sustainable development is achieved when decisions are informed by evidence, communities are heard, accountability is embraced and learning becomes part of organizational culture.**
