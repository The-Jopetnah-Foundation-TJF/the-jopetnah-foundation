# Logical Framework (Logframe) Development Guide

## Executive Summary

The Logical Framework (Logframe) is The Jopetnah Foundation's standard tool for planning, implementing, monitoring and evaluating projects and programmes.

It translates the Foundation's Theory of Change and Results Framework into project-level objectives, indicators, means of verification and assumptions.

This guide establishes a consistent methodology for preparing logframes across all Foundation initiatives.

---

# Purpose

The Guide aims to:

- Standardize project design.
- Improve proposal quality.
- Strengthen monitoring.
- Improve donor reporting.
- Improve project management.
- Strengthen accountability.
- Support evidence-based decision making.

---

# What is a Logframe?

A Logical Framework is a structured planning matrix that demonstrates:

- What the project intends to achieve.
- How success will be measured.
- Where evidence will come from.
- Which assumptions may influence success.

The Foundation uses the logframe as both a planning and management tool.

---

# The Four-Level Results Structure

Every project should define four levels of results:

```text
Goal (Impact)

        ▲

Purpose (Outcome)

        ▲

Outputs

        ▲

Activities
```

Each level contributes logically to the next.

---

# Level 1 – Goal (Impact)

The Goal represents the long-term development change to which the project contributes.

Characteristics:

- Long-term
- Broad
- Strategic
- Often influenced by multiple stakeholders

Example:

> Improved socioeconomic wellbeing of vulnerable communities.

---

# Level 2 – Purpose (Outcome)

The Purpose describes the direct change expected as a result of the project.

Characteristics:

- Achievable within the project period
- Directly influenced by project activities
- Clearly measurable

Example:

> Increased employment opportunities for youth through vocational skills development.

---

# Level 3 – Outputs

Outputs are the immediate products and services delivered by project activities.

Examples:

- Training completed
- Community groups established
- Equipment distributed
- Schools renovated
- Health campaigns conducted

Outputs should always be measurable.

---

# Level 4 – Activities

Activities are the tasks undertaken to produce outputs.

Examples include:

- Conduct training sessions
- Recruit participants
- Procure equipment
- Develop learning materials
- Carry out community mobilization
- Conduct supervision visits

---

# Standard Logframe Matrix

| Results Level | Performance Indicators | Means of Verification | Assumptions |
|----------------|-----------------------|-----------------------|-------------|
| Goal | Long-term indicators | National surveys, evaluations | External conditions remain favorable |
| Purpose | Outcome indicators | Project surveys, assessments | Beneficiaries apply knowledge |
| Outputs | Output indicators | Activity reports, registers | Activities completed on schedule |
| Activities | Implementation milestones | Work plans, reports | Resources available |

---

# Developing Indicators

Every indicator should be:

- Specific
- Measurable
- Achievable
- Relevant
- Time-bound

Indicators should align with the Foundation's Indicator Reference Guide.

Each indicator should specify:

- Definition
- Unit of measurement
- Baseline
- Target
- Data source
- Collection frequency
- Responsible person

---

# Means of Verification

Evidence should be credible and accessible.

Examples include:

- Attendance registers
- Survey reports
- Financial reports
- Evaluation reports
- Photographs (where appropriate)
- GIS data
- Observation records
- Government statistics
- Monitoring reports

Verification methods should be proportionate to the scale of the project.

---

# Assumptions and Risks

Projects operate within changing environments.

Each logframe should identify assumptions that are necessary for success.

Examples:

- Community participation remains high.
- Funding is released on schedule.
- Weather conditions allow implementation.
- Local authorities continue supporting the project.

Significant risks should be documented in the project's Risk Register.

# Quality Assurance Checklist

Before approving a logframe, confirm that:

- Objectives align with the Foundation's mission.
- Results are logically connected.
- Indicators are measurable.
- Data sources are available.
- Risks have been considered.
- Assumptions are realistic.
- Targets are achievable.
- Budget supports planned activities.

- Monitor implementation.
- Report progress.

---

## MEAL Team

- Review indicators.
- Ensure consistency.
- Support data quality.
- Advise on measurement methods.

---

- Confirm budget alignment.
- Verify financial assumptions.

---

## Executive Director

- Approve final project logframes.
- Ensure strategic alignment.

---

# Common Mistakes to Avoid

Avoid:

- Confusing outputs with outcomes.
- Writing vague objectives.
- Using indicators that cannot be measured.
- Setting unrealistic targets.
- Ignoring assumptions.
- Designing activities without clear outputs.
- Collecting unnecessary data.
- Developing indicators without baselines.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Following project completion, logframes should be reviewed to determine:

- Which assumptions proved correct.
- Which indicators were most useful.
- Lessons learned.
- Opportunities to improve future project design.

---

# Review

This Guide shall be reviewed every two years or whenever significant changes occur in organizational practice or donor requirements.
