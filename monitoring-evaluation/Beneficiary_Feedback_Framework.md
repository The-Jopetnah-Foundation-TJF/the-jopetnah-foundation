# Beneficiary Feedback Framework

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) recognizes that communities and programme participants are essential partners in development.

This Beneficiary Feedback Framework establishes mechanisms through which individuals, communities and stakeholders can provide opinions, suggestions, concerns and recommendations regarding Foundation programmes and services.

The Framework ensures that community voices contribute to programme improvement, accountability and organizational learning.

---

# Purpose

This Framework aims to:

- Strengthen accountability to communities.
- Improve programme quality.
- Encourage meaningful participation.
- Identify challenges early.
- Promote transparency.
- Build trust with stakeholders.
- Support evidence-based adaptation.

---

# Scope

This Framework applies to:

- All Foundation programmes.
- Community development initiatives.
- Training programmes.
- Health and education interventions.
- Livelihood projects.
- Partnerships involving community engagement.
- Digital and physical service delivery channels.

---

# Guiding Principles

## Respect and Dignity

Every individual providing feedback should be treated respectfully regardless of age, gender, background or status.

---

## Accessibility

Feedback mechanisms should be accessible to different groups, including:

- Women.
- Youth.
- Persons with disabilities.
- Vulnerable populations.
- Remote communities.

---

## Confidentiality

Feedback information should be handled responsibly and shared only with authorized personnel.

---

## Responsiveness

Feedback should receive acknowledgement, review and appropriate action.

---

## Non-Retaliation

Individuals should be able to provide feedback without fear of punishment, discrimination or negative consequences.

---

# Types of Feedback

Feedback may include:

## Compliments

Positive experiences, appreciation or recognition of good practice.

---

## Suggestions

Ideas for improving programmes, services or operations.

---

## Questions

Requests for information, clarification or assistance.

---

## Concerns

Issues affecting programme quality, accessibility or effectiveness.

---

## Complaints

Expressions of dissatisfaction requiring investigation and response.

Complaints are managed according to the Complaints and Response Mechanism.

---

# Feedback Channels

The Foundation may provide multiple feedback channels including:

## Community Meetings

Used for direct engagement, consultation and discussion.

---

## Feedback Forms

Available during programme activities or through Foundation offices.

---

## Telephone Channels

Dedicated contact numbers may be used for feedback submission.

---

## Digital Platforms

Feedback may be received through:

- Email.
- Website forms.
- Social media platforms.
- Digital surveys.

---

## Partner Channels

Where programmes are implemented through partners, feedback mechanisms should be integrated into partnership arrangements.

---

# Feedback Process

The standard feedback cycle is:

```text
Receive Feedback
        │
        ▼
Register Feedback
        │
        ▼
Classify Issue
        │
        ▼
Review & Assess
        │
        ▼
Respond
        │
        ▼
Take Corrective Action
        │
        ▼
Record Learning
```

---

# Feedback Management Standards

All feedback should:

- Be recorded appropriately.
- Receive timely attention.
- Be assigned responsibility.
- Be tracked until resolution.
- Inform improvement decisions where relevant.

---

# Response Time Standards

The Foundation should establish response timelines appropriate to the type and seriousness of feedback.

Example:

| Feedback Type | Target Response |
|---|---|
| General enquiry | Within 10 working days |
| Programme concern | Within 15 working days |
| Serious complaint | Immediate acknowledgement and priority review |
| Safeguarding concern | Immediate escalation |

---

# Roles and Responsibilities

## Board of Trustees

Responsible for:

- Oversight of accountability systems.
- Reviewing significant systemic issues.

---

## Executive Director

Responsible for:

- Ensuring feedback informs organizational decisions.
- Supporting accountability culture.

---

## MEAL Team

Responsible for:

- Managing feedback systems.
- Analysing trends.
- Reporting findings.
- Supporting learning processes.

---

## Programme Teams

Responsible for:

- Encouraging feedback participation.
- Responding appropriately.
- Implementing improvements.

---

## Community Representatives

Where appropriate:

- Support communication.
- Encourage participation.
- Help identify community priorities.

---

# Feedback Analysis

Feedback should be analysed to identify:

- Common concerns.
- Emerging risks.
- Programme strengths.
- Service improvement opportunities.
- Stakeholder satisfaction trends.

Analysis may inform:

- Programme redesign.
- Staff training.
- Policy improvement.
- Resource allocation.

---

# Inclusion Considerations

The Foundation should consider barriers affecting feedback participation, including:

- Language.
- Literacy.
- Disability.
- Location.
- Cultural norms.
- Fear of authority.

Feedback mechanisms should be adapted accordingly.

---

# Data Protection

Feedback information should:

- Be stored securely.
- Protect personal information.
- Limit access appropriately.
- Follow applicable data protection requirements.

---

# Monitoring Feedback Systems

The Foundation may track:

- Number of feedback submissions received.
- Percentage of feedback resolved.
- Average response time.
- Satisfaction with responses.
- Common categories of feedback.
- Improvements resulting from feedback.

---

# Learning and Improvement

Feedback should contribute to:

- Better programme design.
- Improved service delivery.
- Stronger community relationships.
- Organizational learning.

Feedback is considered a valuable source of evidence for decision-making.

---

# Review

This Framework shall be reviewed every two years or sooner if organizational needs, stakeholder expectations or regulatory requirements change.

---

> **Communities are not only recipients of development—they are partners whose voices guide better decisions, stronger programmes and sustainable impact.**
