# Disaster Risk Reduction Framework

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) recognizes that disasters can reverse development gains, threaten lives and livelihoods, and increase vulnerability among already at-risk populations.

This Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) Framework establishes TJF's systematic approach to identifying, assessing and reducing disaster risks through prevention, mitigation, preparedness and resilience-building. The Framework aligns humanitarian action with sustainable development by integrating disaster risk reduction into programmes, partnerships and community engagement.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Framework aims to:

- Reduce disaster risks and vulnerabilities.
- Strengthen community resilience.
- Protect lives, livelihoods and assets.
- Integrate disaster risk reduction into TJF programmes.
- Promote climate adaptation and sustainable development.
- Support evidence-based disaster risk management.

---

# 2. Scope

This Framework applies to:

- Natural hazards.
- Climate-related disasters.
- Public health emergencies.
- Environmental degradation.
- Technological and industrial hazards.
- Community resilience programmes.

It applies to:

- All TJF departments.
- Country and regional offices.
- Programme teams.
- Volunteers.
- Partner organizations.
- Community stakeholders.

---

# 3. Guiding Principles

TJF's disaster risk reduction efforts shall be guided by:

## Prevention

Take proactive action to reduce disaster risks before emergencies occur.

---

## Resilience

Strengthen the ability of communities and institutions to prepare for, withstand and recover from disasters.

---

## Community Participation

Engage communities as active partners in identifying risks and developing solutions.

---

## Inclusion

Ensure that disaster risk reduction activities address the needs of women, children, older persons, persons with disabilities and other vulnerable groups.

---

## Sustainability

Promote environmentally responsible and long-term risk reduction measures.

---

## Evidence-Based Decision Making

Use reliable data, scientific knowledge and local experience to inform planning and action.

---

# 4. Strategic Objectives

TJF seeks to:

- Reduce disaster-related losses.
- Improve preparedness at community and organizational levels.
- Strengthen local disaster management capacity.
- Promote resilient infrastructure and livelihoods.
- Encourage climate-smart development.
- Foster partnerships for disaster resilience.

---

# 5. Disaster Risk Reduction Components

## Risk Identification

Identify hazards, vulnerabilities and exposure through regular assessments.

---

## Risk Prevention

Implement measures that reduce the likelihood of disasters.

Examples include:

- Environmental conservation.
- Improved land-use planning.
- Public awareness.
- Safe construction practices.

---

## Risk Mitigation

Reduce the impact of identified hazards through:

- Structural mitigation measures.
- Community preparedness.
- Emergency planning.
- Infrastructure improvements.

---

## Preparedness

Maintain readiness through:

- Training.
- Early warning systems.
- Emergency planning.
- Resource pre-positioning.

---

## Recovery and Resilience

Support long-term recovery by:

- Restoring livelihoods.
- Strengthening institutions.
- Building resilient communities.
- Promoting sustainable development.

---

# 6. Disaster Risk Assessment Process

TJF shall use the following process:

```
Hazard Identification
          ↓
Vulnerability Assessment
          ↓
Exposure Analysis
          ↓
Risk Evaluation
          ↓
Risk Prioritization
          ↓
Mitigation Planning
          ↓
Monitoring and Review
```

---

# 7. Community-Based Disaster Risk Reduction

TJF shall support communities to:

- Conduct local risk mapping.
- Develop community disaster plans.
- Form community emergency committees.
- Participate in simulation exercises.
- Protect natural resources.
- Strengthen local coping capacities.

---

# 8. Early Warning and Early Action

TJF shall promote:

- Access to reliable hazard information.
- Community alert systems.
- Timely dissemination of warnings.
- Predefined early action procedures.
- Coordination with government warning systems.

---

# 9. Climate Change Integration

Recognizing the increasing impact of climate change, TJF shall integrate climate adaptation into disaster risk reduction by:

- Supporting climate-resilient livelihoods.
- Promoting sustainable agriculture.
- Encouraging ecosystem restoration.
- Supporting water resource management.
- Enhancing community adaptation planning.

---

# 10. Risk-Sensitive Programme Design

All major TJF programmes shall consider disaster risks during:

- Project planning.
- Budget preparation.
- Infrastructure development.
- Procurement.
- Community engagement.
- Monitoring and evaluation.

---

# 11. Capacity Building

TJF shall strengthen disaster risk reduction capacity through:

- Staff training.
- Community education.
- Volunteer development.
- Technical workshops.
- Knowledge exchange.
- Joint exercises with partners.

---

# 12. Partnership and Coordination

TJF shall collaborate with:

- National disaster management authorities.
- Local governments.
- Meteorological agencies.
- Academic institutions.
- NGOs.
- United Nations agencies.
- Community organizations.
- Private sector partners.

---

# 13. Monitoring and Evaluation

The effectiveness of disaster risk reduction initiatives shall be assessed through:

- Periodic risk assessments.
- Community resilience surveys.
- Programme evaluations.
- Lessons learned reviews.
- Independent assessments where appropriate.

This Framework shall be reviewed:

- Every three years.
- Following major disaster events.
- After significant changes in disaster risk profiles.
- Based on programme evaluations and lessons learned.

# Conclusion

Reducing disaster risk is essential to protecting development gains and strengthening resilient communities.

Through prevention, preparedness, collaboration and continuous learning, The Jopetnah Foundation is committed to supporting communities in anticipating, reducing and managing disaster risks while promoting sustainable development and human well-being.
