# Humanitarian Partnership Framework

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) recognizes that effective humanitarian action requires strong collaboration among governments, humanitarian organizations, communities, donors, private sector actors and development partners.

This Humanitarian Partnership Framework establishes the principles, processes and governance arrangements that guide TJF’s humanitarian partnerships to ensure coordinated, ethical, accountable and impactful emergency response.

Through strategic partnerships, TJF seeks to expand humanitarian reach, strengthen operational capacity and deliver sustainable support to communities affected by crises.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Framework aims to:

- Establish principles for humanitarian partnerships.
- Strengthen coordination during emergencies.
- Improve collective humanitarian impact.
- Promote accountability among partners.
- Support effective resource mobilization.
- Build long-term humanitarian networks.

---

# 2. Scope

This Framework applies to partnerships with:

- Government institutions.
- United Nations agencies.
- International NGOs.
- Local NGOs.
- Community-based organizations.
- Faith-based organizations.
- Academic institutions.
- Private sector partners.
- Philanthropic organizations.
- Humanitarian networks.

---

# 3. Partnership Principles

All humanitarian partnerships shall be guided by:

## Shared Purpose

Partners shall collaborate toward common humanitarian objectives.

---

## Respect and Inclusion

Partners shall value local knowledge, diversity and community participation.

---

## Accountability

Partners shall maintain transparency and responsibility for resources and activities.

---

## Complementarity

Partnerships shall strengthen existing capacities and avoid duplication.

---

## Ethical Conduct

Partners shall comply with TJF values, policies and safeguarding standards.

---

## Mutual Benefit

Partnerships shall create meaningful value for all stakeholders while prioritizing affected communities.

---

# 4. Strategic Partnership Objectives

TJF seeks to:

- Improve humanitarian coordination.
- Expand emergency response capacity.
- Access technical expertise.
- Mobilize additional resources.
- Strengthen local humanitarian leadership.
- Improve programme quality and sustainability.

---

# 5. Types of Humanitarian Partnerships

## Government Partnerships

Collaboration may include:

- Emergency coordination.
- Programme authorization.
- Technical cooperation.
- Public service support.
- Disaster response planning.

---

## United Nations and Multilateral Partnerships

TJF may collaborate with:

- Humanitarian coordination mechanisms.
- International development programmes.
- Technical agencies.
- Emergency response platforms.

---

## NGO Partnerships

Collaboration may involve:

- Joint emergency programmes.
- Shared technical expertise.
- Resource coordination.
- Capacity building.

---

## Community-Based Partnerships

TJF shall prioritize partnerships with:

- Local organizations.
- Community groups.
- Traditional leadership structures.
- Volunteer networks.

These partnerships strengthen local ownership and sustainability.

---

## Private Sector Partnerships

Private sector collaboration may include:

- Emergency funding.
- Logistics support.
- Technology solutions.
- In-kind donations.
- Corporate volunteering.

---

# 6. Partnership Identification Process

Potential partners shall be assessed based on:

- Strategic alignment.
- Technical capacity.
- Reputation.
- Legal compliance.
- Financial integrity.
- Humanitarian experience.
- Safeguarding commitment.

---

# 7. Partner Due Diligence

Before establishing partnerships, TJF shall review:

## Organizational Profile

Including:

- Registration status.
- Governance structure.
- Experience.

---

## Financial Capacity

Including:

- Financial systems.
- Audit history.
- Resource management capability.

---

## Compliance Standards

Including:

- Anti-fraud practices.
- Safeguarding policies.
- Ethical standards.
- Data protection measures.

---

# 8. Partnership Agreements

Humanitarian partnerships shall be formalized through appropriate agreements, including:

- Memoranda of Understanding (MOUs).
- Partnership agreements.
- Consortium agreements.
- Service agreements.
- Grant agreements.

Agreements shall define:

- Roles and responsibilities.
- Funding arrangements.
- Reporting requirements.
- Risk management responsibilities.
- Duration and termination conditions.

---

# 9. Humanitarian Coordination Mechanisms

TJF shall participate in:

- Emergency coordination meetings.
- Humanitarian clusters where appropriate.
- Government coordination platforms.
- Community consultation forums.
- Information-sharing networks.

---

# 10. Information Sharing

Partners shall establish mechanisms for:

- Situation updates.
- Needs assessments.
- Programme information.
- Resource coordination.
- Lessons learned.

Information sharing shall respect:

- Confidentiality requirements.
- Data protection standards.
- Beneficiary privacy.

---

# 11. Joint Emergency Planning

Where appropriate, TJF shall collaborate with partners on:

- Emergency preparedness planning.
- Joint assessments.
- Response strategies.
- Resource mobilization.
- Recovery planning.

---

# 12. Partnership Performance Management

Partnerships shall be monitored through:

- Progress reviews.
- Activity reports.
- Financial reporting.
- Field monitoring.
- Performance assessments.
- Feedback mechanisms.

---

# 13. Accountability and Safeguarding

All humanitarian partners shall comply with:

- TJF Code of Conduct.
- Safeguarding requirements.
- Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) standards.
- Child protection requirements.
- Anti-corruption policies.

Violations shall result in appropriate corrective action.

---

# 14. Conflict of Interest Management

Partners shall disclose:

- Potential conflicts of interest.
- Financial relationships.
- Personal interests affecting decisions.

TJF shall maintain impartial and transparent partnership decisions.

---

# 15. Partnership Risk Management

Potential risks include:

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Partner capacity limitations | Due diligence and capacity strengthening |
| Misuse of resources | Financial monitoring and audits |
| Reputation risks | Compliance checks and performance reviews |
| Coordination failures | Clear roles and communication systems |
| Safeguarding violations | Partner screening and reporting mechanisms |

---

# 16. Exit and Transition Management

Partnerships may conclude due to:

- Completion of objectives.
- Strategic changes.
- Non-compliance.
- Funding changes.
- Operational considerations.

Transitions shall be managed responsibly to protect affected communities.

---

## Board of Directors

- Strategic oversight.
- Approval of major partnerships.

---

- Partnership authorization.

- Strategic relationship management.

- Partner identification.
- Due diligence.
- Agreement management.
- Partnership monitoring.

---

## Finance Department

Responsible for:

- Financial oversight.
- Partner funding management.
- Compliance reviews.

---

## Programme Teams

Responsible for:

- Joint implementation.
- Technical coordination.
- Reporting.

This Framework shall be reviewed:

- Every three years.
- Following major humanitarian operations.
- After partnership evaluations.
- Following changes in humanitarian coordination standards.

# Conclusion

Strong humanitarian partnerships enable organizations to achieve greater impact than working alone.

Through ethical collaboration, shared responsibility and coordinated action, The Jopetnah Foundation strengthens its ability to respond effectively to crises while supporting communities with dignity, compassion and sustainable solutions.

---

> **Together, partnerships transform humanitarian challenges into collective opportunities for hope, recovery and resilience.**
