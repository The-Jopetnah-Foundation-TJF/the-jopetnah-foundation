# Emergency Preparedness Plan

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) recognizes that effective emergency response begins with thorough preparedness.

This Emergency Preparedness Plan establishes the systems, procedures and responsibilities required to ensure TJF can respond rapidly, safely and effectively to emergencies while protecting staff, volunteers, beneficiaries, assets and organizational operations.

The Plan promotes readiness through planning, training, coordination, resource management and continuous improvement.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Plan aims to:

- Strengthen organizational readiness.
- Improve emergency response capacity.
- Reduce disaster impacts.
- Protect people and organizational assets.
- Support business continuity.
- Enhance coordination with emergency partners.

---

# 2. Scope

This Plan applies to:

- Natural disasters.
- Disease outbreaks.
- Civil emergencies.
- Humanitarian crises.
- Fire incidents.
- Major accidents.
- Security incidents.
- Technology disruptions affecting emergency operations.

It applies to:

- All TJF offices.
- Field programmes.
- Staff.
- Volunteers.
- Consultants.
- Partner personnel working under TJF operations.

---

# 3. Preparedness Principles

Preparedness activities shall be guided by:

- Prevention where possible.
- Early action.
- Risk-informed planning.
- Coordination.
- Community participation.
- Continuous learning.

---

# 4. Preparedness Objectives

TJF seeks to:

- Detect emerging risks early.
- Maintain operational readiness.
- Ensure clear decision-making.
- Protect lives.
- Maintain essential services.
- Improve emergency coordination.

---

# 5. Hazard Identification

Potential hazards include:

## Natural Hazards

- Floods.
- Drought.
- Landslides.
- Earthquakes.
- Severe storms.
- Extreme temperatures.

---

## Biological Hazards

- Epidemics.
- Pandemics.
- Animal disease outbreaks.
- Waterborne diseases.

---

## Human-Caused Hazards

- Fire.
- Conflict.
- Civil unrest.
- Industrial accidents.
- Hazardous material incidents.

---

## Organizational Risks

- IT failures.
- Utility interruptions.
- Facility damage.
- Supply chain disruptions.

---

# 6. Risk Assessment Process

TJF shall assess:

```
Identify Hazard
        ↓
Determine Likelihood
        ↓
Assess Potential Impact
        ↓
Prioritize Risks
        ↓
Develop Preparedness Measures
        ↓
Monitor and Review
```

Risk assessments shall be reviewed at least annually and whenever significant operational changes occur.

---

# 7. Early Warning System

TJF shall monitor information from:

- National meteorological authorities.
- Disaster management agencies.
- Public health authorities.
- Government alerts.
- Humanitarian coordination platforms.
- Community leaders.
- Security advisories.

Alerts shall be categorized as:

| Alert Level | Meaning | Required Action |
|-------------|----------|-----------------|
| Green | Normal conditions | Routine monitoring |
| Yellow | Elevated risk | Increased preparedness |
| Orange | High probability | Activate preparedness measures |
| Red | Emergency | Activate response operations |

---

# 8. Emergency Preparedness Team

TJF shall establish an Emergency Preparedness Team consisting of:

### Executive Director
Overall strategic leadership.

### Emergency Response Coordinator
Operational command and coordination.

### Programme Lead
Programme continuity and field coordination.

### Logistics Lead
Transportation, warehousing and supplies.

### Finance Lead
Emergency financial support and expenditure tracking.

### Communications Lead
Internal and external communications.

### Safety and Security Officer
Personnel safety and security oversight.

---

# 9. Emergency Operations Readiness

Preparedness measures include:

- Maintaining emergency contact lists.
- Identifying evacuation routes.
- Maintaining emergency supplies.
- Updating response procedures.
- Confirming partner contact information.
- Reviewing communication systems.

---

# 10. Resource Preparedness

TJF shall maintain:

- Emergency supply inventories.
- First aid kits.
- Personal protective equipment.
- Communication equipment.
- Emergency transport arrangements.
- Backup power sources where feasible.
- Emergency procurement procedures.

---

# 11. Staff Preparedness

Staff shall receive training on:

- Emergency procedures.
- Fire safety.
- First aid awareness.
- Incident reporting.
- Evacuation procedures.
- Personal safety.
- Safeguarding during emergencies.

---

# 12. Simulation Exercises

Preparedness shall be strengthened through:

- Tabletop exercises.
- Emergency drills.
- Evacuation simulations.
- Communication tests.
- Multi-agency coordination exercises where appropriate.

Exercises should occur at least once each year, with lessons documented and incorporated into future planning.

---

# 13. Emergency Communication

During emergencies, communication shall be:

- Timely.
- Accurate.
- Consistent.
- Confidential where necessary.
- Accessible to affected stakeholders.

Primary communication channels may include:

- Telephone.
- SMS.
- Email.
- Radio communication.
- Approved messaging platforms.
- Official media statements.

---

# 14. Business Continuity Considerations

Preparedness shall support continuity of critical functions through:

- Backup records.
- Alternative work locations.
- Succession planning.
- ICT recovery arrangements.
- Essential supplier identification.

---

# 15. Community Preparedness

TJF shall promote community preparedness by supporting:

- Disaster awareness campaigns.
- Community emergency planning.
- Volunteer preparedness initiatives.
- Local risk mapping.
- Public education activities.

---

# 16. Coordination with External Partners

Preparedness activities shall be coordinated with:

- National disaster management authorities.
- Local governments.
- Health authorities.
- Emergency services.
- Humanitarian organizations.
- Community leaders.
- Development partners.

---

# 17. Monitoring and Readiness Assessment

Preparedness shall be evaluated through:

- Readiness checklists.
- Training completion rates.
- Equipment inspections.
- Drill performance.
- Risk assessment updates.
- Corrective action tracking.

---

- Updating response procedures.

# Conclusion

Preparedness is the foundation of effective humanitarian action.

By investing in planning, training, coordination and continuous improvement, The Jopetnah Foundation strengthens its ability to protect lives, maintain essential operations and respond quickly and responsibly when emergencies occur.
