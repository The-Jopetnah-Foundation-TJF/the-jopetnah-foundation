# Crisis Management System

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) recognizes that crises can arise unexpectedly and significantly affect its people, programmes, operations, reputation and strategic objectives.

This Crisis Management System establishes a structured approach for identifying, assessing, responding to and recovering from crises through coordinated leadership, effective communication and timely decision-making.

The System ensures that TJF remains resilient, minimizes disruption and continues serving communities during periods of uncertainty.

---

# 1. Purpose

This System aims to:

- Establish a coordinated crisis management structure.
- Protect life, assets and organizational reputation.
- Ensure timely and informed decision-making.
- Maintain operational continuity.
- Strengthen stakeholder confidence.
- Promote organizational learning after crises.

---

# 2. Scope

This System applies to crises affecting:

- Personnel.
- Beneficiaries.
- Facilities.
- Programmes.
- Information systems.
- Financial resources.
- Organizational reputation.
- Partnerships.

It applies to:

- Board Members.
- Executive Management.
- Staff.
- Volunteers.
- Consultants.
- Country and regional offices.
- Partner organizations where applicable.

---

# 3. Crisis Management Principles

TJF shall manage crises according to the following principles:

## Leadership

Strong leadership shall guide all crisis decisions.

---

## Accountability

Decisions shall be documented and justified.

---

## Timeliness

Responses shall be prompt and proportionate.

---

## Coordination

Departments shall work together using unified procedures.

---

## Transparency

Information shall be accurate, timely and appropriate.

---

## Continuous Improvement

Every crisis shall contribute to organizational learning.

---

# 4. Crisis Classification

## Level 1 – Minor Incident

Characteristics:

- Localized impact.
- Minimal operational disruption.
- Managed by departmental leadership.

Examples:

- Minor office fire.
- Short-term IT outage.
- Localized equipment failure.

---

## Level 2 – Major Incident

Characteristics:

- Significant operational impact.
- Cross-departmental coordination required.

Examples:

- Major security incident.
- Serious programme disruption.
- Disease outbreak affecting operations.
- Significant financial fraud.

---

## Level 3 – Organizational Crisis

Characteristics:

- Severe organizational impact.
- National or international implications.
- Executive and Board oversight required.

Examples:

- Major humanitarian emergency.
- Widespread cyberattack.
- Serious reputational crisis.
- Large-scale natural disaster affecting operations.

# 6. Crisis Management Team (CMT)

The CMT shall include:

- Executive Director (Chair).
- Deputy Executive Director (where applicable).
- Humanitarian Affairs Lead.
- Finance Director.
- Human Resources Director.
- ICT Manager.
- Communications Manager.
- Security and Safety Officer.
- Legal and Compliance Representative.
- Programme Director.
- Logistics Coordinator.

Subject matter experts may be invited depending on the nature of the crisis.

---

# 7. Responsibilities of the CMT

The Crisis Management Team shall:

- Assess the situation.
- Classify the crisis.
- Activate response procedures.
- Allocate resources.
- Coordinate internal and external communication.
- Monitor evolving risks.
- Approve recovery measures.
- Conduct post-crisis reviews.

# 9. Decision-Making Authority

## Board of Directors

Responsible for:

- Strategic oversight.
- Approval of extraordinary decisions.
- Oversight of high-impact crises.

---

## Executive Director

Responsible for:

- Declaring organizational crises.
- Activating the Crisis Management Team.
- Approving emergency resource mobilization.
- Representing TJF externally.

---

## Department Heads

Responsible for:

- Departmental response.
- Staff coordination.
- Reporting to the CMT.

---

# 10. Crisis Communication

Communication shall be:

- Accurate.
- Consistent.
- Timely.
- Respectful.
- Confidential where appropriate.

Communication audiences include:

- Staff.
- Beneficiaries.
- Donors.
- Government authorities.
- Partners.
- Media.
- The public.

Only authorized spokespersons may communicate officially on behalf of TJF.

---

# 11. Information Management

During crises, TJF shall maintain:

- Incident logs.
- Situation reports.
- Decision registers.
- Action trackers.
- Resource deployment records.
- Communication records.

---

# 12. Resource Mobilization

Emergency resources may include:

- Personnel.
- Emergency funding.
- Relief supplies.
- Transport.
- ICT support.
- External technical assistance.

Resource allocation shall follow emergency procurement and financial procedures.

---

# 13. Staff Welfare

TJF shall prioritize:

- Physical safety.
- Mental health and psychosocial support.
- Rest and rotation for emergency personnel.
- Family notification procedures where appropriate.
- Occupational health and safety.

---

# 14. Business Continuity Coordination

The Crisis Management Team shall coordinate with the Business Continuity Plan to:

- Maintain essential services.
- Protect critical information.
- Restore priority operations.
- Reduce operational downtime.

---

# 15. Recovery Planning

Recovery activities may include:

- Infrastructure restoration.
- Programme recovery.
- Financial reconciliation.
- Community recovery support.
- Staff support.
- Stakeholder engagement.

---

# 16. Post-Crisis Review

Following every major crisis, TJF shall conduct:

- After-action reviews.
- Lessons learned workshops.
- Root cause analysis.
- Policy updates.
- Improvement planning.

---

# 17. Training and Exercises

Crisis management capacity shall be strengthened through:

- Leadership simulations.
- Crisis communication exercises.
- Tabletop exercises.
- Multi-department drills.
- Annual Crisis Management Team training.

---
