# Community Knowledge Systems Framework

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) recognizes that communities are not only recipients of development interventions but also valuable sources of knowledge, experience, innovation and practical solutions.

Across generations, communities have developed unique approaches to agriculture, healthcare, environmental protection, conflict resolution, livelihoods and social organization. These knowledge systems provide important insights that can strengthen sustainable development.

This Community Knowledge Systems Framework establishes TJF’s approach to identifying, documenting, protecting and integrating community knowledge into research, programmes and policy initiatives while respecting ownership, cultural identity and ethical considerations.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Framework aims to:

- Recognize communities as knowledge partners.
- Preserve valuable local knowledge.
- Promote participatory research approaches.
- Integrate community insights into TJF programmes.
- Support local innovation and sustainable solutions.
- Strengthen community ownership of development processes.

---

# 2. Scope

This Framework applies to community knowledge related to:

- Agriculture and food systems.
- Environmental conservation.
- Traditional practices.
- Health and wellbeing.
- Livelihood strategies.
- Cultural heritage.
- Disaster resilience.
- Social organization.
- Local innovations.

It applies across:

- TJF programmes.
- Research activities.
- Innovation initiatives.
- Community partnerships.
- Policy engagement.

---

# 3. Vision

To establish TJF as an institution that values, protects and applies community knowledge to create inclusive and sustainable solutions.

---

# 4. Mission

To work alongside communities to capture, preserve and apply local knowledge while promoting dignity, participation and shared ownership of development outcomes.

---

# 5. Guiding Principles

Community knowledge initiatives shall follow:

## Respect

Recognizing the value of community experiences, traditions and expertise.

---

## Participation

Communities shall actively contribute to knowledge generation and decision-making.

---

## Consent

Knowledge sharing shall occur with appropriate informed consent.

---

## Inclusion

The voices of women, youth, older persons and marginalized groups shall be considered.

---

## Protection

Community knowledge shall be protected from misuse or exploitation.

---

## Mutual Benefit

Communities shall benefit from knowledge-based initiatives.

---

# 8. Community Knowledge Identification Process

TJF shall identify knowledge through:

- Community consultations.
- Participatory assessments.
- Focus group discussions.
- Interviews with knowledge holders.
- Community mapping.
- Field observations.
- Participatory research.

TJF shall promote:

```
Community Engagement
        ↓
Knowledge Identification
        ↓
Documentation
        ↓
Validation
        ↓
Application
        ↓
Feedback and Learning
```

# 10. Knowledge Documentation

Documentation may include:

- Case studies.
- Research reports.
- Videos.
- Audio recordings.
- Photographs.
- Digital archives.
- Community stories.
- Technical guides.

Documentation shall respect privacy and cultural sensitivities.

---

# 11. Community Knowledge Validation

Before applying community knowledge, TJF shall ensure:

- Accuracy verification.
- Community review.
- Technical assessment where necessary.
- Ethical approval where applicable.

Community members shall participate in validating documented knowledge.

# 14. Indigenous and Local Knowledge Protection

TJF shall promote protection through:

- Recognition of knowledge ownership.
- Ethical documentation.
- Confidentiality agreements where required.
- Appropriate attribution.
- Respect for cultural rights.

Community knowledge shall not be commercialized or transferred without appropriate consent.
