Biography

Mr Okello Carlos Torach is the Founder and Executive Director of The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF), a Uganda-based organisation committed to building stronger, healthier, more resilient communities through integrated, family-centred development.

His leadership is grounded in the conviction that every person deserves dignity, every child deserves an opportunity to thrive, every young person deserves a chance to fulfil their potential, and every family deserves a fair opportunity to build a secure and hopeful future.

He champions a holistic approach to community development that recognises the interconnected nature of human needs. Education, healthcare, sustainable livelihoods, environmental stewardship and community empowerment must work together to create the conditions in which individuals and families can move beyond vulnerability towards stability, opportunity, resilience and self-reliance.

As Founder and Executive Director, he provides strategic leadership and helps shape the Foundation’s vision, institutional development, partnerships and commitment to responsible community engagement. He advocates for practical, locally informed interventions that respond to genuine community priorities and respect the dignity of the people they serve.

Mr Torach also recognises that lasting impact requires more than good intentions. It demands integrity, sound governance, transparency, accountability, collaboration and responsible stewardship of the resources entrusted to an organisation. These principles are central to the institution he seeks to build for TJF.

“Our greatest achievement will not be measured by what we build as an organisation, but by the lives strengthened, the dignity preserved, and the opportunities created for generations to come.”

Mr Okello Carlos Torach, Founder & Executive Director

Why The Jopetnah Foundation was founded

When a hardship became a purpose.

Education was part of Mr Torach’s world long before TJF. Both his parents were teachers, and he grew up in a home where learning was valued and where knowledge was seen as something that opens doors. That inheritance gave meaning to the experience that followed.

Every mission has a beginning. For Mr Torach, it began with a personal experience of how quickly financial stability can be shaken. At one point in his professional life, a serious breach of trust by a manager severely disrupted his income and cash flow, and the effects reached well beyond his work.

The experience taught him a truth that professional experience cannot fully explain until one confronts it personally: when financial stability is disrupted, the consequences can reach far beyond money. They can interrupt education, unsettle family life, and put a person’s future at risk.

Read the rest of the story

If such a disruption could affect him, he began to see the wider struggles of many families, with limited reserves and little support to fall back on when hardship arrives. Behind every child who misses school there may be a household carrying an invisible burden. Behind every community facing persistent challenges there may be people whose potential is unrealised, not because they lack ability or ambition, but because the support they need is beyond their reach.

What began as a personal setback became a deeper conviction: families need more than sympathy when life becomes difficult. They need opportunities, practical support, resilience, and systems that protect their ability to build a better future.

From this conviction came The Jopetnah Foundation, built on the understanding that human needs are interconnected. A child’s education cannot always be separated from the economic stability of the family. A family’s wellbeing cannot be separated from access to healthcare, livelihood opportunities and a supportive community. Mr Torach therefore envisioned an organisation that looks beyond isolated problems to a holistic, family-centred approach, one that recognises the dignity of every person, the importance of every family and the potential within every community.

“I founded The Jopetnah Foundation because I learned how quickly financial hardship can disrupt lives, and how much difference practical support and opportunity can make. That experience deepened my understanding of the struggles other families face and strengthened my resolve to help build communities where people have greater opportunities to overcome hardship and realise their potential.”

Mr Okello Carlos Torach

A hardship became a purpose. A personal experience became a commitment. And that commitment became a vision for building whole communities, one life at a time.

Professional background

Mr Okello Carlos Torach is a finance and business professional based in Kampala, Uganda, whose career spans financial management, organisational administration, governance, compliance, inventory control, and executive leadership. He is the Managing Director of Jopetnah International Limited, where he provides strategic direction and oversees the company’s business operations and development.

His professional experience includes serving at Radio Maria Uganda Limited, where he progressed from Finance and Administrative Officer to Acting National Coordinator, with responsibilities encompassing financial stewardship, administration, governance, and compliance within a geographically dispersed non-profit organisation. Earlier, he served as Assistant Inventory Control Manager at Makss Packaging Industries Ltd, gaining practical experience in inventory management and operational controls.

Read more about his background

His professional perspective is complemented by academic training in economic policy management, business administration, accounting, and finance. Through professional development, international business exposure, cross-border engagements, and strategic partnerships, he has also developed a broader appreciation of international commerce, institutional collaboration, and the opportunities and complexities of working across diverse business environments.

This combination of financial expertise, operational experience, and business leadership informs his approach to organisational development, responsible management, and sustainable community impact through The Jopetnah Foundation.

Academic Qualifications: Master of Arts in Economic Policy Management, Makerere University; Bachelor of Business Administration (Accounting Option), Makerere University; and Diploma in Accounting and Finance, Makerere University Business School.

A message from the Founder

“A community is not truly thriving when a few are doing well while others are left behind. We build a better future when every life is valued, every family has hope, and every person has an opportunity to rise.”

There is something deeply moving about the realisation that, behind every struggling family, there is a story; behind every child who dreams, there is a future waiting to unfold; and behind every community facing hardship, there is a strength that deserves to be recognised, supported and nurtured.

It is this belief that inspires the heart of The Jopetnah Foundation. Our journey begins with a simple but powerful conviction: every life matters, every person deserves dignity, and no community should be left without hope.

Across our communities in Uganda, many families face challenges that are interconnected. A child’s education may be affected by a family’s financial circumstances. Poor health may undermine a household’s livelihood. Limited opportunities may prevent young people from realising their potential. Environmental challenges may threaten the very resources on which communities depend.

Read the full message

These realities remind us that meaningful development must go beyond temporary relief. It must address the circumstances that shape people’s lives, strengthen the foundations on which families depend, and create opportunities for lasting progress.

Our commitment is to support education and youth development, health and wellbeing, sustainable livelihoods, environmental resilience and community empowerment through an integrated, family-centred approach. When families are strengthened, children are better positioned to thrive; when young people are empowered, communities gain new possibilities; and when communities are equipped to manage challenges and opportunities, the foundations for a better future become stronger.

Yet at the heart of our work, we must never lose sight of the human being behind every need. A child is more than a statistic. A family is more than a beneficiary record. A community is more than a project location. Every person carries hopes, abilities, experiences and aspirations that deserve respect.

We want to listen before we act, learn from the people whose lives we seek to support, and work alongside communities rather than make decisions on their behalf. Compassion must be matched by responsibility. Good intentions alone are not enough. Lasting progress requires integrity, careful planning, accountable leadership, transparent use of resources, meaningful partnerships, and a willingness to learn and improve.

To our communities: your voices matter. Your experiences matter. You are not merely recipients of development; you are essential partners in shaping it.

To our prospective partners, institutions, volunteers and supporters: I invite you to join us in a shared commitment to human dignity and sustainable community development. We welcome partnerships built on mutual respect, shared responsibility, transparency and a genuine commitment to the people at the centre of our work.

To the children and young people whose futures we hope to help strengthen: where you begin in life should not determine the limits of what you can become. Your dreams deserve encouragement, your potential deserves opportunity, and your future deserves investment.

Building a trusted institution takes time. It requires patience, discipline, humility, and the courage to remain committed even when progress is gradual. We do not claim to have every answer, nor do we believe that one organisation can solve every challenge. What we can commit to is the determination to keep learning, to serve responsibly, to work with others, and to pursue practical solutions that place people at the centre of development.

The Jopetnah Foundation is founded on the belief that lasting change begins with people, grows through partnership, and endures when communities are empowered to shape their own future.

Let us build communities where hope is nurtured, dignity is protected, opportunity is expanded, and no one’s potential is needlessly lost because the support they needed was beyond their reach. Together, let us build whole communities, one life at a time.

With commitment and hope,
Mr Okello Carlos Torach
Founder & Executive Director, The Jopetnah Foundation

Guiding principles

Human dignity before everything

Every person deserves respect, compassion and the opportunity to live a meaningful life, regardless of their circumstances.

Communities as partners, not spectators

Sustainable change happens when communities help identify their priorities, shape solutions and take part in their own development.

Empowerment beyond temporary relief

We aspire to strengthen capacities, expand opportunities and help families build resilience and greater self-reliance.

Integrity in every responsibility

Compassion must be supported by transparent decisions, responsible stewardship, sound governance and accountability.

Partnership for lasting impact

Meaningful progress requires collaboration among communities, institutions, development partners, public bodies and individuals who share a commitment to positive change.
