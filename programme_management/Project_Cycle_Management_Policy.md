# Project Cycle Management Policy

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) recognizes that successful development interventions require structured planning, effective implementation, continuous monitoring and evidence-based learning.

This Project Cycle Management Policy establishes a standardized approach for managing all TJF projects throughout their lifecycle.

The Policy ensures that projects are strategically aligned, technically sound, financially responsible, community-driven and focused on measurable results.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Policy aims to:

- Establish a consistent project management approach.
- Improve project quality and effectiveness.
- Strengthen planning and accountability.
- Ensure efficient resource utilization.
- Promote learning and sustainability.
- Support successful project outcomes.

---

# 2. Scope

This Policy applies to all TJF projects including:

- Community development projects.
- Donor-funded projects.
- Partnership initiatives.
- Research projects.
- Emergency interventions.
- Capacity-building projects.
- Social innovation initiatives.

---

# 3. Project Cycle Management Principles

TJF projects shall follow these principles:

## Alignment

Projects must support:

- TJF mission.
- Strategic priorities.
- Community needs.
- Sustainable development goals.

---

## Participation

Communities and stakeholders should contribute throughout the project cycle.

---

## Evidence-Based Planning

Project decisions should be informed by:

- Research.
- Data.
- Assessments.
- Lessons learned.

---

## Accountability

Projects must demonstrate responsible use of resources and measurable results.

---

## Sustainability

Projects should create lasting benefits beyond implementation periods.

---

# 4. Project Lifecycle Overview

Every TJF project shall follow six major phases:

```
1. Identification
        ↓
2. Design and Planning
        ↓
3. Approval and Mobilization
        ↓
4. Implementation
        ↓
5. Monitoring and Evaluation
        ↓
6. Closure and Learning
```

---

# PHASE 1: Project Identification

## Purpose

To understand challenges, opportunities and community priorities.

---

## Activities

May include:

- Needs assessments.
- Community consultations.
- Research.
- Stakeholder analysis.
- Problem identification.

---

## Required Outputs

- Project concept note.
- Problem statement.
- Preliminary stakeholder analysis.
- Initial feasibility assessment.

---

# PHASE 2: Project Design and Planning

## Purpose

To develop a complete project model.

---

## Design Requirements

Each project should define:

### Project Goal

The intended long-term change.

---

### Objectives

Specific measurable achievements.

---

### Target Beneficiaries

Groups and communities expected to benefit.

---

### Activities

Actions required to achieve objectives.

---

### Timeline

Implementation schedule and milestones.

---

### Budget

Financial resources required.

---

### Monitoring Framework

Indicators and measurement methods.

---

### Risk Management Plan

Potential risks and mitigation strategies.

---

# Project Design Tools

TJF may use:

- Logical Framework Approach (Logframe).
- Theory of Change.
- Results Framework.
- Work Breakdown Structure.
- Implementation Schedule.

---

# PHASE 3: Approval and Mobilization

## Approval Review Areas

Projects shall be reviewed for:

- Strategic relevance.
- Technical feasibility.
- Financial viability.
- Risk considerations.
- Sustainability potential.

---

## Mobilization Activities

After approval:

- Assign project team.
- Confirm budget.
- Establish partnerships.
- Develop implementation plan.
- Prepare resources.

---

# PHASE 4: Project Implementation

## Implementation Requirements

Projects must have:

- Approved work plan.
- Assigned responsibilities.
- Budget controls.
- Procurement arrangements.
- Reporting schedules.

---

## Project Management Areas

### Scope Management

Ensuring activities remain aligned with objectives.

---

### Time Management

Tracking:

- Milestones.
- Deadlines.
- Delays.

---

### Cost Management

Monitoring:

- Budget utilization.
- Expenditure.
- Financial controls.

---

### Quality Management

Ensuring:

- Standards are maintained.
- Outputs meet requirements.

---

# PHASE 5: Monitoring and Evaluation

## Monitoring

Monitoring tracks:

- Activities completed.
- Resources used.
- Outputs produced.
- Challenges encountered.

---

## Evaluation

Evaluation assesses:

- Relevance.
- Effectiveness.
- Efficiency.
- Impact.
- Sustainability.

---

## Monitoring Tools

May include:

- Progress reports.
- Field visits.
- Surveys.
- Data dashboards.
- Community feedback.

---

# PHASE 6: Project Closure and Learning

## Closure Activities

Projects should include:

- Final reporting.
- Financial reconciliation.
- Asset management.
- Documentation.
- Stakeholder communication.

---

## Lessons Learned

Projects should capture:

- Success factors.
- Challenges.
- Innovations.
- Recommendations.

---

# 5. Project Governance Structure

## Project Sponsor

Usually:

- Executive Director.
- Authorized senior leader.

Responsibilities:

- Strategic oversight.
- Major approvals.
- Resource support.

---

## Project Manager

Responsibilities:

- Daily project coordination.
- Team management.
- Progress reporting.
- Risk management.

---

## Project Team

Responsibilities:

- Activity implementation.
- Documentation.
- Technical delivery.

---

## Finance Team

Responsibilities:

- Financial monitoring.
- Budget compliance.
- Reporting.

---

## MEAL Team

Responsibilities:

- Results measurement.
- Data verification.
- Learning.

---

# 6. Project Documentation Requirements

Each project file should contain:

- Concept note.
- Proposal.
- Budget.
- Agreements.
- Work plans.
- Reports.
- Monitoring data.
- Evaluation reports.
- Lessons learned.

---

# 7. Project Risk Management

Common risks include:

| Risk Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Operational | Delays, resource limitations |
| Financial | Budget overruns |
| Security | Field safety concerns |
| Programme | Low community participation |
| Partnership | Coordination challenges |
| Environmental | Climate-related impacts |

---

# 8. Project Change Management

Changes affecting:

- Scope.
- Budget.
- Timeline.
- Objectives.

must be documented and approved.

---

# 9. Project Performance Indicators

| Indicator | Measurement |
|---|---|
| Projects completed successfully | Percentage |
| Activities delivered on schedule | Percentage |
| Budget compliance | Percentage |
| Beneficiary satisfaction | Survey |
| Outcomes achieved | Percentage |
| Lessons documented | Number |

---

# 10. Policy Compliance

Failure to follow project cycle requirements may result in:

- Corrective action.
- Management review.
- Additional oversight requirements.

---

# 11. Review

This Policy shall be reviewed:

- Every three years.
- Following major project evaluations.
- When programme management standards change.

---

# Conclusion

Effective project cycle management enables TJF to transform ideas into successful interventions that generate measurable and sustainable impact.

Through structured planning, accountable implementation and continuous learning, The Jopetnah Foundation strengthens its ability to deliver meaningful development outcomes.

> **Successful projects are built through disciplined planning, community partnership, responsible management and continuous improvement.**
