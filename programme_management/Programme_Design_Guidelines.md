# Programme Design Guidelines

## Executive Summary

Effective programme design is fundamental to achieving sustainable social impact.

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) uses evidence-based programme design approaches to ensure that interventions respond to genuine community needs, address root causes, maximize available resources and create measurable improvements.

These Guidelines provide a structured approach for developing programmes from initial concept through approval and implementation readiness.

---

# 1. Purpose

These Guidelines aim to:

- Establish consistent programme design standards.
- Improve programme relevance and effectiveness.
- Strengthen planning quality.
- Promote evidence-based interventions.
- Support successful implementation.
- Improve sustainability of programme outcomes.

---

# 2. Scope

These Guidelines apply to:

- New programmes.
- Programme expansions.
- Pilot initiatives.
- Community interventions.
- Donor-funded projects.
- Partnership programmes.

---

# 3. Programme Design Principles

TJF programme design shall follow:

## Need-Based Approach

Programmes must respond to clearly identified community challenges.

---

## Evidence-Based Decision Making

Design decisions should be supported by:

- Research.
- Data.
- Assessments.
- Community feedback.

---

## Community Participation

Communities should contribute to:

- Problem identification.
- Solution design.
- Implementation planning.
- Evaluation.

---

## Results Orientation

Programmes must define:

- Expected changes.
- Measurable indicators.
- Impact pathways.

---

## Sustainability Focus

Programme designs should consider:

- Local ownership.
- Capacity building.
- Long-term continuation.

---

# 4. Programme Design Process

TJF programme design follows eight stages:

```
1. Situation Analysis
          ↓
2. Stakeholder Analysis
          ↓
3. Problem Identification
          ↓
4. Theory of Change Development
          ↓
5. Programme Planning
          ↓
6. Monitoring Framework Design
          ↓
7. Budget Development
          ↓
8. Programme Approval
```

---

# 5. Situation Analysis

Before designing a programme, teams should understand:

- Community context.
- Existing challenges.
- Available resources.
- Previous interventions.
- Stakeholder environment.

---

## Assessment Methods

May include:

- Surveys.
- Interviews.
- Focus group discussions.
- Community meetings.
- Secondary research.
- Field observations.

---

# 6. Problem Analysis

Programme teams should identify:

## Core Problem

The main challenge requiring intervention.

---

## Root Causes

Underlying factors contributing to the problem.

---

## Effects

Consequences affecting communities.

---

## Problem Tree Analysis

TJF may use:

```
Effects
   |
Core Problem
   |
Root Causes
```

to understand intervention opportunities.

---

# 7. Stakeholder Analysis

Stakeholders may include:

- Communities.
- Government institutions.
- Local leaders.
- Donors.
- NGOs.
- Private sector.
- Academic institutions.

Assessment should consider:

- Influence.
- Interest.
- Role.
- Expectations.

---

# 8. Theory of Change

Every major programme should define:

## Desired Impact

The long-term change TJF seeks to contribute to.

---

## Outcomes

Changes expected among beneficiaries.

---

## Outputs

Direct results produced by activities.

---

## Activities

Actions undertaken.

---

## Inputs

Resources required.

---

Example:

```
Resources
    ↓
Activities
    ↓
Outputs
    ↓
Outcomes
    ↓
Impact
```

---

# 9. Programme Objectives

Objectives should be:

- Specific.
- Measurable.
- Achievable.
- Relevant.
- Time-bound.

(SMART Objectives)

---

# 10. Target Beneficiary Identification

Programme designs must define:

- Primary beneficiaries.
- Secondary beneficiaries.
- Selection criteria.
- Geographic coverage.
- Vulnerability considerations.

---

# 11. Programme Strategy Development

The programme strategy should explain:

- What will be done.
- Why it will work.
- Who will participate.
- How success will be measured.

---

# 13. Monitoring and Evaluation Design

Every programme should establish:

## Indicators

Measures of progress and success.

---

## Data Sources

Examples:

- Surveys.
- Reports.
- Registers.
- Assessments.

---

## Measurement Frequency

Define:

- Monthly.
- Quarterly.
- Annual.

---

# 14. Budget Design

Programme budgets should include:

## Direct Costs

Examples:

- Programme activities.
- Materials.
- Field operations.

---

## Personnel Costs

Examples:

- Staff.
- Consultants.
- Technical experts.

---

## Operational Costs

Examples:

- Transport.
- Communication.
- Administration.

---

Programme designs should identify:

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| | | |

Possible risks:

- Funding gaps.
- Security issues.
- Low participation.
- Environmental challenges.
- Operational delays.

# 16. Sustainability Planning

Designs should address:

## Institutional Sustainability

Building local capacity.

---

## Financial Sustainability

Identifying future resources.

---

## Community Sustainability

Encouraging ownership.

---

## Environmental Sustainability

Reducing negative impacts.

---

# 17. Programme Innovation

TJF encourages:

- Digital solutions.
- New partnership models.
- Community-led approaches.
- Evidence-driven improvements.

---

# 18. Programme Design Review Checklist

Before approval, confirm:

☐ Need is clearly identified.  
☐ Beneficiaries are defined.  
☐ Objectives are measurable.  
☐ Activities are realistic.  
☐ Budget is appropriate.  
☐ Risks are assessed.  
☐ Monitoring framework exists.  
☐ Sustainability plan is included.
