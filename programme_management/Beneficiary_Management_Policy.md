# Beneficiary Management Policy

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) places communities and individuals at the centre of its development work.

Effective beneficiary management ensures that TJF resources reach intended participants in a fair, transparent, inclusive and accountable manner.

This Beneficiary Management Policy establishes the procedures and principles for identifying, selecting, registering, supporting, monitoring and protecting beneficiaries across all TJF programmes.

The Policy promotes dignity, fairness, data protection, accountability and responsible stewardship of resources.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Policy aims to:

- Establish consistent beneficiary management practices.
- Ensure fair and transparent beneficiary selection.
- Protect beneficiary rights and dignity.
- Strengthen programme accountability.
- Improve service delivery.
- Support accurate impact measurement.

---

# 2. Scope

This Policy applies to:

- All TJF programmes.
- Community projects.
- Grant-funded activities.
- Emergency interventions.
- Training programmes.
- Support initiatives.

It applies to:

- Programme staff.
- Field officers.
- Volunteers.
- Partners.
- Data collection teams.

---

# 3. Beneficiary Management Principles

TJF beneficiary management shall follow:

---

## Fairness

Beneficiaries shall be selected based on transparent and objective criteria.

---

## Inclusion

Programmes shall consider participation of:

- Vulnerable groups.
- Persons with disabilities.
- Women and girls.
- Youth.
- Marginalized communities.

---

## Dignity and Respect

Beneficiaries shall be treated with:

- Respect.
- Confidentiality.
- Compassion.
- Professionalism.

---

## Transparency

Communities should understand:

- Programme objectives.
- Eligibility requirements.
- Selection processes.
- Available support.

---

## Accountability

TJF shall maintain systems to:

- Track services delivered.
- Receive feedback.
- Address concerns.

---

# 4. Beneficiary Categories

TJF beneficiaries may include:

## Direct Beneficiaries

Individuals or groups directly receiving programme support.

Examples:

- Training participants.
- Healthcare recipients.
- Students.
- Entrepreneurs.

---

## Indirect Beneficiaries

Individuals benefiting from broader programme outcomes.

Examples:

- Families.
- Communities.
- Local institutions.

---

# 5. Beneficiary Identification Process

Beneficiary identification should consider:

- Programme objectives.
- Community needs.
- Vulnerability factors.
- Geographic location.
- Eligibility requirements.

Methods may include:

- Community consultations.
- Assessments.
- Registration exercises.
- Partner referrals.

---

# 6. Beneficiary Selection Criteria

Selection criteria should be:

- Relevant.
- Objective.
- Documented.
- Communicated clearly.

Possible criteria include:

- Level of need.
- Programme eligibility.
- Community priorities.
- Availability of support.

---

# 7. Beneficiary Registration

Where required, TJF shall maintain beneficiary records containing:

- Identification information.
- Programme participation details.
- Support received.
- Monitoring information.

Registration systems must comply with data protection requirements.

---

# 8. Beneficiary Data Management

Beneficiary information must be:

## Accurate

Information should be verified where appropriate.

---

## Secure

Data must be protected from unauthorized access.

---

## Limited

Only necessary information should be collected.

---

## Confidential

Personal information should only be shared for authorized purposes.

---

# 9. Beneficiary Communication

TJF shall communicate clearly regarding:

- Programme purpose.
- Available services.
- Selection processes.
- Rights and responsibilities.
- Feedback channels.

Communication should consider:

- Language.
- Accessibility.
- Cultural context.

---

# 10. Beneficiary Participation

Beneficiaries should participate in:

- Programme planning.
- Feedback activities.
- Monitoring processes.
- Evaluation exercises.

Their experiences should inform programme improvement.

---

# 11. Beneficiary Feedback and Complaints

TJF shall provide accessible mechanisms for beneficiaries to:

- Ask questions.
- Provide suggestions.
- Report concerns.
- Submit complaints.

Feedback channels may include:

- Community meetings.
- Contact points.
- Suggestion systems.
- Digital platforms.

---

# 12. Beneficiary Protection

TJF shall ensure beneficiaries are protected from:

- Exploitation.
- Discrimination.
- Abuse.
- Harassment.
- Harm.

All programmes must follow safeguarding requirements.

---

# 13. Beneficiary Monitoring

Monitoring may track:

- Participation.
- Services received.
- Progress.
- Outcomes.
- Satisfaction.

Tools may include:

- Surveys.
- Interviews.
- Attendance records.
- Field monitoring visits.

---

# 14. Beneficiary Exit and Transition

Where programmes end, TJF should consider:

- Communication with beneficiaries.
- Transition planning.
- Sustainability measures.
- Referral pathways where necessary.

---

# 15. Partner Responsibilities

Partners managing beneficiaries on behalf of TJF must:

- Follow beneficiary standards.
- Protect beneficiary information.
- Maintain accurate records.
- Report concerns.

---

# 16. Roles and Responsibilities

## Executive Director

Responsible for:

- Ensuring accountability standards.
- Approving major beneficiary management approaches.

---

## Programmes Department

Responsible for:

- Beneficiary planning.
- Selection processes.
- Service coordination.

---

## MEAL Department

Responsible for:

- Data verification.
- Impact measurement.
- Feedback analysis.

---

## Field Teams

Responsible for:

- Registration.
- Community communication.
- Beneficiary support.

---

## IT Department

Responsible for:

- Supporting secure beneficiary information systems.

---

---

# 18. Compliance

Non-compliance with beneficiary management standards may result in:

- Corrective action.
- Additional monitoring.
- Partner review.
- Staff disciplinary measures.

---

# 19. Review

This Policy shall be reviewed:

- Every three years.
- Following programme evaluations.
- Following significant safeguarding or data incidents.

---

# Conclusion

Responsible beneficiary management ensures that TJF programmes reach the right people, provide meaningful support and protect the dignity and rights of every participant.

By placing beneficiaries at the centre of decision-making, The Jopetnah Foundation strengthens trust, accountability and sustainable impact.

---

> **Every beneficiary represents a person, a story and a future that deserves respect, opportunity and dignity.**
