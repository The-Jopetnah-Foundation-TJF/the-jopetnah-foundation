# Programme Management Framework

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) implements programmes designed to create sustainable improvements in communities through evidence-based interventions, strategic partnerships and accountable delivery.

This Programme Management Framework establishes the principles, structures, processes and standards that guide the identification, design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of TJF programmes.

The Framework ensures that all TJF programmes are strategically aligned, efficiently managed, community-responsive and focused on measurable impact.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Framework aims to:

- Establish consistent programme management practices.
- Improve programme quality and effectiveness.
- Strengthen accountability and transparency.
- Ensure efficient use of resources.
- Promote evidence-based decision-making.
- Support sustainable community impact.

---

# 2. Scope

This Framework applies to:

- All TJF programmes.
- Projects and initiatives.
- Grants-funded activities.
- Community interventions.
- Partnership programmes.
- Emergency response activities.

It applies to:

- Programme staff.
- Field teams.
- Project managers.
- Partners.
- Consultants.
- Volunteers.

---

# 3. Programme Management Philosophy

TJF believes that sustainable impact requires:

- Understanding community needs.
- Designing appropriate solutions.
- Engaging stakeholders.
- Measuring results.
- Learning continuously.
- Scaling effective approaches.

---

# 4. Programme Management Principles

## 4.1 Strategic Alignment

All programmes must contribute to TJF's:

- Vision.
- Mission.
- Strategic objectives.
- Development priorities.

---

## 4.2 Community Ownership

Communities should participate in:

- Needs identification.
- Programme design.
- Implementation.
- Monitoring.
- Evaluation.

---

## 4.3 Results-Based Management

Programmes shall focus on:

- Inputs.
- Activities.
- Outputs.
- Outcomes.
- Long-term impact.

---

## 4.4 Accountability

TJF shall remain accountable to:

- Communities.
- Donors.
- Partners.
- Government authorities.
- Stakeholders.

---

## 4.5 Inclusion

Programmes shall consider:

- Gender.
- Age.
- Disability.
- Vulnerability.
- Social equity.

---

## 4.6 Sustainability

Programme designs should consider:

- Local ownership.
- Capacity building.
- Long-term benefits.
- Environmental responsibility.

# 7. Programme Development Cycle

TJF programmes follow six stages:

---

# Stage 1: Identification

Activities:

- Community assessments.
- Research.
- Stakeholder consultation.
- Problem analysis.

Outputs:

- Needs assessment report.
- Programme concept.

---

# Stage 2: Design

Activities:

- Define objectives.
- Develop intervention strategy.
- Create budget.
- Establish indicators.

Outputs:

- Programme proposal.
- Logical framework.
- Implementation plan.

---

# Stage 3: Approval

Review areas:

- Strategic alignment.
- Technical feasibility.
- Budget availability.
- Risk assessment.

Approval authority depends on programme size and complexity.

---

# Stage 4: Implementation

Implementation requires:

- Work plans.
- Assigned responsibilities.
- Resource management.
- Monitoring systems.

---

# Stage 5: Monitoring and Evaluation

Monitoring tracks:

- Progress.
- Performance.
- Resource use.
- Challenges.

Evaluation examines:

- Effectiveness.
- Impact.
- Sustainability.

---

# Stage 6: Learning and Scaling

Lessons are used to:

- Improve programmes.
- Replicate successful approaches.
- Inform strategy.

---
