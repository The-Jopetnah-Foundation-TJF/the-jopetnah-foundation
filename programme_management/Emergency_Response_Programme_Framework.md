# Emergency Response Programme Framework

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) recognizes that communities may face emergencies caused by natural disasters, conflicts, disease outbreaks, displacement, climate-related events and other humanitarian crises.

This Emergency Response Programme Framework establishes TJF’s approach to preparing for, responding to and recovering from emergencies while ensuring that affected populations receive timely, dignified and accountable support.

The Framework enables TJF to coordinate emergency interventions, mobilize resources, protect vulnerable groups and strengthen community resilience.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Framework aims to:

- Establish TJF’s emergency response approach.
- Enable rapid humanitarian action.
- Protect affected communities.
- Coordinate emergency operations.
- Ensure accountable resource utilization.
- Support recovery and resilience.

---

# 2. Scope

This Framework applies to:

- Humanitarian emergencies.
- Disaster response activities.
- Crisis interventions.
- Community recovery programmes.
- Emergency partnerships.

It guides:

- TJF leadership.
- Programme teams.
- Field personnel.
- Volunteers.
- Partners.

---

# 3. Emergency Response Principles

TJF emergency response shall follow:

---

## Humanity

Providing assistance based on human need and dignity.

---

## Neutrality

Supporting affected populations without discrimination.

---

## Impartiality

Prioritizing assistance based on vulnerability and need.

---

## Accountability

Ensuring responsible use of resources and transparency.

---

## Do No Harm

Avoiding actions that increase risks or vulnerabilities.

---

# 4. Types of Emergencies

TJF may respond to:

## Natural Disasters

Including:

- Floods.
- Droughts.
- Storms.
- Earthquakes.
- Environmental disasters.

---

## Health Emergencies

Including:

- Disease outbreaks.
- Public health crises.

---

## Humanitarian Crises

Including:

- Displacement.
- Conflict-related impacts.
- Food insecurity.

---

## Socioeconomic Emergencies

Including:

- Severe livelihood disruption.
- Community crises.

---

# 5. Emergency Response Cycle

TJF follows four stages:

```
1. Preparedness
        ↓
2. Emergency Response
        ↓
3. Recovery
        ↓
4. Resilience Building
```

---

# Stage 1: Preparedness

Preparedness activities include:

- Emergency planning.
- Risk assessments.
- Staff training.
- Partner coordination.
- Resource planning.
- Emergency supplies preparation.

---

# Stage 2: Emergency Response

Response activities may include:

- Rapid assessments.
- Emergency assistance.
- Community support.
- Relief distribution.
- Protection services.
- Coordination with authorities.

---

# Stage 3: Recovery

Recovery focuses on:

- Restoring livelihoods.
- Rebuilding community capacity.
- Supporting affected services.
- Strengthening local systems.

---

# Stage 4: Resilience Building

Long-term actions may include:

- Disaster preparedness.
- Climate adaptation.
- Community training.
- Sustainable solutions.

---

# 6. Emergency Activation Process

Emergency response may be activated when:

- A significant crisis occurs.
- Communities face urgent needs.
- Government or partners request support.
- TJF leadership determines intervention is required.

---

# 7. Emergency Assessment

Before intervention, TJF should assess:

## Situation

- What happened?
- Where?
- When?
- Who is affected?

---

## Needs

Assess:

- Food.
- Shelter.
- Healthcare.
- Protection.
- Water and sanitation.
- Livelihood needs.

---

## Risks

Consider:

- Safety.
- Access challenges.
- Environmental risks.
- Protection concerns.

---

## Finance Department

## Communications Team

Responsibilities:

- Public communication.
- Donor updates.

# 9. Emergency Programme Design

Emergency programmes should define:

- Crisis context.
- Target population.
- Response objectives.
- Activities.
- Budget.
- Timeline.
- Monitoring approach.
- Exit strategy.

---

# 10. Community Participation During Emergencies

Even during crises, TJF shall promote:

- Community consultation.
- Transparent communication.
- Feedback collection.
- Local participation.

---

# 11. Protection and Safeguarding

Emergency responses must protect:

- Children.
- Women.
- Persons with disabilities.
- Older persons.
- Vulnerable groups.

All emergency activities must follow safeguarding standards.

---

# 12. Resource Mobilization

Emergency resources may come from:

- Donors.
- Partners.
- Government collaboration.
- Corporate support.
- Public fundraising.

All resources must be managed transparently.

---

# 13. Emergency Logistics

Emergency operations may require:

- Procurement.
- Transportation.
- Warehousing.
- Distribution systems.
- Supplier coordination.

---

# 14. Monitoring and Reporting

Emergency programmes should track:

- People reached.
- Services delivered.
- Resources used.
- Challenges.
- Outcomes.

Reports may include:

- Situation reports.
- Donor reports.
- Completion reports.

---

# 15. Communication During Emergencies

Emergency communication should be:

- Accurate.
- Timely.
- Responsible.
- Respectful.

Communications must protect:

- Privacy.
- Dignity.
- Safety of affected people.

---

# 16. Emergency Exit Strategy

TJF should plan transition through:

- Handover arrangements.
- Local capacity strengthening.
- Recovery programmes.
- Community ownership.

---

# 18. Review and Learning

After emergency responses, TJF shall conduct:

- After-action reviews.
- Lessons learned sessions.
- Performance assessments.
- Improvement planning.

---

# 19. Compliance

Emergency activities must comply with:

- TJF policies.
- Applicable laws.
- Partner requirements.
- Humanitarian principles.

---

# Conclusion

Emergencies require compassion, coordination and decisive action.

Through preparedness, responsible response and community-centered recovery, The Jopetnah Foundation strengthens its ability to protect lives, support vulnerable communities and build resilience.
