# Monitoring Policy

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) recognizes monitoring as a critical management function that enables the organization to track progress, measure performance, identify challenges and improve programme effectiveness.

This Monitoring Policy establishes TJF’s standards for systematic data collection, analysis, reporting and utilization of information to support evidence-based decision-making and accountability.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Policy aims to:

- Establish a consistent monitoring approach across TJF programmes.
- Track progress against objectives and indicators.
- Identify implementation challenges early.
- Improve programme quality and effectiveness.
- Strengthen accountability to stakeholders.

---

# 2. Scope

This Policy applies to:

- All TJF programmes.
- Projects.
- Field activities.
- Partnerships.
- Emergency interventions.
- Research initiatives.

It applies to:

- Management.
- Programme teams.
- MEAL personnel.
- Field staff.
- Partners.

---

# 3. Definition of Monitoring

Monitoring is the continuous and systematic collection, analysis and use of information to assess whether programmes are progressing as planned.

Monitoring focuses on:

- Activities completed.
- Resources utilized.
- Outputs achieved.
- Outcomes emerging.
- Risks and challenges.

---

# 4. Monitoring Principles

TJF monitoring shall follow:

---

## Relevance

Data collected must support programme decisions.

---

## Accuracy

Information must be reliable and verified.

---

## Timeliness

Monitoring information should be available when needed.

---

## Participation

Stakeholders should contribute where appropriate.

---

## Learning Orientation

Monitoring should identify improvements, not only measure performance.

---

# 5. Monitoring Framework

Each TJF programme should establish:

## Objectives

What the programme intends to achieve.

---

## Indicators

How progress will be measured.

---

## Targets

Expected achievements within a specific period.

---

## Data Sources

Where information will come from.

---

## Collection Methods

How data will be gathered.

---

## Reporting Schedule

When information will be reviewed.

---

# 6. Types of Monitoring

## Input Monitoring

Tracks resources invested:

Examples:

- Funding.
- Equipment.
- Staff.
- Materials.

---

## Activity Monitoring

Tracks implementation:

Examples:

- Trainings conducted.
- Communities visited.
- Services delivered.

---

## Output Monitoring

Measures immediate results:

Examples:

- Number of beneficiaries reached.
- Number of services provided.

---

## Outcome Monitoring

Measures changes resulting from programmes:

Examples:

- Improved skills.
- Improved access.
- Behaviour changes.

---

## Compliance Monitoring

Ensures adherence to:

- TJF policies.
- Donor requirements.
- Legal standards.

---

# 7. Monitoring Methods

TJF may use:

## Field Visits

To verify activities and assess implementation quality.

---

## Surveys

To collect beneficiary information and feedback.

---

## Interviews and Discussions

To understand experiences and challenges.

---

## Observation

To confirm programme activities and conditions.

---

## Document Review

To verify records and reports.

---

## Digital Data Collection

Using approved electronic tools.

---

# 8. Monitoring Frequency

Monitoring frequency depends on:

- Programme size.
- Risk level.
- Donor requirements.
- Implementation complexity.

Possible schedules include:

- Weekly monitoring.
- Monthly reviews.
- Quarterly assessments.
- Annual programme reviews.

---

All programmes should maintain an indicator tracking system containing:

| Indicator | Target | Current Status | Data Source | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Indicators should measure:

- Progress.
- Quality.
- Reach.
- Outcomes.
- Impact.

# 10. Field Monitoring Procedures

Field monitoring should include:

## Preparation

- Review programme plans.
- Identify monitoring objectives.
- Prepare tools.

---

## Field Assessment

Review:

- Activities completed.
- Beneficiary participation.
- Resource use.
- Challenges.

---

## Reporting

Document:

- Findings.
- Recommendations.
- Corrective actions.

---

# 11. Data Quality Assurance

TJF shall ensure:

## Completeness

Required information is collected.

---

## Accuracy

Information reflects actual conditions.

---

## Consistency

Data follows approved standards.

---

## Verification

Information is checked before reporting.

---

# 12. Monitoring Reports

Monitoring reports may include:

## Monthly Reports

Tracking:

- Activities.
- Progress.
- Challenges.

---

## Quarterly Reports

Reviewing:

- Performance trends.
- Indicator achievements.
- Risks.

---

## Annual Reports

Assessing:

- Overall programme contribution.
- Lessons learned.
- Strategic outcomes.

# 13. Use of Monitoring Information

Monitoring findings should support:

- Management decisions.
- Programme adjustments.
- Resource allocation.
- Risk management.
- Strategic planning.

# 15. Monitoring Tools

TJF may use:

- Monitoring checklists.
- Activity reports.
- Attendance registers.
- Surveys.
- Dashboards.

# 16. Monitoring Challenges Management

Where challenges are identified, TJF shall:

1. Document the issue.
2. Analyse causes.
3. Develop corrective actions.
4. Assign responsibility.
5. Follow up implementation.
