# Impact Measurement Framework

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) exists to create meaningful and sustainable transformation in communities.

Impact measurement enables TJF to understand whether its programmes contribute to long-term improvements beyond immediate activities and outputs.

This Impact Measurement Framework establishes the principles, systems and approaches used by TJF to measure social, economic, environmental and institutional change resulting from its work.

---

# 1. Purpose

This Framework aims to:

- Measure TJF’s long-term contribution to change.
- Understand programme effectiveness.
- Demonstrate impact to stakeholders.
- Improve strategic decision-making.
- Strengthen accountability.

---

# 2. Scope

This Framework applies to:

- All TJF programmes.
- Development initiatives.
- Emergency interventions.
- Partnerships.
- Strategic projects.

It guides:

- Programme teams.
- MEAL Department.
- Leadership.
- Partners.

---

# 3. Definition of Impact

Impact refers to the significant and lasting changes that occur because of TJF interventions.

Impact may include:

- Improved livelihoods.
- Increased access to services.
- Stronger communities.
- Improved resilience.
- Sustainable environmental outcomes.

---

# 4. Impact Measurement Principles

TJF impact measurement follows:

---

## Long-Term Perspective

Impact is assessed beyond immediate programme activities.

---

## Evidence-Based Assessment

Impact claims must be supported by reliable evidence.

---

## Community-Centered Measurement

Communities contribute to defining meaningful change.

---

## Transparency

Findings should be documented and communicated responsibly.

---

## Learning and Improvement

Impact information should guide future programmes.

---

# 5. TJF Impact Measurement Model

TJF applies the results chain:

```
INPUTS
(Resources invested)

        ↓

ACTIVITIES
(What TJF does)

        ↓

OUTPUTS
(Immediate deliverables)

        ↓

OUTCOMES
(Changes achieved)

        ↓

IMPACT
(Long-term transformation)
```

# 7. Impact Measurement Approaches

TJF may use:

---

## Quantitative Measurement

Examples:

- Number of people reached.
- Percentage changes.
- Income improvements.
- Service access rates.

---

## Qualitative Measurement

Examples:

- Personal stories.
- Community experiences.
- Behaviour changes.
- Case studies.

---

## Participatory Impact Assessment

Including:

- Community discussions.
- Beneficiary feedback.
- Stakeholder assessments.

---

## Comparative Analysis

Comparing:

- Baseline conditions.
- Programme outcomes.
- Long-term changes.

---

# 9. Impact Assessment Process

## Stage 1: Define Intended Impact

Identify:

- Desired changes.
- Target groups.
- Expected contribution.

---

## Stage 2: Develop Indicators

Define:

- Measurement criteria.
- Data sources.
- Targets.

---

## Stage 3: Collect Evidence

Gather information through:

- Surveys.
- Evaluations.
- Research.
- Community feedback.

---

## Stage 4: Analyse Results

Assess:

- Changes achieved.
- Contributing factors.
- Challenges.

---

## Stage 5: Apply Learning

Use findings to:

- Improve programmes.
- Inform strategy.
- Strengthen partnerships.

---
