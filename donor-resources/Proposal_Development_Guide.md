# Proposal Development Guide

## Purpose

This guide establishes the standard process for developing funding proposals, concept notes, expressions of interest, and partnership submissions on behalf of The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF).

It aims to ensure that all proposals are strategic, evidence-based, compliant with donor requirements, and aligned with the Foundation's mission and strategic priorities.

---

# Guiding Principles

Proposal development at TJF shall be:

- Mission-aligned
- Community-informed
- Evidence-based
- Collaborative
- Financially realistic
- Results-oriented
- Compliant with donor requirements
- Subject to internal quality assurance

# Stage 1 – Opportunity Identification

Funding opportunities may be identified through:

- Donor websites
- Government calls
- Foundation announcements
- Development partner networks
- Corporate CSR programmes
- Research institutions
- Professional associations
- Existing partners

Every opportunity should be logged in the Foundation's funding pipeline.

---

# Stage 2 – Eligibility Assessment

Before investing time in proposal development, confirm:

- Mission alignment
- Geographic eligibility
- Programme eligibility
- Organization eligibility
- Funding amount
- Timeline
- Compliance requirements
- Reporting obligations
- Co-funding requirements

---

# Stage 3 – Go / No-Go Decision

The Executive Director (or delegated authority) should decide whether to proceed based on:

- Strategic fit
- Organizational capacity
- Available resources
- Competitive positioning
- Partnership opportunities
- Risk profile

Reasons for declining should be documented for institutional learning.

---

# Stage 4 – Proposal Team

Depending on the opportunity, the proposal team may include:

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|----------------|
| Proposal Lead | Coordinates the proposal process |
| Programme Lead | Technical content |
| Finance Officer | Budget preparation |
| MEL Officer | Results framework and indicators |
| Partnerships Officer | Donor liaison |
| Executive Director | Final approval |

Additional specialists may be included where appropriate.

---

# Stage 5 – Proposal Design

The proposal should clearly define:

- Problem statement
- Target beneficiaries
- Geographic coverage
- Objectives
- Expected outcomes
- Activities
- Timeline
- Risks
- Sustainability approach
- Monitoring framework

Design should be informed by community needs and available evidence.

---

# Stage 6 – Budget Development

Budgets should be:

- Realistic
- Transparent
- Cost-effective
- Fully justified
- Consistent with donor rules

The Finance team should review all budgets before submission.

---

# Stage 7 – Quality Assurance

Before submission, verify:

- All donor instructions followed
- Required documents attached
- Budget matches narrative
- Figures are accurate
- Acronyms explained
- Formatting compliant
- References checked
- Internal approvals obtained

A quality assurance checklist should be completed for every proposal.

---

# Stage 8 – Executive Approval

The Executive Director (or delegated authority) should confirm that:

- The proposal aligns with the Foundation's mission.
- The Foundation has capacity to deliver.
- Risks are understood.
- Financial commitments are acceptable.
- Submission is authorized.

No proposal should be submitted without formal approval.

---

# Stage 9 – Submission

Maintain a submission record including:

- Donor name
- Opportunity title
- Submission date
- Contact person
- Requested funding
- Proposal version
- Supporting documents
- Submission confirmation

This information should be retained for future reference.

---

# Stage 10 – Post-Submission

Following submission:

- Monitor donor communications.
- Respond promptly to clarification requests.
- Record outcomes.
- Update the funding pipeline.

If unsuccessful, request feedback where possible.

---

# Lessons Learned

Following each proposal, conduct a brief review covering:

- What worked well?
- What challenges arose?
- Were timelines realistic?
- Was the budget accurate?
- What improvements are needed?

Lessons should be documented and shared internally.

---

# Proposal Quality Standards

Every proposal should demonstrate:

- Clear alignment with donor priorities
- Strong evidence of need
- Measurable objectives
- Logical implementation plan
- Sound financial planning
- Risk awareness
- Sustainability
- Community participation
- Institutional capacity

---

# Ethical Standards

Proposal development shall be conducted with integrity.

The Foundation will not:

- Misrepresent organizational capacity
- Exaggerate impact
- Fabricate data
- Duplicate funding requests without disclosure
- Submit plagiarized content

All submissions should accurately represent the Foundation's experience, partnerships, and capabilities.

---

# Document Management

Maintain secure records of:

- Submitted proposals
- Supporting documents
- Budgets
- Donor correspondence
- Review comments
- Award decisions

Version control should be applied throughout the proposal development process.

---

# Review

This guide shall be reviewed annually and updated to reflect changes in donor expectations, organizational processes, and lessons learned.

---

> **Every proposal represents the Foundation's reputation. A disciplined proposal development process strengthens credibility, improves quality, and increases the likelihood of securing partnerships that create lasting impact.**
