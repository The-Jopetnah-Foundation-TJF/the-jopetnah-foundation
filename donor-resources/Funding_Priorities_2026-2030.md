# The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF)

# Funding Priorities 2026–2030

> **Building whole communities, one life at a time.**

## Executive Summary

The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF) is committed to creating sustainable, inclusive and measurable social impact through integrated community development.

TJF recognises that the families and communities it serves rarely experience challenges in isolation. Education, health, livelihoods and environmental conditions are interconnected. Accordingly, TJF seeks to mobilize and deploy resources through an integrated model that addresses priority needs while strengthening the systems, capabilities and resilience required for lasting impact.

This **Funding Priorities 2026–2030** document establishes the Foundation's strategic direction for resource mobilization during the period. It identifies the programme areas, institutional capabilities, populations, funding approaches and partnership categories that should receive priority consideration when TJF develops funding proposals, cultivates donors or evaluates prospective opportunities.

The document is intended to guide:

* Donor and funder engagement
* Grant prospecting and opportunity qualification
* Programme and concept development
* Partnership development
* Resource allocation
* Institutional strengthening
* Funding diversification
* Annual fundraising planning
* Strategic decisions on whether prospective opportunities align with TJF's mission and direction

This document is **strategic rather than operational**. It defines where TJF seeks to mobilize resources and why. Specific funding opportunities, deadlines, eligibility requirements and application activities are managed through the Foundation's **`GRANTS_CALENDAR.md`** and **`FUNDING_PIPELINE.md`**.

---

# 1. Strategic Resource Mobilization Goal

TJF seeks to mobilize sustainable, diversified and mission-aligned financial, technical and partnership resources to expand its capacity to deliver high-quality programmes, strengthen communities and institutions, and generate measurable and lasting development outcomes.

Resource mobilization should therefore pursue not only the **volume of funding**, but also its:

* Strategic alignment
* Predictability
* Flexibility
* Sustainability
* Additionality
* Quality of partnership
* Potential for measurable impact
* Contribution to institutional resilience

TJF will seek to build a funding portfolio that supports both **programme delivery and organizational sustainability**, while maintaining sound governance, independence, accountability and responsible stewardship of resources.

---

# 2. Purpose of This Framework

This framework answers the strategic question:

> **What funding should TJF pursue, and why?**

It provides a common reference for determining whether a prospective funding opportunity is consistent with TJF's strategic direction.

It should be used to:

1. Prioritize resource-mobilization efforts.
2. Guide development of programme concepts and funding proposals.
3. Identify appropriate donor and partner categories.
4. Strengthen funding diversification.
5. Protect TJF from mission drift.
6. Support responsible allocation of fundraising effort.
7. Guide institutional investment and capacity development.
8. Provide donors and partners with a transparent view of TJF's funding priorities.

---

# 3. TJF's Funding Philosophy

TJF believes that effective development investment should strengthen people, households, communities and institutions simultaneously.

The Foundation therefore favours funding approaches that:

* Address clearly identified community needs.
* Strengthen local capacity and ownership.
* Produce measurable outcomes.
* Promote inclusion and equitable access.
* Build resilience rather than dependency.
* Encourage innovation where it adds practical value.
* Strengthen institutional systems alongside programme delivery.
* Support evidence, learning and continuous improvement.
* Encourage collaboration rather than unnecessary duplication.
* Create pathways toward sustainability and scale.

TJF will seek partnerships in which financial resources are combined, where appropriate, with technical expertise, knowledge, technology, networks, infrastructure, mentorship and other forms of support.

---

# 4. Strategic Investment Principles

Funding pursued or accepted by TJF should contribute to one or more of the following principles.

### 4.1 Community Ownership

Programmes should encourage meaningful participation and ownership by the communities they are intended to benefit.

### 4.2 Sustainability

Investment should, where feasible, strengthen long-term capacity, resilience and locally sustainable solutions.

### 4.3 Inclusion and Equity

TJF seeks to reduce barriers affecting vulnerable, underserved and excluded populations.

### 4.4 Evidence and Learning

Programme decisions should increasingly be informed by credible evidence, monitoring, evaluation, learning and community feedback.

### 4.5 Accountability and Transparency

TJF is committed to responsible stewardship, clear reporting, appropriate controls and measurable results.

### 4.6 Innovation and Practical Technology

Technology and innovation should be pursued where they provide practical solutions, improve access, reduce costs, strengthen systems or increase impact.

### 4.7 Partnership

Complex development challenges are best addressed through collaboration among communities, civil society, governments, businesses, academia, development partners and other stakeholders.

### 4.8 Safeguarding and Do-No-Harm

TJF's resource mobilization and programme activities should uphold safeguarding, ethical conduct, dignity, inclusion and responsible engagement with communities.

### 4.9 Mission Alignment

Funding should strengthen TJF's mission rather than create unnecessary mission drift or dependence on activities unrelated to its strategic purpose.

# 5. TJF's Integrated Development Model

TJF's core funding priorities are built around four interconnected programme pillars:

1. **Education**
2. **Health**
3. **Sustainable Livelihoods**
4. **Environmental Sustainability and Climate Resilience**

These pillars are supported by two institutional and enabling priorities:

5. **Governance and Institutional Strengthening**
6. **Innovation, Research and Digital Transformation**

The Foundation's approach recognises that progress in one area can reinforce progress in another.

For example:

* Education can improve employability and health awareness.
* Health can improve school attendance and household productivity.
* Livelihoods can improve household capacity to invest in education and health.
* Environmental resilience can protect livelihoods and community wellbeing.
* Strong institutions improve the quality and sustainability of all programmes.
* Research, data and technology can improve decision-making across every pillar.

Accordingly, TJF encourages **integrated programme concepts** where appropriate rather than treating development challenges as isolated issues.

# 14. Geographic Focus

## Primary Geographic Focus

TJF's principal geographic focus is **Uganda**, where the Foundation seeks to build locally relevant, sustainable and community-driven development initiatives.

## Regional and International Opportunities

TJF may participate in regional or international initiatives where:

* The opportunity is consistent with TJF's mission.
* Appropriate partnerships exist.
* TJF has the required capacity.
* The geographic arrangement is permitted by the funder.
* Participation creates meaningful strategic value.
* Governance, compliance and implementation responsibilities are clearly defined.

# 15. Preferred Funding Approaches

TJF will seek a diversified funding portfolio comprising different forms of financial, technical and partnership support.

## 15.1 Core and Institutional Funding

Flexible funding that strengthens organizational systems, people, governance, infrastructure and operational resilience is a strategic priority.

Core support can enable TJF to maintain the systems necessary to implement restricted programmes effectively and responsibly.

## 15.2 Multi-Year Programme Funding

TJF will prioritize multi-year funding where possible because longer funding horizons can improve programme continuity, planning, learning, sustainability and measurable impact.

## 15.3 Restricted Programme Grants

Restricted grants aligned with TJF's programme pillars remain an important component of the funding portfolio.

## 15.4 Catalytic and Innovation Funding

Smaller or catalytic grants can be strategically valuable where they enable TJF to test, demonstrate or scale promising approaches.

## 15.5 Research and Evidence Funding

Funding for research, monitoring, evaluation, learning and evidence generation should strengthen TJF's ability to understand and demonstrate impact.

## 15.6 Technical Assistance and Capacity Building

TJF welcomes support that strengthens institutional systems, programme capabilities, leadership, technology, financial management, safeguarding, MEL and other organizational capacities.

## 15.7 Corporate and In-Kind Partnerships

Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR), technology partnerships, professional expertise, equipment, educational resources, health-related support and other in-kind contributions may complement financial funding.

## 15.8 Consortium and Partnership Funding

TJF may participate in consortia, sub-granting arrangements and strategic partnerships where responsibilities, resources, governance and value to communities are clearly defined.

# 17. Funding Portfolio Diversification

TJF will seek to avoid excessive dependence on any single:

* Donor
* Funding mechanism
* Geographic source
* Programme pillar
* Corporate partner
* Government programme
* Short-term funding cycle

Diversification is intended to strengthen institutional resilience and reduce the risk that changes in one funding relationship disproportionately affect programme continuity or organizational stability.

Portfolio targets may be established through annual resource-mobilization plans and approved organizational budgets.

# 19. Funding Opportunity Qualification Criteria

Before committing significant resources to a funding opportunity, TJF should assess:

### Strategic Fit

* Does the opportunity align with TJF's mission?
* Does it support one or more funding priorities?
* Does it reinforce TJF's integrated development model?

### Community Value

* Is there a clearly identifiable community need?
* Are intended beneficiaries consistent with TJF's priority populations?
* Is the proposed intervention likely to generate meaningful outcomes?

### Eligibility

* Is TJF eligible?
* Are geographic and legal requirements satisfied?
* Are partnership or consortium requirements manageable?

### Institutional Readiness

* Does TJF have the personnel, systems and technical capability required?
* Can the programme be implemented responsibly?
* Are financial and reporting requirements manageable?

### Financial and Strategic Value

* Is the funding proportionate to the effort and compliance burden?
* Does the opportunity provide meaningful strategic value?
* Is there potential for longer-term partnership, scale or learning?

### Risk and Integrity

* Are the donor and funding source appropriate?
* Are contractual requirements acceptable?
* Are safeguarding, ethical, reputational and compliance risks manageable?
* Could accepting the funding compromise TJF's independence or mission?

Opportunities that fail fundamental mission, eligibility, integrity or feasibility requirements should not be pursued merely because funding is available.

---

# 20. Funding Opportunities TJF Should Generally Avoid

TJF should generally avoid opportunities that:

* Fall substantially outside its mission.
* Require activities for which TJF lacks the necessary capability.
* Create significant mission drift.
* Have unreasonable reporting or compliance requirements relative to funding value.
* Require commitments TJF cannot realistically meet.
* Present unacceptable legal, ethical, safeguarding or reputational risks.
* Are geographically or legally inaccessible.
* Provide insufficient time for responsible proposal development.
* Require unsupported claims or misleading representations.
* Create unsustainable programme obligations without an appropriate continuation strategy.
* Depend on arrangements that are unclear, unverifiable or potentially fraudulent.

A decline decision is not a failure. Protecting organizational integrity and strategic focus is itself a resource-mobilization discipline.

# 22. Strategic Funding Horizons: 2026–2030

## 2026–2027 — Consolidation and Readiness

Priority should be given to:

* Strengthening institutional systems
* Improving funding readiness
* Building a diversified donor prospect base
* Developing strong programme concepts
* Establishing reliable MEL and evidence systems
* Strengthening governance and compliance
* Building strategic donor and partner relationships

## 2028–2029 — Expansion and Strategic Partnership

Subject to demonstrated capacity and results, TJF should seek to:

* Expand successful programmes
* Develop larger and multi-year partnerships
* Increase participation in strategic consortia
* Strengthen regional collaboration where appropriate
* Increase institutional and programme funding diversity
* Develop stronger evidence of outcomes and impact

## 2030 — Sustainability, Scale and Institutional Maturity

By the end of the strategic period, TJF should seek to strengthen:

* Long-term funding sustainability
* Donor retention
* Institutional resilience
* Programme evidence
* Replication and scale of successful approaches
* Strategic partnerships
* Local ownership
* Knowledge and learning systems

These horizons represent strategic direction rather than fixed commitments. Annual planning should determine the specific activities and targets appropriate to TJF's capacity and operating environment.

# 23. Measuring Funding Strategy Success

Progress against this framework should be monitored using a balanced set of financial, relationship, institutional and impact indicators.

### Resource Mobilization

* Total funds mobilized
* Value of multi-year funding
* Value of unrestricted/core support
* Value of programme funding
* Funding pipeline value
* Funding conversion rate

### Portfolio Health

* Number of active funding relationships
* Donor retention rate
* Funding concentration
* Funding diversification by donor category
* Funding diversification by programme pillar
* Proportion of multi-year funding

### Partnership Development

* Number of strategic partnerships
* Number of consortium opportunities
* Technical assistance partnerships
* Corporate partnerships
* Research collaborations

### Institutional Capacity

* Proposal readiness
* Compliance performance
* Reporting timeliness
* MEL capacity
* Safeguarding capacity
* Financial management improvements

### Programme Contribution

* Communities reached
* Beneficiaries served
* Programme outcomes achieved
* Evidence generated
* Lessons incorporated into programme design

Funding volume should not be treated as the sole measure of success. **Quality, sustainability, strategic alignment and community impact are equally important.**
