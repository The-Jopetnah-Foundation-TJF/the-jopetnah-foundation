# TJF Funding Pipeline

> **The Jopetnah Foundation (TJF)**
> **Building whole communities, one life at a time.**

The **TJF Funding Pipeline** is the Foundation's central operational framework for managing prospective, active and completed funding relationships from initial identification through qualification, cultivation, concept development, proposal submission, due diligence, award, implementation handover, reporting, renewal and institutional learning.

It works alongside `GRANTS_CALENDAR.md` and other TJF donor-resource documents to provide a complete resource-mobilisation management system.

---

# 1. Purpose

The Funding Pipeline exists to ensure that TJF does not merely identify funding opportunities, but manages them systematically.

It is intended to help TJF:

* convert donor intelligence into actionable opportunities;
* qualify prospects before committing significant resources;
* prioritise opportunities according to strategic value and probability of success;
* manage donor relationships deliberately and professionally;
* track concepts and proposals through defined stages;
* assign clear ownership and accountability;
* prevent missed deadlines and uncompleted actions;
* coordinate programme, finance, leadership and partnership inputs;
* maintain a reliable record of donor communications;
* manage due-diligence and compliance requirements;
* track funding decisions;
* prepare successful awards for implementation handover;
* manage reporting and renewal opportunities;
* capture lessons from both successful and unsuccessful applications; and
* build long-term, diversified and sustainable funding relationships.

> **Core principle:** Every active prospect should have a clear status, responsible owner, next action and next action date.

# 3. TJF Funding Lifecycle

All significant funding prospects should move through a controlled lifecycle.

```text
IDENTIFY
   ↓
SCREEN
   ↓
QUALIFY
   ↓
PRIORITISE
   ↓
CULTIVATE
   ↓
ENGAGE
   ↓
CONCEPT
   ↓
GO / NO-GO
   ↓
PROPOSAL
   ↓
INTERNAL REVIEW
   ↓
SUBMIT
   ↓
FUNDER REVIEW
   ↓
DUE DILIGENCE
   ↓
AWARD / DECLINE
   ↓
CONTRACT / AGREEMENT
   ↓
IMPLEMENTATION HANDOVER
   ↓
REPORTING
   ↓
RENEWAL / SCALE / CLOSE
   ↓
LESSONS & DONOR STEWARDSHIP
```

Not every funding route will pass through every stage.

For example:

* a **referral-based donor** may move from Cultivate → Engage → Invitation → Concept;
* an **open grant call** may move rapidly from Screen → Qualify → Proposal;
* an **in-kind corporate partnership** may move from Engage → Concept → Partnership Agreement;
* a **consortium opportunity** may require Partner Identification before Concept;
* a **scholarship programme** may be managed primarily as a partnership/referral relationship.

# 4. Pipeline Stage Definitions

## Stage 0 — Identified

A potential donor, programme, partnership or opportunity has been identified but has not yet undergone basic screening.

### Required information

* donor/funder name;
* programme name, if known;
* source;
* official website;
* date identified;
* initial relevance;
* person who identified it.

### Exit criteria

Move to **Screening** when enough information exists to assess basic relevance.

---

# 5. Stage 1 — Screening

The opportunity receives a rapid initial assessment.

### Screening questions

* Is Uganda eligible?
* Is TJF or an appropriate TJF partner eligible?
* Does the opportunity relate to one or more TJF pillars?
* Is the funding route genuine and verifiable?
* Is the opportunity currently active, upcoming or relationship-based?
* Is the potential funding material enough to justify further research?
* Are there obvious exclusions?

### Outcome

One of:

* Proceed to Qualification
* Monitor
* Partner Required
* Not Eligible
* Archive

---

# 6. Stage 2 — Qualified

The opportunity has passed basic eligibility and strategic screening.

A qualified prospect should have:

* confirmed or reasonably established eligibility;
* identified funding route;
* geographic fit;
* programme fit;
* indicative funding level;
* deadline/cycle;
* application mechanism;
* preliminary TJF readiness assessment;
* preliminary strategic-fit score;
* identified owner.

### Exit criteria

A prospect becomes **Qualified** only when there is sufficient evidence to justify investing further staff time.

---

# 7. Stage 3 — Prioritised

Qualified prospects are ranked according to strategic importance.

### Priority categories

| Priority           | Definition                                    | Management                         |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **P1 — High**      | Strong fit and realistic pursuit              | Active management                  |
| **P2 — Strategic** | Good fit but requires cultivation/preparation | Planned management                 |
| **P3 — Watch**     | Potentially relevant but uncertain            | Monitor                            |
| **Archive**        | No current strategic value                    | Retain for historical intelligence |

---

# 8. Stage 4 — Cultivation

Cultivation applies primarily to relationship-based fundraising.

The objective is to develop trust and understanding before a formal funding request.

### Cultivation activities may include

* researching donor priorities;
* mapping mutual connections;
* securing introductions;
* attending relevant events;
* participating in donor briefings;
* sharing appropriate organisational information;
* requesting an introductory conversation;
* understanding the donor's current strategy;
* identifying areas of mutual interest;
* identifying possible technical or consortium partners.

### Important principle

> **Do not force a proposal before a relationship or funding mechanism is ready.**

A donor may remain in cultivation for months or years before an appropriate funding opportunity emerges.

---

# 9. Stage 5 — Engagement

TJF has established substantive communication with the funder or relevant partner.

Examples include:

* introductory meeting;
* donor briefing;
* expression of interest;
* invitation to submit;
* programme discussion;
* partnership meeting;
* consortium discussion;
* request for further information.

### Required tracking

Record:

* date;
* participants;
* communication channel;
* key points;
* donor interests;
* questions raised;
* commitments made;
* follow-up action;
* responsible person;
* next contact date.

---

# 10. Stage 6 — Concept Development

TJF develops a concise project concept aligned to:

* donor priorities;
* TJF's mission;
* relevant programme pillar(s);
* beneficiary needs;
* measurable outcomes;
* implementation capacity;
* sustainability;
* safeguarding;
* partnership arrangements.

### Typical concept package

* problem statement;
* context;
* target population;
* geographic area;
* proposed intervention;
* objectives;
* expected outcomes;
* preliminary indicators;
* implementation approach;
* partnership structure;
* sustainability;
* indicative budget;
* organisational capacity.

---

# 11. Stage 7 — Go / No-Go Decision

Before significant proposal-development resources are committed, an explicit decision should be recorded.

## GO

Proceed when:

* eligibility is confirmed;
* strategic fit is strong;
* the funding route is realistic;
* TJF has or can secure required capacity;
* the deadline is achievable;
* partnerships are adequate;
* the opportunity justifies the effort.

## NO-GO

Do not proceed where:

* TJF is ineligible;
* the geography is excluded;
* the funding route is unrealistic;
* necessary partners cannot be secured;
* the programme is materially outside TJF's strategy;
* the deadline cannot support a quality submission;
* compliance requirements cannot responsibly be met; or
* expected value does not justify the resources required.

### Required record

Every significant No-Go decision should include a short reason.

This prevents repeated research and enables institutional learning.

---

# 12. Stage 8 — Proposal Development

Once a Go decision is approved, the opportunity becomes an active proposal.

### Proposal workstreams

| Workstream                        | Lead                      |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Programme design                  | Programme Lead            |
| Technical narrative               | Technical/Programme Lead  |
| Budget                            | Finance                   |
| Monitoring, Evaluation & Learning | MEL / Programme           |
| Safeguarding                      | Safeguarding Lead         |
| Partnerships                      | Partnership Lead          |
| Organisational information        | Administration            |
| Compliance                        | Leadership / Compliance   |
| Final approval                    | Authorised TJF leadership |

One person should remain the **Proposal Owner**, even when several people contribute.

---

# 14. Stage 9 — Internal Quality Review

No major proposal should be submitted immediately after drafting.

A structured internal review should assess:

### Strategic quality

* Does the proposal advance TJF's mission?
* Does it fit TJF's programme pillars?
* Is the donor alignment credible?

### Technical quality

* Is the intervention technically sound?
* Is the theory of change logical?
* Are outputs and outcomes measurable?

### Financial quality

* Is the budget accurate?
* Are costs defensible?
* Is the budget consistent with the narrative?

### Risk quality

* Are implementation risks addressed?
* Are safeguarding risks addressed?
* Are financial and compliance risks addressed?

### Presentation quality

* Is the document clear?
* Is the language professional?
* Are instructions followed?
* Are all required attachments included?

# 15. Stage 10 — Submitted

Once submitted, record:

* submission date;
* submission time where relevant;
* submission portal;
* application/reference number;
* final requested amount;
* final project title;
* submitted version;
* responsible officer;
* expected decision date;
* funder contact;
* next follow-up date.

### Important

The submitted version should be preserved.

Do not overwrite the final submitted proposal with later drafts.

# 16. Stage 11 — Funder Review

During funder review, maintain an active communication record.

Track:

* acknowledgement;
* clarification requests;
* interview requests;
* due-diligence requests;
* revised budgets;
* revised concepts;
* additional documents;
* expected decision date;
* funder communications.

No prospect should remain in "Submitted" indefinitely without a recorded review date.

# 17. Stage 12 — Due Diligence

Due diligence may include:

* legal registration;
* governance;
* financial records;
* bank verification;
* organisational policies;
* safeguarding;
* anti-fraud controls;
* procurement;
* conflict of interest;
* references;
* programme evidence;
* monitoring systems;
* site visits;
* key-person interviews;
* partner verification.

### TJF principle

> **Due diligence is part of donor confidence-building, not merely an administrative burden.**

TJF should maintain a reusable organisational due-diligence package to reduce repeated requests.

# 18. Stage 13 — Award

An award is not considered final until the appropriate agreement or formal confirmation is received.

Record:

* donor;
* programme;
* award title;
* award amount;
* currency;
* award start date;
* award end date;
* payment schedule;
* reporting requirements;
* restricted/unrestricted status;
* approved budget;

* key contacts;
* agreement reference;
* compliance requirements.

---

# 19. Stage 14 — Contract / Grant Agreement

Before implementation begins:

* review the agreement;
* confirm permitted activities;
* confirm budget restrictions;
* identify reporting deadlines;
* identify procurement requirements;
* identify branding requirements;
* identify safeguarding obligations;
* identify audit requirements;
* identify amendment procedures;
* identify donor approval requirements;
* identify data/reporting requirements;
* identify grant close-out requirements.

The agreement should be reviewed by the appropriate authorised TJF personnel.

---

# 20. Stage 15 — Implementation Handover

The fundraising team should formally hand over an awarded project to implementation.

### Handover package

* signed agreement;
* approved proposal;
* approved budget;
* results framework;
* implementation schedule;
* donor conditions;
* reporting calendar;
* procurement requirements;
* safeguarding requirements;
* communications/branding requirements;
* donor contact information;
* risk register;
* monitoring framework.

### Handover principle

> **The proposal is a promise. Implementation is the delivery of that promise.**

Fundraising and programme teams should therefore remain connected after award.

* progress against indicators;

* lessons learned;
* challenges;

### Financial reporting

### Compliance

* procurement;

---

# 23. Stage 18 — Renewal / Scale / Close

Before a grant closes, determine whether the relationship should:

### Renew

Continue substantially the same intervention.

### Scale

Expand geographical or beneficiary coverage.

### Adapt

Modify the programme based on learning.

### Diversify

Seek additional donors for the same programme.

### Graduate

Move from donor-funded implementation toward greater sustainability.

### Close

Complete obligations and formally close the relationship/project.

---

# 24. Stage 19 — Donor Stewardship

Donor stewardship continues after submission and after award.

It may include:

* timely reporting;
* impact updates;
* appropriate appreciation;
* sharing verified results;
* invitations to relevant programme events;
* site visits;
* learning exchanges;
* programme updates;
* strategic conversations;
* renewal discussions.

Stewardship should never become inappropriate solicitation or excessive communication.

---

# 25. Stage 20 — Institutional Learning

Every significant funding process should produce learning.

After a decision, record:

### If successful

* what worked;
* strongest proposal elements;
* donor feedback;
* partnership strengths;
* implementation commitments;
* renewal potential.

### If unsuccessful

* reason for decline, if provided;
* eligibility issue;
* competitiveness;
* programme fit;
* budget concerns;
* donor portfolio limitations;
* timing;
* partnership weakness;
* proposal-quality issue;
* lessons for future applications.

A declined proposal should not automatically be deleted.

It may contain reusable research, narrative, budget structures and donor intelligence.

# 29. Probability of Success

Probability estimates should be conservative and evidence-based.

| Rating        | Meaning                                              |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Very Low**  | Significant eligibility or strategic barriers        |
| **Low**       | Eligible but highly competitive or weak relationship |
| **Moderate**  | Good fit and credible route                          |
| **High**      | Strong fit, relationship and readiness               |
| **Very High** | Invitation/renewal/strong established relationship   |

Probability should never be represented as a guarantee.

# 30. Relationship Ownership

Where appropriate, distinguish:

### Relationship Owner

Responsible for donor communication and relationship development.

### Proposal Owner

Responsible for getting the application completed and submitted.

### Programme Lead

Responsible for technical programme content.

### Finance Lead

Responsible for budget and financial compliance.

### Senior Approver

Responsible for final institutional authorisation.

One person may hold several roles in a small organisation, but the responsibilities should still be explicit.

# 31. Next-Action Discipline

Every active pipeline record must have:

**Current State → Next Action → Owner → Date**

For example:

> **Current State:** GEF-SGP future-cycle prospect
> **Next Action:** Develop environmental livelihoods concept
> **Owner:** Programme Lead
> **Date:** 2026-11-15

Avoid vague actions such as:

* "Follow up"
* "Look into"
* "Monitor"
* "Consider"

Instead use:

* "Verify eligibility against the next Uganda call."
* "Email prospective research partner."
* "Prepare two-page concept note."
* "Request introduction to donor contact."
* "Review current application guidance."
* "Complete organisational due-diligence pack."

# 32. Deadline Management

Deadlines should be classified as:

| Category              | Definition                               |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **Critical**          | 0–7 days                                 |
| **Urgent**            | 8–14 days                                |
| **Near-term**         | 15–30 days                               |
| **Preparation**       | 31–90 days                               |
| **Future**            | More than 90 days                        |
| **No fixed deadline** | Genuine rolling/relationship opportunity |

TJF should ideally begin substantive proposal preparation **well before** the formal deadline.

A deadline is not a proposal-start date.

---

# 33. Proposal Effort Classification

Not every opportunity deserves the same level of effort.

| Level             | Typical Effort                                                  |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Light**         | Simple expression of interest / short form                      |
| **Moderate**      | Concept note and basic budget                                   |
| **Major**         | Full proposal, detailed budget and supporting documents         |
| **Institutional** | Major multi-partner, multi-year or highly compliant application |

This allows leadership to allocate staff time realistically.

| --------------------------- | ------------ | ------------ | ------------------------ |

# 36. Funding Concentration Risk

The pipeline should be managed alongside TJF's overall funding portfolio.

Leadership should monitor:

* excessive dependence on one donor;
* excessive dependence on one funding mechanism;
* excessive dependence on one programme pillar;
* excessive dependence on short-term grants;
* excessive dependence on unrestricted or restricted income;
* geographic concentration;
* partnership concentration.

The goal is not merely to maximise funding.

> **The goal is to build a resilient and strategically diversified funding base.**

# 41. Declined Proposal Protocol

A declined proposal should trigger a short review.

### Review within 30 days where practical

Ask:

1. Was eligibility correctly assessed?
2. Was donor fit strong?
3. Was the problem clearly evidenced?
4. Was the intervention compelling?
5. Was the budget competitive?
6. Was the partnership structure adequate?
7. Was the proposal technically strong?
8. Did the donor provide feedback?
9. Was the donor simply oversubscribed?
10. Is resubmission or relationship development appropriate?

Record the lesson.

Do not automatically classify the donor as "bad" because a proposal was unsuccessful.

---

# 42. Successful Award Protocol

Within an appropriate period after award:

1. confirm agreement;
2. confirm funds/payment schedule;
3. establish project code;
4. establish project bank/accounting arrangements where required;
5. appoint implementation team;
6. establish monitoring framework;
7. establish reporting calendar;
8. establish safeguarding arrangements;
9. establish procurement plan;
10. establish donor communication plan;
11. conduct implementation handover;
12. update funding portfolio.

# 43. Grant Close-Out Protocol

At the end of a funded project:

* final narrative report submitted;
* final financial report submitted;
* outstanding funds reconciled;
* assets accounted for;
* audit requirements completed;
* donor obligations closed;
* documentation archived;
* lessons captured;
* impact evidence retained;
* donor thanked appropriately;
* renewal opportunity assessed;
* future relationship strategy recorded.

# 49. Ethical Fundraising Standards

TJF's funding pipeline should be managed according to high standards of integrity.

TJF should:

* provide accurate information;
* never fabricate beneficiaries, results or organisational experience;
* never misrepresent partnerships;
* never inflate impact statistics;
* respect donor restrictions;
* disclose material conflicts of interest;
* protect beneficiary privacy;
* obtain appropriate consent for photographs and stories;
* respect safeguarding obligations;
* avoid inappropriate influence;
* reject fraudulent funding schemes;
* verify unusual payment requests;
* maintain appropriate financial controls; and
* preserve the dignity of communities and beneficiaries.

> **TJF should never compromise its mission, integrity or beneficiary protection merely to secure funding.**

# 50. Funding Fraud and Scam Controls

Before responding to a funding opportunity, confirm:

* the funder's official identity;
* official website;
* official email domain;
* official application portal;
* published eligibility requirements;
* published funding mechanism;
* credible programme history.

Treat requests for:

* upfront "processing fees";
* personal bank transfers;
* cryptocurrency payments;
* unofficial registration fees;
* suspicious identity documents;
* passwords or account credentials;

as potential fraud indicators.

When uncertain, independently verify the opportunity through the funder's official communication channels.

# 51. Confidentiality and Data Protection

The pipeline may contain sensitive information.

TJF should avoid storing unnecessary:

* personal identification numbers;
* passwords;
* bank credentials;
* confidential beneficiary information;
* sensitive donor correspondence;
* private contact information beyond legitimate business requirements.

Where sensitive documents are required, store them in the appropriate controlled repository rather than in a publicly accessible GitHub file.

The public repository should contain **process information and non-sensitive funding intelligence**, not confidential due-diligence material.

# 55. Pipeline Entry Quality Standard

An opportunity should not be considered **Active** unless it has:

* a named funder;
* a defined opportunity or relationship;
* a documented funding route;
* a relevant TJF pillar;
* an identified stage;
* a priority;
* an owner;
* a next action;
* a next action date; and
* a verification date where external information is involved.

# 56. Definition of an "Active Prospect"

An active prospect is one for which TJF has a reasonable and documented basis for taking action.

A prospect is **not active** merely because:

* its website exists;
* TJF likes the funder;
* the organisation has funded similar work in the past;
* an old call once existed; or
* someone mentioned it informally.

This distinction protects staff time and improves pipeline quality.

---

# 57. Definition of a "Qualified Prospect"

A qualified prospect should meet most of the following:

* relevant geography;
* relevant programme;
* plausible eligibility;
* credible funding route;
* strategic alignment;
* realistic timing;
* reasonable funding value;
* identifiable next action;
* acceptable compliance burden.

---

# 58. Definition of a "Proposal"

A prospect becomes a proposal only when TJF has made an internal decision to invest substantive resources in preparing an application or formal funding request.

This prevents the pipeline from being inflated by dozens of casual opportunities.

---

# 59. Definition of "Award"

An opportunity should be classified as **Awarded** only after formal confirmation from the funder or authorised donor representative.

Verbal indications, informal conversations or anticipated decisions should not be recorded as confirmed awards.

---

# 60. Definition of "Funding Secured"

Funding should be considered **secured** only when the applicable formal conditions have been satisfied.

Depending on the donor, this may include:

* signed agreement;
* formal award letter;
* completion of due diligence;
* satisfaction of conditions precedent;
* approved budget;
* confirmation of payment arrangements.

---

# 61. Definition of "Closed"

A pipeline item may be closed because:

* the grant was awarded and transferred to grant management;
* the proposal was declined;
* TJF withdrew;
* the donor closed the programme;
* the opportunity expired;
* TJF determined it was not strategically appropriate;
* the relationship was completed;
* or the prospect was permanently disqualified.

Every closure should have a reason.

---

# 62. Institutional Knowledge Principle

The pipeline belongs to **TJF as an institution**, not to an individual staff member.

Accordingly:

* donor relationships should not depend entirely on one person;
* key correspondence should be appropriately documented;
* proposal knowledge should be retained;
* contacts should be institutional where possible;
* major donor histories should be preserved;
* handovers should be possible.

This protects TJF against loss of institutional knowledge when responsibilities change.

# 64. Escalation Rules

An opportunity should be escalated to TJF leadership where:

* the funding value is strategically significant;
* the donor requires major organisational commitments;
* a major partnership is required;
* there are substantial legal/compliance obligations;
* co-financing is required;
* the donor requires significant unrestricted commitments;
* the project could materially affect TJF's reputation;
* safeguarding risk is significant;
* the proposal requires senior institutional representation;
* the opportunity involves government or high-level institutional relationships.
