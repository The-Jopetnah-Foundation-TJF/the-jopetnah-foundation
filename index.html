The problem

It never arrives in one piece.

In communities TJF hopes to serve, a child out of school, a family without healthcare, and a household without income may be connected challenges. TJF is being designed to address the whole picture, with communities helping define priorities and solutions.

Most organizations specialize in one slice of it. Jopetnah is built to address the whole picture, for the whole family.

Our model

Four pillars.
One point of entry.

TJF is developing an integrated model in which future programmes could reinforce one another around a household’s priorities. Any funding, delivery arrangements and expected results will be defined only after programme design, readiness checks and appropriate approvals.

Scholarships, mentorship, and digital-skills labs.

Health & Wellbeing

Community health champions, malaria prevention, and mobile wellness clinics.

Livelihoods

Micro-grants, vocational-to-job pipelines, savings circles.

Environment

Clean water, sanitation access, and climate-smart farming.

Our Values

How we intend to work.

The principles that shape every program decision, from pilot design to how we report back.

Integrity

We seek to act honestly, responsibly and transparently in how we manage relationships, resources and commitments.

Community Ownership

We believe sustainable development is stronger when communities participate meaningfully in defining priorities and solutions.

Transparency

We aim to communicate clearly about our plans, progress, limitations, resources and results — including publishing key working documents on our public GitHub repository.

Sustainability

We seek approaches that strengthen capacity and resilience rather than creating unnecessary long-term dependency.

Inclusion

We seek to create opportunities that respect dignity, participation and fair access for underserved people and communities.

Learning

We intend to measure, reflect, adapt and improve as programs develop and evidence becomes available.

FAQ

Questions serious partners ask

Why should we trust TJF?

Trust should be earned. TJF is developing its governance, financial controls and public disclosures. Approved documents will be published when they are ready and authorised for release; draft or internal documents should not be treated as public approvals.

Why an integrated model instead of a single-issue charity?

Education, health, livelihoods and environmental conditions can reinforce one another. TJF is developing an integrated model intended to consider these connections rather than treating each challenge in isolation.

What will our support actually accomplish?

Before any pilot begins, TJF intends to define the intended outcomes, indicators, data-collection approach and reporting arrangements. We will distinguish planned targets from results actually measured.

How will we know it worked?

Every major program will track a clear chain: who we reached → what access they gained → what capability they built → what behavior changed → what outcome resulted. Numbers alone can mislead ("10,000 people trained" says nothing about what happened to them afterward) — so our reporting will combine data with real beneficiary outcomes, not activity counts alone.

Is TJF able to receive donations right now?

No. Online donations are not active. TJF is developing its legal, governance and financial arrangements before any public giving pathway is activated. Please do not send funds to personal accounts or respond to unofficial payment requests.

What can a corporate or institutional partner contribute besides money?

Technology, equipment, professional expertise, employee volunteering, research capacity, logistics, market access, or mentorship — a partnership with TJF doesn't have to start with "here's a check." It can start with "here's what our organization is good at."

What makes TJF different from other NGOs?

Not a claim to be the only organization doing this work — that would be hard to substantiate. Our differentiation is the integrated model itself: connecting interventions across pillars rather than treating each as an isolated project, backed by governance and evidence discipline from day one rather than added later.

Readiness roadmap

Where we are, and what comes next.

This is an indicative sequence, not a fixed delivery timetable. Each stage depends on legal status, governance decisions, resources, safeguarding and operational readiness.

Stage 1 · Foundation

Establish the essentials

Continue the registration process, formalise governance, approve core controls and prepare programme designs and accountability systems.

Stage 2 · Readiness

Build responsible partnerships

Assess potential partners, confirm community priorities, define pilot plans and budgets, and establish the monitoring and safeguarding arrangements needed before delivery.

Stage 3 · Learn and grow

Implement only when ready

If resources and approvals allow, launch appropriately scoped pilots, review evidence with communities and partners, and publish verified updates.
